## Psalm XXXV.

*A Psalm of David.*

In this Psalm (1) David complains of the cruelty of his enemies in striving with him—in persecuting him—in seeking his ruin—and in reproaching, contemning, deriding, and triumphing over him, ver. 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 15, 16, 20, 21, 25, 26. (2) He pleads his own innocence, that he had never given them any provocation; but, amidst their abuse of him, had earnestly and affectionately studied to promote their welfare, ver. 7, 12-14, 19. (3) He supplicates that God would espouse his cause, protect, deliver, and comfort his soul; defeat the designs, and disappoint the expectations of his enemies; and that he would countenance and encourage his friends, ver. 1, 2, 4, 17, 22-27. (4) He predicts the destruction of his enemies, and the abounding of his own comfort; and, in the views hereof, resolves to thank and praise the Lord, ver. 4-10, 18, 28. ¶ While I sing, let me, with grief and shame, call to mind the infernal opposition I and others have made to our all-compassionate Redeemer. Let me beware of exposing myself to that justice which is laid up in store for his incorrigible enemies. Let me never avenge myself on my injurious neighbours. But amidst all attacks from hell or earth, or from my own corrupt heart, let me commit all my concerns to him who judgeth righteously, that he may bring them to pass.

P<span class="sc">LEAD</span>, Lord, with those that plead; and fight
&emsp;with those that fight with me.

2 Of shield and buckler take thou hold,
&emsp;stand up mine help to be.

3 Draw also out the spear, and do
&emsp;against them stop the way
That me pursue: unto my soul,
&emsp;I'm thy salvation, say.

4 Let them confounded be and sham'd
&emsp;that for my soul have sought:
Who plot my hurt turn'd back be they,
&emsp;and to confusion brought.

5 Let them be like unto the chaff
&emsp;that flies before the wind;
And let the angel of the Lord
&emsp;pursue them hard behind.

6 With darkness cover thou their way,
&emsp;and let it slipp'ry prove;
And let the angel of the Lord
&emsp;pursue them from above.

7 For without cause have they for me
&emsp;their net hid in a pit,
They also have without a cause
&emsp;for my soul digged it.

8 Let ruin seize him unawares;
&emsp;his net he hid withal
Himself let catch; and in the same
&emsp;destruction let him fall.

9 My soul in God shall joy; and glad
&emsp;in his salvation be:

10 And all my bones shall say, O Lord,
&emsp;who is like unto thee,
Which dost the poor set free from him
&emsp;that is for him too strong;
The poor and needy from the man
&emsp;that spoils and does him wrong?

11 False witnesses rose; to my charge
&emsp;things I not knew they laid.

12 They, to the spoiling of my soul,
&emsp;me ill for good repaid.

13 But as for me, when they were sick,
&emsp;in sackcloth sad I mourn'd:
My humbled soul did fast, my pray'r
&emsp;into my bosom turn'd.

14 Myself I did behave as he
&emsp;had been my friend or brother;
I heavily bow'd down, as one
&emsp;that mourneth for his mother.

15 But in my trouble they rejoic'd,
&emsp;gath'ring themselves together;
Yea, abjects vile together did
&emsp;themselves against me gather:
I knew it not; they did me tear,
&emsp;and quiet would not be.

16 With mocking hypocrites, at feasts
&emsp;they gnash'd their teeth at me.

17 How long, Lord, look'st thou on? from those
&emsp;destructions they intend
Rescue my soul, from lions young
&emsp;my darling do defend.

18 I will give thanks to thee, O Lord,
&emsp;within th' assembly great;
And where much people gather'd are
&emsp;thy praises forth will set.

19 Let not my wrongful enemies
&emsp;proudly rejoice o'er me;
Nor who me hate without a cause,
&emsp;let them wink with the eye.

20 For peace they do not speak at all;
&emsp;but crafty plots prepare
Against all those within the land
&emsp;that meek and quiet are.

21 With mouths set wide, they 'gainst me said,
&emsp;Ha, ha! our eye doth see.

22 Lord, thou hast seen, hold not thy peace;
&emsp;Lord, be not far from me.

23 Stir up thyself; wake, that thou may'st
&emsp;judgment to me afford,
Ev'n to my cause, O thou that art
&emsp;my only God and Lord.

24 O Lord my God, do thou me judge
&emsp;after thy righteousness;
And let them not their joy 'gainst me
&emsp;triumphantly express:

25 Nor let them say within their hearts,
&emsp;Ah, we would have it thus;
Nor suffer them to say, that he
&emsp;is swallow'd up by us.

26 Sham'd and confounded be they all
&emsp;that at my hurt are glad;
Let those against me that do boast
&emsp;with shame and scorn be clad.

27 Let them that love my righteous cause
&emsp;be glad, shout, and not cease
To say, The Lord be magnify'd,
&emsp;who loves his servant's peace.

28 Thy righteousness shall also be
&emsp;declared by my tongue;
The praises that belong to thee
&emsp;speak shall it all day long.

## Psalm XXXVI.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David, the servant of the <span class="sc">Lord</span>.*

Observe here (1) How great is the wickedness of men! They naturally contemn God; flatter themselves in sin; and abandon themselves to falsehood and mischief, ver. 1-4. (2) How great is the excellency of God, in truth, in righteousness, and in mercy! and what a fountain of preservation, support, comfort, light, and life he is to his people! ver. 5-9. (3) How, from the excellency and goodness of God, the Psalmist draws encouragement to pray for himself and other saints, and to triumph in the view of his enemies' ruin, ver. 10-12. ¶ While I sing, let me review my natural abominations and wretchedness, and try whether the Lord hath made me a new creature, created in Christ Jesus unto good works, and whether he hath made me taste of, admire, and trust in, the exceeding riches of his grace.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> wicked man's transgression
&emsp;within my heart thus says,
Undoubtedly the fear of God
&emsp;is not before his eyes.

2 Because himself he flattereth
&emsp;in his own blinded eye,
Until the hatefulness be found
&emsp;of his iniquity.

3 Words from his mouth proceeding are
&emsp;fraud and iniquity:
He to be wise, and to do good,
&emsp;hath left off utterly.

4 He mischief, lying on his bed,
&emsp;most cunningly doth plot:
He sets himself in ways not good,
&emsp;ill he abhorreth not.

5 Thy mercy, Lord, is in the heav'ns;
&emsp;thy truth doth reach the clouds:

6 Thy justice is like mountains great;
&emsp;thy judgments deep as floods:
Lord, thou preservest man and beast.

7 How precious is thy grace!
&emsp;Therefore in shadow of thy wings
&emsp;men's sons their trust shall place.

8 They with the fatness of thy house
&emsp;shall be well satisfy'd;
From rivers of thy pleasures thou
&emsp;wilt drink to them provide.

9 Because of life the fountain pure
&emsp;remains alone with thee;
And in that purest light of thine
&emsp;we clearly light shall see.

10 Thy loving-kindness unto them
&emsp;continue that thee know;
And still on men upright in heart
&emsp;thy righteousness bestow.

11 Let not the foot of cruel pride
&emsp;come, and against me stand;
And let me not removed be,
&emsp;Lord, by the wicked's hand.

12 There fallen are they, and ruined,
&emsp;that work iniquities:
Cast down they are, and never shall
&emsp;be able to arise.

## Psalm XXXVII.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm is wholly of the instructive kind. As the Mosaic system, and the covenant between God and Israel as his peculiar people, promised remarkable temporal felicity to such as were obedient to the law, and denounced temporal miseries against those that were impious and profane, the Psalmist here cautions against stumbling at particular providences which might appear contrary to the tenor of that covenant. Here are (1) Plain and express warnings against fretfulness at the prosperity of the wicked in their wickedness, ver. 1, 7, 8, with the arguments and reasons enforcing the same, viz. that the character of the wicked is ignominious, while that of the righteous is honourable, ver. 12, 14, 21, 26, 30, 31, 32;—that the wicked, in the very height of their prosperity, are near to destruction; while the righteous, even in their adversity, have special protection from God, and shall never be ruined, ver. 2, 9, 10, 13, 15, 17, 20, 28, 33-40;—and that God hath special blessings in store for, and even in this life manifests distinguished kindness to, the righteous and their seed, ver. 11, 16, 18, 19, 22-25, 28, 29, 37. (2) Here are proper and effectual remedies to prevent sinful fretting at the prosperity of the wicked, or troubles of the godly, viz. hoping in God as our Saviour; delighting in God as our companion, friend, and portion; following of him as our guide; departing from evil and doing good; waiting on the Lord, and keeping his way, ver. 3-6, 27, 34. ¶ Be thou, my soul, an accurate observer of the matter and circumstances of every providence. Carefully compare them one with another, and all with the perfections, covenant, and promises of God, in order that thou mayest never be offended thereat.

F<span class="sc">OR</span> evil-doers fret thou not
&emsp;thyself unquietly;
Nor do thou envy bear to those
&emsp;that work iniquity.

2 For, even like unto the grass,
&emsp;soon be cut down shall they;
And, like the green and tender herb,
&emsp;they wither shall away.

3 Set thou thy trust upon the Lord,
&emsp;and be thou doing good;
And so thou in the land shalt dwell
&emsp;and verily have food.

4 Delight thyself in God; he'll give
&emsp;thine heart's desire to thee.

5 Thy way to God commit, him trust,
&emsp;it bring to pass shall he.
