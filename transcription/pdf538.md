&emsp;a healing virtue flows;
Who looks to him with lively faith
&emsp;is sav'd from endless woes.

3 For God gave up his Son to death,
&emsp;so gen'rous was his love,
That all the faithful might enjoy
&emsp;eternal life above.

&emsp;the Son of God appear'd;

&emsp;and bids us fear no more.

&emsp;and scorn his offer'd grace.

## Paraphrase XLII.

*John xiv. 1-7.*

How tender and condescending is Christ to his people, wishing to remove all their anxieties and fears! What a safe and suitable object of faith! what time I am afraid let me trust in him. How great the glory to which he is advanced! What strong consolation to our soul, that he is our new and living way to God and glory, and that in his person, atonement, and administration; that he is risen from the dead, and in our nature ascended to heaven to secure our interest there, and provide delightful mansions for us. Amidst all my present uneasiness and fears, let me look forward to the glory which shall be revealed, and say, Thou shalt guide me with thy counsel while I live, and afterward bring me to glory.

L<span class="sc">ET</span> not your hearts with anxious thoughts
&emsp;be troubled or dismay'd;
But trust in Providence divine,
&emsp;and trust my gracious aid.

2 I to my Father's house return;
&emsp;there num'rous mansions stand,
And glory manifold abounds
&emsp;through all the happy land.

3 I go your entrance to secure,
&emsp;and your abode prepare;
Regions unknown are safe to you,
&emsp;when I, your friend, am there.

4 Thence shall I come, when ages close,
&emsp;to take you home with me;
There we shall meet to part no more,
&emsp;and still together be.

5 I am the way, the truth, the life:
&emsp;no son of human race,
But such as I conduct and guide,
&emsp;shall see my Father's face.

## Paraphrase XLIII.

*John xiv. 25-28.*

Though the Redeemer is now gone from the world, with respect to his personal presence in flesh, blessed be his name, that for his sake the Spirit as a comforter, instructor, remembrancer, and intercessor, is sent in his room: by his indwelling and influential presence he will refresh and comfort us. All-blessed Redeemer, we praise thee, that though silver and gold thou hadst none, thou leftest us that peace that passeth all understanding, to solace us amid every tribulation we can endure. Let me never vex, resist, nor quench this Spirit, that by his benign influence I may enjoy this peace.

Y<span class="sc">OU</span> now must hear my voice no more;
&emsp;my Father calls me home;
But soon from heav'n the Holy Ghost,
&emsp;your Comforter, shall come.

2 That heav'nly Teacher, sent from God,
&emsp;shall your whole soul inspire;
Your minds shall fill with sacred truth,
&emsp;your hearts with sacred fire.

3 Peace is the gift I leave with you;
&emsp;my peace to you bequeath;
Peace that shall comfort you through life,
&emsp;and cheer your souls in death.

4 I give not as the world bestows,
&emsp;with promise false and vain;
Nor cares, nor fears, shall wound the heart
&emsp;in which my words remain.

## Paraphrase XLIV.

*John xix. 30.*

Our Lord's last triumphant words are here expounded. O what glorious words, *It is finished.*—The divine purpose of my mission, with respect to the blessing of redemption, is finished; all that is written in the Prophets and in the Psalms, concerning my death, is now finished; the contest with the Prince of darkness, and all the powers of hell, is now finished; the pardon for sin, the hope of the redeemed, and redemption is now finished, and all that the Father gave me to do in the work of redemption. Our Lord declared this for the confusion of his enemies, to show his complacency in redemption work, and for the joy of all his friends. O my soul, let the finished work of Jesus be my only hope for eternity; and may he who hath begun a good work in me finish it unto the day of Christ.

B<span class="sc">EHOLD</span> the Saviour on the cross,
&emsp;a spectacle of woe!
See from his agonizing wounds
&emsp;the blood incessant flow;

2 Till death's pale ensigns o'er his cheek
&emsp;and trembling lips were spread;
Till light forsook his closing eyes,
&emsp;and life his drooping head!

3 'Tis finish'd—was his latest voice;
&emsp;these sacred accents o'er,
He bow'd his head, gave up the ghost,
&emsp;and suffer'd pain no more.

4 'Tis finish'd--The Messiah dies
&emsp;for sins, but not his own;
The great redemption is complete,
&emsp;and Satan's pow'r o'erthrown.

5 'Tis finish'd—All his groans are past;
&emsp;his blood, his pain, and toils,
Have fully vanquished our foes,
&emsp;and crown'd him with their spoils.

6 'Tis finish'd—Legal worship ends,
&emsp;and gospel ages run;
All old things now are past away,
&emsp;and a new world begun.

## Paraphrase XLV.

*Romans ii. 4-8.*

When I sing these lines, let me think how desperate-like their condition who harden themselves in sin from the mercies of God apprehended or experienced. How certain and awfully exact the future judgment on men by Jesus Christ! How correspondent to their qualities and works shall be the eternal punishments or gracious rewards of men; and all the means of illumination and grace will come into the account. Let us judge ourselves, and then we will not be condemned with the world. Enter not into judgment with thy servant: see God our shield, look on the face of thine Anointed.

U<span class="sc">NGRATEFUL</span> sinners! whence this scorn
&emsp;of God's long-suff'ring grace?
And whence this madness that insults
&emsp;th' Almighty to his face?

2 Is it because his patience waits,
&emsp;and pitying bowels move,
You multiply transgressions more,
&emsp;and scorn his offer'd love?

3 Dost thou not know, self-blinded man!
&emsp;his goodness is design'd
To wake repentance in thy soul,
&emsp;and melt thy harden'd mind?

4 And wilt thou rather choose to meet
&emsp;th' Almighty as thy foe,
And treasure up his wrath in store
&emsp;against the day of woe?

5 Soon shall that fatal day approach
&emsp;that must thy sentence seal,
And righteous judgments, now unknown,
&emsp;in awful pomp reveal;

6 While they, who full of holy deeds
&emsp;to glory seek to rise,
Continuing patient to the end,
&emsp;shall gain th' immortal prize.

## Paraphrase XLVI.

*Romans iii. 19-22.*

We have here an explanation of a sinner's justification before God. What fearful work the Divine denunciations must make on men's consciences, when God chargeth them home upon them; and how absurd to expect happiness by that law[?] which so deeply chargeth us with crimes! But how amazing that the righteousness of Jesus, and salvation through it, should be prepared for, offered and given to such monsters of guilt, Jews and Gentiles, and how exactly suited to all our needs! O let me count all things but dung that I may win Christ, and be found in him, not having mine own righteousness[?], which is of the law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, the righteousness which is of God by faith.

V<span class="sc">AIN</span> are the hopes the sons of men
&emsp;upon their works have built;
Their hearts by nature are unclean,
&emsp;their actions full of guilt.

2 Silent let Jew and Gentile stand,
&emsp;without one vaunting word;
And, humbled low, confess their guilt
&emsp;before heav'n's righteous Lord.

3 No hope can on the law be built
&emsp;of justifying grace;
The law, that shows the sinner's guilt,
&emsp;condemns him to his face.

4 Jesus! how glorious is thy grace!
&emsp;when in thy name we trust,
Our faith receives a righteousness
&emsp;that makes the sinner just.

## Paraphrase XLVII.

*Romans vi. 1-7.*

Here we may reflect how groundless, how injurious is every charge of evil men, on the doctrine of free justification through the blessed righteousness of Christ! Nothing is more detestable to a renewed heart than to continue in sin, because, or that grace may abound. Though our good works do not found, yet they necessarily follow our justification by grace. To indulge ourselves in sin, or to neglect the study of holiness, is a flat contradiction to all the signification and engagements of our baptism, and absolutely inconsistent with our interest in the atonement, and with our character, privilege, or duty, as members of Christ, and partakers of his grace and glory.

A<span class="sc">ND</span> shall we then go on to sin,
&emsp;that grace may more abound?
Great God, forbid that such a thought
&emsp;should in our breast be found!

2 When to the sacred font we came,
&emsp;did not the rite proclaim,
That, wash'd from sin, and all its stains,
&emsp;new creatures we became?

3 With Christ the Lord we dy'd to sin;
&emsp;with him to life we rise,
To life, which now begun on earth,
&emsp;is perfect in the skies.

4 Too long enthrall'd to Satan's sway,
&emsp;we now are slaves no more;
For Christ hath vanquish'd death and sin,
&emsp;our freedom to restore.

## Paraphrase XLVIII.

*Romans viii. 31, to the end.*

Happy are the people whose God is the Lord. His perfections, purposes, and providences, and the justifying death and powerful intercession of Christ, secure their full and eternal welfare. Why then as one, though the least of his people, should I not always triumph in what he is to us, hath done, and will do for and to us! Why not always rejoice exceedingly in hopes of the glory of God! Why are we not always wrapt up in wonder— all inflamed by the views of redeeming love! Why should any created thing disquiet our heart, when nothing can separate from Jesus' person, or deprive us of his and his Father's love! Blessed are the people who can give such a defiance to every enemy; in Jehovah's name they may rejoice all the day, and in his righteousness they are exalted.

L<span class="sc">ET</span> Christian faith and hope dispel
&emsp;the fears of guilt and woe;
The Lord Almighty is our friend,
&emsp;and who can prove a foe?

2 He who his Son, most dear and lov'd,
&emsp;gave up for us to die,
Shall he not all things freely give
&emsp;that goodness can supply!

3 Behold the best, the greatest gift,
&emsp;of everlasting love!
Behold the pledge of peace below,
&emsp;and perfect bliss above!

4 Where is the judge who can condemn,
&emsp;since God hath justify'd?
Who shall charge those with guilt or crime
&emsp;for whom the Saviour dy'd?
