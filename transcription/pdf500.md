## Psalm LXII.

*To the chief Musician, to Jeduthun, A Psalm of David.*

In this Psalm (1) David solemnly avows his manifold relations to God; his composed dependence on, fixed trust in, and quiet waiting for God; and his hopes of the ruin of his enemies, ver. 1-7. (2) He earnestly encourages others to trust in, and call on God, who is infinite in power, mercy, and justice; and to beware of trusting in men, or in outward riches or honours, which are deceitful and unsubstantial, ver. 8-12.

¶ While I sing, go thou, my soul, and do likewise. Be weaned from all dependence on creatures; but cleave to, and depend on this all-sufficient <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>, as answerable to all thy needs, all thy desires, all thy enjoying powers, for time and for eternity.—Then, O how fixed in safety! and how filled with the consolations of Christ!

M<span class="sc">Y</span> soul with expectation
&emsp;depends on God indeed:
My strength and my salvation doth
&emsp;from him alone proceed.

2 He only my salvation is,
&emsp;and my strong rock is he:
He only is my sure defence;
&emsp;much mov'd I shall not be.

3 How long will ye against a man
&emsp;plot mischief? ye shall all
Be slain; ye as a tott'ring fence
&emsp;shall be, and bowing wall.

4 They only plot to cast him down
&emsp;from his excellency:
They joy in lies; with mouth they bless,
&emsp;but they curse inwardly.

5 My soul, wait thou with patience
&emsp;upon thy God alone;
On him dependeth all my hope
&emsp;and expectation.

6 He only my salvation is,
&emsp;and my strong rock is he;
He only is my sure defence:
&emsp;I shall not moved be.

7 In God my glory placed is,
&emsp;and my salvation sure;
In God the rock is of my strength,
&emsp;my refuge most secure.

8 Ye people, place your confidence
&emsp;in him continually;
Before him pour ye out your heart;
&emsp;God is our refuge high.

9 Surely mean men are vanity,
&emsp;and great men are a lie;
In balance laid, they wholly are
&emsp;more light than vanity.

10 Trust ye not in oppression,
&emsp;in robb'ry be not vain;
On wealth set not your hearts, when as
&emsp;increased is your gain.

11 God hath it spoken once to me,
&emsp;yea, this I heard again,
That power to Almighty God
&emsp;alone doth appertain.

12 Yea, mercy also unto thee
&emsp;belongs, O Lord, alone:
For thou according to his work
&emsp;rewardest every one.

## Psalm LXIII.

*A Psalm of David, when he was in the wilderness of Judah.*

This Psalm was penned by David while he hid himself from the fury of Saul in the wilderness of Judah, 1 Sa. xxii. 5—or xxiii. 14, 24, 25—or xxiv. 1;—or when he fled from Absalom, 2
Sa. xv. xvi. We have (1) His strong faith, working in ardent desires after the most familiar fellowship with God in public ordinances, ver. 1, 2. (2) His superlative estimation of God, issuing in hearty resolutions to be for ever employed in his praise, ver. 3, 4. (3) His heart ravishing satisfaction in God amidst secret and fixed meditation on his excellencies and love, ver. 5, 6. (4) His joyful dependence on God as his Saviour and portion, ver. 7, 8. (5) His holy triumph in God as his preserver, and the destroyer of his enemies, ver. 9-11.

¶ While I sing these high praises of God, let my soul be strong in the faith, giving glory to God; embracing the promise of himself, and calling all the fulness of the Godhead my own. Let me be all in raptures with divine love and holy delight. Let no guile, no corruption, no carnal care, enervate or damp my inward powers, but let all be opened, be wide stretched, to grasp my infinite <span class="sc">all</span>, the great <span class="sc">I am</span>.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, thee my God, I'll early seek:
&emsp;my soul doth thirst for thee;
My flesh longs in a dry parch'd land,
&emsp;wherein no waters be:

2 That I thy power may behold,
&emsp;and brightness of thy face,
As I have seen thee heretofore
&emsp;within thy holy place.

3 Since better is thy love than life,
&emsp;my lips thee praise shall give.
4 I in thy name will lift my hands,
&emsp;and bless thee while I live.

5 Ev'n as with marrow and with fat
&emsp;my soul shall filled be;
Then shall my mouth with joyful lips
&emsp;sing praises unto thee:

6 When I do thee upon my bed
&emsp;remember with delight,
And when on thee I meditate
&emsp;in watches of the night.

7 In shadow of thy wings I'll joy;
&emsp;for thou mine help hast been.
8 My soul thee follows hard; and me
&emsp;thy right hand doth sustain.

9 Who seek my soul to spill shall sink
&emsp;down to earth's lowest room.
10 They by the sword shall be cut off,
&emsp;and foxes' prey become.

11 Yet shall the king in God rejoice,
&emsp;and each one glory shall
That swear by him: but stopp'd shall be
&emsp;the mouth of liars all.

## Psalm LXIV.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm contains (1) David's earnest supplications for preservation from his enemies, who were so spiteful in their calumnious reproaches, so close and subtle in their malicious plots, and so industrious and active in executing their cruel projects, ver. 1-6. (2) His predictions, that God by just means would quickly render these enemies a terror to themselves and others, and an occasion of holy joy and confidence to the saints, ver. 7-10.

¶ While I sing, let me commit my spiritual enemies into the hand of God, who is able to defeat them, and will quickly, to my everlasting comfort.

W<span class="sc">HEN</span> I to thee my prayer make,
&emsp;Lord, to my voice give ear;
My life save from the enemy,
&emsp;of whom I stand in fear.

2 Me from their secret counsel hide
&emsp;who do live wickedly;
From insurrection of those men
&emsp;that work iniquity:

3 Who do their tongues with malice whet,
&emsp;and make them cut like swords;
In whose bent bows are arrows set,
&emsp;ev'n sharp and bitter words:

4 That they may at the perfect man
&emsp;in secret aim their shot;
Yea, suddenly they dare at him
&emsp;to shoot, and fear it not.

5 In ill encourage they themselves,
&emsp;and their snares close do lay:
Together conference they have;
&emsp;Who shall them see? they say.

6 They have search'd out iniquities,
&emsp;a perfect search they keep:
Of each of them the inward thought,
&emsp;and very heart, is deep.

7 God shall an arrow shoot at them,
&emsp;and wound them suddenly:
8 So their own tongue shall them confound;
&emsp;all who them see shall fly.

9 And on all men a fear shall fall,
&emsp;God's works they shall declare;
For they shall wisely notice take
&emsp;what these his doings are.

10 In God the righteous shall rejoice,
&emsp;and trust upon his might;
Yea, they shall greatly glory all
&emsp;in heart that are upright.

## Psalm LXV.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm and Song of David.*

Here we are directed to praise God (1) For his kindness manifested in his kingdom of grace in hearing prayers, in pardoning iniquities, in satisfying the souls of his people with his blessings, and in protecting and supporting them in every exigence, ver. 1-5. (2) For his kindness in the kingdom of providence, in fixing the mountains, in calming the seas in preserving the regular succession of day and night, and in rendering the fields fruitful, and the flocks numerous and happy, ver. 6-13.

¶ While I sing, let praise wait in my soul for the Lord; and let me pour out my heart before him. Under deep sense of guilt and pollution, let me believe his unbounded forgiveness and sanctifying influence. Let me come even to his seat, and enjoy ravishing fellowship with him. Let all my confidence be fixed on him; and even in the blessings of providence, let me discern the exceeding riches of his grace.

P<span class="sc">RAISE</span> waits for thee in Sion, Lord:
&emsp;to thee vows paid shall be.

2 O thou that hearer art of pray'r,
&emsp;all flesh shall come to thee.

3 Iniquities, I must confess,
&emsp;prevail against me do:
But as for our transgressions,
&emsp;them purge away shalt thou.

4 Bless'd is the man whom thou dost chase,
&emsp;and mak'st approach to thee,
That he within thy courts, O Lord,
&emsp;may still a dweller be:
We surely shall be satisfy'd
&emsp;with thy abundant grace,
And with the goodness of thy house,
&emsp;ev'n of thy holy place.

5 O God of our salvation,
&emsp;thou, in thy righteousness,
By fearful works unto our pray'rs
&emsp;thine answer dost express:
Therefore the ends of all the earth,
&emsp;and those afar that be
Upon the sea, their confidence,
&emsp;O Lord, will place in thee.

6 Who, being girt with pow'r, sets fast
&emsp;by his great strength the hills.
7 Who noise of seas, noise of their waves,
&emsp;and people's tumult, stills.

8 Those in the utmost parts that dwell
&emsp;are at thy signs afraid:
Th' outgoings of the morn and ev'n
&emsp;by thee are joyful made.

9 The earth thou visit'st, wat'ring it;
&emsp;thou mak'st it rich to grow
With God's full flood; thou corn prepar'st,
&emsp;when thou provid'st it so.

10 Her rigs thou wat'rest plenteously,
&emsp;her furrows settelest:
With show'rs thou dost her mollify,
&emsp;her spring by thee is blest.

11 So thou the year most lib'rally
&emsp;dost with thy goodness crown;
And all thy paths abundantly
&emsp;on us drop fatness down.

12 They drop upon the pastures wide,
&emsp;that do in deserts lie;
The little hills on ev'ry side
&emsp;rejoice right pleasantly.

13 With flocks the pastures clothed be,
&emsp;the vales with corn are clad;
And now they shout and sing to thee,
&emsp;for thou hast made them glad.
