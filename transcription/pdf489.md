## Psalm XXIX.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was probably composed on the occasion of a thunder-storm. It is a solemn charge from heaven to great men to worship and glorify God; who (1) in his magnificence and power thundereth in the most terrible, alarming, and destructive manner, ver. 1-9. (2) Who is supreme Governor of the world, and bestoweth strength and peace on his peculiar people, ver. 10, 11.

¶ While I sing, let me, by faith, behold the glory of God in Christ, and be filled with reverential awe of his power and grace. And while I adore his perfections, admire and praise his excellencies, let Jerusalem come into my mind, and let me apprehend the promise, and pour forth a prayer for the remnant which is left.

G<span class="sc">IVE</span> ye unto the Lord, ye sons
&emsp;that of the mighty be,
&emsp;All strength and glory to the Lord
&emsp;with cheerfulness give ye.
2 Unto the Lord the glory give
&emsp;that to his name is due;
&emsp;And in the beauty of holiness
&emsp;unto <span class="sc">Jehovah</span> bow.

3 The Lord's voice on the waters is:
&emsp;the God of majesty
&emsp;Doth thunder, and on multitudes
&emsp;of waters sitteth he.
4 A pow'rful voice it is that comes
&emsp;out from the Lord most high;
&emsp;The voice of that great Lord is full
&emsp;of glorious majesty.

5 The voice of the Eternal doth
&emsp;asunder cedars tear;
&emsp;Yea, God the Lord doth cedars break

&emsp;his glory doth declare.

10 The Lord sits on the floods; the Lord
&emsp;sits King, and ever shall.
11 The Lord will give his people strength,
&emsp;and with peace bless them all.

## Psalm XXX.

*A Psalm and Song at the Dedication of the House of David.*

In this Psalm, composed for the dedication of the new palace which David had built for himself at Jerusalem, 2 Sa. v 11; or for the dedication of it after it had been polluted by Absalom, 2 Sa. xvi., he (1) Offers thanksgiving to God for the answering of his prayers, the overthrowing of his enemies, and the preserving of his life, ver. 1-3, 11, 12. (2) He calls and charges others to praise the Lord on account of the purity of his nature, the short duration of his frowns, and the sweetness of his favours, ver. 4, 5. (3) He remarks, how his carnal security under prosperity had occasioned his sudden fall into divine hidings and frowns, ver 6, 7. (4) He recollects what supplications he had made to God in his former distress, ver. 8-10. (5) He triumphs in the kindness of God towards him, and resolves to praise and thank him for evermore on account of it. ver. 11, 12.

¶ While I sing, let me. with a grateful heart, remember the Lord's mercies. Let me remember my own follies, and how the Lord corrected me for them Let me look after my prayers, and wait for an answer. And the nearer my end draweth, let my heart and mouth be the more abundantly filled with the high praises of my God and my Saviour.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, I will thee extol, for thou
&emsp;hast lifted me on high,
&emsp;And over me thou to rejoice
&emsp;mad'st not mine enemy.
2 O thou who art the Lord my God,
&emsp;I in distress to thee,
&emsp;With loud cries lifted up my voice,
&emsp;and thou hast healed me.

3 O Lord, my soul thou hast brought up,
&emsp;and rescu'd from the grave;
&emsp;That I to pit should not go down,
&emsp;alive thou didst me save.
4 O ye that are his holy ones,
&emsp;sing praise unto the Lord;
&emsp;And give unto him thanks, when ye
&emsp;his holiness record.

5 For but a moment lasts his wrath;
&emsp;life in his favour lies:
&emsp;Weeping may for a night endure,
&emsp;at morn doth joy arise.
6 In my prosperity I said,
&emsp;that nothing shall me move.
7 O Lord, thou hast my mountain made
&emsp;to stand strong by thy love:

&emsp;But when that thou, O gracious God,
&emsp;didst hide thy face from me,
&emsp;Then quickly was my prosp'rous state
&emsp;turn'd into misery.

&emsp;O Lord my God, for evermore

## Psalm XXXI.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

Observe here (1) David's solemn professions of his dependence upon God, and his prayers for support and deliverance, ver. 1-8. (2) His sad complaints of inward grief bodily weakness, unkindness of friends, unjust censures of enemies, and horror of death, attended with a solemn commitment of himself to God's mercy and care, and earnest supplication for deliverance from enemies, ver. 9-18. (3) Amidst admiration of God's kindness to his people. and thanksgiving for favours to himself, he encourageth himself and others firmly to trust in God, ver 19-24.

¶ While I sing, let me be deeply affected with my sores, my maladies, and troubles. and cast all my burdens on the Lord. In the assured faith that he is God, even my God, let me admire his gracious thoughts, words. and deeds to me-ward, and commit myself wholly to his care and protection.

I<span class="sc">N</span> thee, O Lord, I put my trust,
&emsp;sham'd let me never be;
&emsp;According to thy righteousness
&emsp;do thou deliver me.
2 Bow down thine ear to me, with speed
&emsp;send me deliverance:
&emsp;To save me, my strong rock be thou,
&emsp;and my house of defence.

3 Because thou art my rock, and thee
&emsp;I for my fortress take;
&emsp;Therefore do thou me lead and guide,
&emsp;ev'n for thine own name's sake.
4 And sith thou art my strength, therefore
&emsp;pull me out of the net,
&emsp;Which they in subtilty for me
&emsp;so privily have set.

5 Into thine hands I do commit
&emsp;my sp'rit: for thou art he,
&emsp;O thou, <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>, God of truth,
&emsp;that hast redeemed me.
6 Those that do lying vanities
&emsp;regard, I have abhorr'd:
&emsp;But as for me, my confidence
&emsp;is fixed on the Lord.

7 I'll in thy mercy gladly joy:
&emsp;for thou my miseries
&emsp;Consider'd hast; thou hast my soul
&emsp;known in adversities:
8 And thou hast not inclosed me
&emsp;within the en'my's hand;
&emsp;And by thee have my feet been made
&emsp;in a large room to stand.

9 O Lord, upon me mercy have,
&emsp;for trouble is on me:
&emsp;Mine eye, my belly, and my soul,
&emsp;with grief consumed be.
10 Because my life with grief is spent,
&emsp;my years with sighs and groans:
&emsp;My strength doth fail; and for my sin
&emsp;consumed are my bones.

11 I was a scorn to all my foes,
&emsp;and to my friends a fear;
&emsp;And specially reproach'd of those
&emsp;that were my neighbours near:
&emsp;When they me saw they from me fled.
12 Ev'n so I am forgot,
&emsp;As men are out of mind when dead:
&emsp;I'm like a broken pot.

13 For slanders I of many heard;
&emsp;fear compass'd me, while they
&emsp;Against me did consult, and plot
&emsp;to take my life away.
14 But as for me, O Lord, my trust
&emsp;upon thee I did lay;
&emsp;And I to thee, Thou art my God,
&emsp;did confidently say.

15 My times are wholly in thine hand:
&emsp;do thou deliver me
&emsp;From their hands that mine enemies
&emsp;and persecutors be.
16 Thy countenance to shine do thou
&emsp;upon thy servant make:
&emsp;Unto me give salvation,
&emsp;for thy great mercies' sake.

17 Let me not be asham'd, O Lord,
&emsp;for on thee call'd I have:
&emsp;Let wicked men be sham'd, let them
&emsp;be silent in the grave.
18 To silence put the lying lips,
&emsp;that grievous things do say,
&emsp;And hard reports, in pride and scorn,
&emsp;on righteous men do lay.

19 How great's the goodness thou for them
&emsp;that fear thee keep'st in store,
&emsp;And wrought'st for them that trust in thee
&emsp;the sons of men before!
20 In secret of thy presence thou
&emsp;shalt hide them from man's pride:
&emsp;From strife of tongues thou closely shalt,
&emsp;as in a tent, them hide.
