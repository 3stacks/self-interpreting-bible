## Psalm XLIX.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm for the sons of Korah.*

This Psalm is a mirror, calculated to exhibit the emptiness of all worldly enjoyments. Observe (1) David's earnest attempt to awaken all ranks of mankind to a serious consideration of this matter, as a point of great importance and universal concern, ver. 1-4. (2) His irrefragable proofs of the vanity of earthly enjoyments, viz. that they cannot save from death either a man's self or his friend and that they cannot make men wise or happy in this world, far less render them happy in a future state, ver. 6-14. (3) His attempt to comfort himself and other saints under the sense of their daily infirmities, and of the chastisements received on account of their sins: against the slavish fears of death; and against temptations arising from the prosperity of the wicked, ver. 5, 15-18.

¶ While I sing, let me bewail my sinful minding of, and idolatrous attachment to earthly things. Let me be henceforth as a weaned child, setting my affections on things above, where Christ is at the right hand of God. Let no uncertain riches or honours, but the <span class="sc">Living God</span>, be the object of all my trust and joy.

H<span class="sc">EAR</span> this, all people, and give ear,
&emsp;all in the world that dwell;
2 Both low and high, both rich and poor.
3 My mouth shall wisdom tell:
My heart shall knowledge meditate.
4 I will incline mine ear
To parables, and on the harp
&emsp;my sayings dark declare.

5 Amidst those days that evil be,
&emsp;why should I, fearing, doubt?
When of my heels th' iniquity
&emsp;shall compass me about.

6 Whoe'er they be that in their wealth
&emsp;their confidence do pitch,
And boast themselves, because they are
&emsp;become exceeding rich:

7 Yet none of these his brother can
&emsp;redeem by any way;
Nor can he unto God for him
&emsp;sufficient ransom pay,

8 (Their soul's redemption precious is,
&emsp;and it can never be,)
9 That still he should for ever live,
&emsp;and not corruption see.

10 For why? he seeth that wise men die,
&emsp;and brutish fools also
Do perish; and their wealth, when dead,
&emsp;to others they let go.

11 Their inward thought is, that their house
&emsp;and dwelling-places shall
Stand through all ages; they their lands
&emsp;by their own names do call.

12 But yet in honour shall not man
&emsp;abide continually;
But passing hence, may be compar'd
&emsp;unto the beasts that die.

13 Thus brutish folly plainly is
&emsp;their wisdom and their way;
Yet their posterity approve
&emsp;what they do fondly say.

14 Like sheep they in the grave are laid,
&emsp;and death shall them devour;
And in the morning upright men
&emsp;shall over them have pow'r:
Their beauty from their dwelling shall
&emsp;consume within the grave.

15 But from hell's hand God will me free,
&emsp;for he shall me receive.

16 Be thou not then afraid when one
&emsp;enriched thou dost see,
Nor when the glory of his house
&emsp;advanced is on high;

17 For he shall carry nothing hence
&emsp;when death his days doth end;
Nor shall his glory after him
&emsp;into the grave descend.

18 Although he his own soul did bless
&emsp;whilst he on earth did live;
(And when thou to thyself dost well,
&emsp;men will thee praises give;)

19 He to his fathers' race shall go,
&emsp;they never shall see light.

20 Man honour'd wanting knowledge is
&emsp;like beasts that perish quite.

## Psalm L.

*A Psalm of Asaph.*

This Psalm may be considered as a rebuke to the carnal Jews who rested in, and boasted of their external ceremonies in worship, to the neglecting of the weightier matters of the law—mercy, judgment, and faith; or as a prediction of the coming of Christ, to abolish the ceremonial worship, eject the Jews from his church, and establish a more pure and spiritual form of worship under the gospel; or, in fine, as a representation of the last judgment, in which Christ shall come to render to every man according to his deeds. Observe (1) The awful appearance of God our Redeemer in the flesh,—in power,—or in the clouds; with the gathering of the people to him, ver. 1-6. (2) An engaging admonition to improve God's new-covenant grant of himself to be our God, as an excitement to exchange legal ceremonies into prayer, thanksgiving, and holy obedience; or, at least, to give a remarkable preference to the latter, ver. 7-15. (3) An awful charge of hypocrisy, slander, contempt of God's word, and of atheistical imaginations concerning God, laid against the wicked, with a fearful sentence of condemnation founded thereon, ver. 16-22. (4) An alarming warning of danger to the forgetters of God, and an encouraging promise to such as study to glorify him by a holy conversation, ver. 22, 23.

¶ Sing this, my soul, with solemn awe, as sisted before the great Searcher of hearts, and as by faith beholding Jesus in my nature, sitting on his great white throne, gathering the nations to his bar. opening the books, and judging mankind out of the things found written therein.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> mighty God, the Lord,
&emsp;hath spoken, and did call
The earth, from rising of the sun,
&emsp;to where he hath his fall.

2 From out of Sion hill,
&emsp;which of excellency
And beauty the perfection is,
&emsp;God shined gloriously.

3 Our God shall surely come,
&emsp;keep silence shall not he:
Before him fire shall waste, great storms
&emsp;shall round about him be.

4 Unto the heavens clear
&emsp;he from above shall call,
And to the earth likewise, that he
&emsp;may judge his people all.

5 Together let my saints
&emsp;unto me gather'd be,
Those that by sacrifice have made
&emsp;a covenant with me.

6 And then the heavens shall
&emsp;his righteousness declare:
Because the Lord himself is he
&emsp;by whom men judged are.

7 My people Isr'el hear,
&emsp;speak will I from on high,
Against thee I will testify;
&emsp;God, ev'n thy God, am I.

8 I for thy sacrifice
&emsp;no blame will on thee lay,
Nor for burnt-off'rings, which to me
&emsp;thou offer'dst ev'ry day.

9 I'll take no calf nor goats
&emsp;from house or fold of thine:
10 For beasts of forests, cattle all
&emsp;on thousand hills, are mine.

11 The fowls on mountains high
&emsp;are all to me well known;
Wild beasts which in the fields do lie,
&emsp;ev'n they are all mine own.

12 Then, if I hungry were,
&emsp;I would not tell it thee;
Because the world, and fulness all
&emsp;thereof, belongs to me.

13 Will I eat flesh of bulls?
&emsp;or goats' blood drink will I?
14 Thanks offer thou to God, and pay
&emsp;thy vows to the most High.

15 And call upon me when
&emsp;in trouble thou shalt be;
I will deliver thee, and thou
&emsp;my name shalt glorify.

16 But to the wicked man
&emsp;God saith, My laws and truth
Should'st thou declare? how dar'st thou take
&emsp;my cov'nant in thy mouth?

17 Sith thou instruction hat'st,
&emsp;which should thy ways direct;
And sith my words behind thy back
&emsp;thou cast'st, and dost reject.

18 When thou a thief didst see,
&emsp;with him thou didst consent;
And with the vile adulterers
&emsp;partaker on thou went.

19 Thou giv'st thy mouth to ill,
&emsp;thy tongue deceit doth frame;
20 Thou sitt'st, and 'gainst thy brother speak'st,
&emsp;thy mother's son dost shame.

21 Because I silence kept,
&emsp;while thou these things hast wrought;
That I was altogether like
&emsp;thyself, hath been thy thought:
Yet I will thee reprove,
&emsp;and set before thine eyes,
In order ranked, thy misdeeds,
&emsp;and thine iniquities.

22 Now, ye that God forget,
&emsp;this carefully consider;
Lest I in pieces tear you all,
&emsp;and none can you deliver.

23 Whoso doth offer praise
&emsp;me glorifies; and I
Will show him God's salvation,
&emsp;that orders right his way.

*Another of the same.*

T<span class="sc">HE</span> mighty God, the Lord, hath spoke,
&emsp;and call'd the earth upon,
Ev'n from the rising of the sun
&emsp;unto his going down.

2 From out of Sion, his own hill,
&emsp;where the perfection high
Of beauty is, from thence the Lord
&emsp;hath shined gloriously.

3 Our God shall come, and shall no more
&emsp;be silent, but speak out:
Before him fire shall waste, great storms
&emsp;shall compass him about.

4 He to the heavens from above,
&emsp;and to the earth below,
Shall call, that he his judgments may
&emsp;before his people show.

5 Let all my saints together be
&emsp;unto me gathered;
Those that by sacrifice with me
&emsp;a covenant have made.

6 And then the heavens shall declare
&emsp;his righteousness abroad:
Because the Lord himself doth come;
&emsp;none else is judge but God.

7 Hear, O my people, and I'll speak;
&emsp;O Israel by name,
Against thee I will testify;
&emsp;God, ev'n thy God, I am.

8 I for thy sacrifices few
&emsp;reprove thee never will,
Nor for burnt-off'rings to have been
&emsp;before me offer'd still.

9 I'll take no bullock nor he-goats
&emsp;from house nor folds of thine:
10 For beasts of forests, cattle all
&emsp;on thousand hills, are mine.

11 The fowls are all to me well known
&emsp;that mountains high do yield;
And I do challenge as mine own
&emsp;the wild beasts of the field.
