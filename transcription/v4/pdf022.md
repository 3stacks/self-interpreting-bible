# Tabular Memoir of St. Paul

| Year of Our Lord | Recorded Events in Chronological Order | Where Recorded |
|---|---|---|
| 1-5 | Born at Tarsus, the capital of Cilicia | Acts xxii. 3. |
| Tarsus. | Learns the trade of tentmaking | Acts xviii. 3. |
| Jerusalem. | Taught according to “the perfect manner of the law of the fathers” by <span class="sc">Gamaliel</span> | Acts xxii. 3. |
| 31 and 32 | While still a “young man” he participates vigorously in the persecution of Christians | Acts vii. 58; viii. 1, 3. |
| 33 | Yet breathing out threatenings and slaughter, he seeks and obtains a commission from the high-priest to go into Damascus on his cruel errand of persecution | Acts ix. 1, 2; xxii. 4, 5; xxvi. 9-12. |
| Near Damascus. | Near Damascus, a vivid miraculous light shines from heaven and blinds him, and <span class="sc">Saul</span> and his company fall to the earth | Acts ix. 3,4,8; xxii.6. |
|  | JESUS appears to him and converses with him, directing him to go into the city to learn what he shall do | Acts ix. 4-6; xxii. 10; xxvi.14-18; 1Cor.xv.8 |
| Damascus. | He arises and is led into Damascus, where he remains blind three days | Acts ix. 7-9; xxii. 11. |
|  | <span class="sc">Ananias</span>, being sent by the LORD, comes to <span class="sc">Saul</span>, restores his sight and baptizes him | Acts ix. 10-18; xxii. 12-16. |
| In Arabia and Damascus. | Then, being thus born again, he retires to Arabia for a season, and returning to Damascus, at once enters upon his Apostolic labors, preaching CHRIST in the synagogues of Damascus, exciting the amazement of the multitude, and increasing the more in spiritual strength, he confounds the Jews | Acts ix. 20-22; Gal. i. 17. |
| 36 | The Jews of Damascus lie in wait day and night to kill him, but he escapes by being let down in a basket at night | Acts ix. 23-25; 2 Cor. xi. 33. |
| Jerusalem. | He proceeds to Jerusalem, where the Disciples are at first afraid of him, but being convinced of the change in his character, receive him into their fellowship | Acts ix. 26-28. |
| Tarsus. | He preaches boldly; the Jews of Jerusalem seek his life, and he escapes to his native city | Acts ix. 29, 30. |
| 43<br>Antioch. | <span class="sc">Barnabas</span> goes to Tarsus after him, and they proceed to Antioch, where they remain a whole year and establish the first Gentile Church (here the title *Christian* is first applied to the Disciples) | Acts xi. 25, 26. |
| 44 | <span class="sc">Paul</span> and <span class="sc">Barnabas</span> carry relief to the poor brethren in Judea | Acts xi. 29, 30. |
| Antioch. | Having fulfilled their charge, they return to Antioch, <span class="sc">John Mark</span> accompanying them | Acts xii. 25. |
| 45<br>Salamis. | <span class="sc">Paul</span> and <span class="sc">Barnabas</span> are set apart by the Church at Antioch, as commanded by the HOLY GHOST, for missionary work among the Gentiles; they start on their first tour, taking <span class="sc">Mark</span> with them; at Salamis they preach in the synagogue | Acts xiii. 1-5. |
| Paphos. | At Paphos, <span class="sc">Elymas</span> (<span class="sc">Bar-jesus</span>), the sorcerer, is struck blind, and <span class="sc">Sergius Paulus</span>, the proconsul, is converted | Acts xiii. 6-12. |
| Perga. | Then they go to Perga, in Pamphylia, where <span class="sc">Mark</span> leaves them | Acts xiii. 13. |
| 46<br>Antioch. | Thence they go to Antioch in Pisidia, where <span class="sc">Paul</span> preaches with good effect a powerful sermon to the Jews, which, by request of the Gentiles, he repeats to them the following Sabbath with marked success | Acts xiii. 14-48. |
| Iconium. | Thence to Iconium, where they make a great many converts | Acts xiii. 51-xiv. 1. |
| Lystra and Derbe. | They go to Lystra and Derbe; at the former, <span class="sc">Paul</span> cures the man who had been lame from birth | Acts xiv. 2-10. |
|  | The people regard the Apostles as gods, and are with difficulty restrained from offering sacrifice to them | Acts xiv. 11-18. |
| 47<br>Derbe. | Not long after, some Jews, from Antioch (in Pisidia) and Iconium, induce the fickle people of Lystra to stone <span class="sc">Paul</span>; they leave him, supposing him dead, but he recovers miraculously, and he and <span class="sc">Barnabas</span> go to Derbe | Acts xiv. 19, 20. |
| 48<br>Antioch. | Thence they go again *via* Lystra, etc., to Perga, in Pamphylia, and into Attalia, and then return to Syrian-Antioch. Thus ends <span class="sc">Paul</span>’s first Missionary Tour, and he remains at Antioch for about four years | Acts xiv. 21-28. |
| 52 | <span class="sc">Paul</span> attends the Church Council held at Jerusalem to decide about the circumcision of the Gentile converts | Acts xv. 1-21. |
| Antioch. | <span class="sc">Paul</span>, with a number of others, goes back to Antioch, bearing the decision to the Gentile Churches in Syria and Cilicia | Acts xv. 22-35. |
| 53 | His Second Missionary Tour commences | Acts xv. 36-40. |
| Derbe and Lystra. | He passes through Syria and Cilicia to Derbe and Lystra, where he meets with <span class="sc">Timothy</span>, whom he takes with him on his tour. | Acts xvi. 1-3. |
|  | He travels from city to city, accomplishing much good | Acts xvi. 4-7. |
| Troas.<br>Philippi. | At Troas, <span class="sc">Luke</span> joins the company, and in response to a vision <span class="sc">Paul</span> goes into Macedonia; at Philippi, <span class="sc">Lydia</span> is converted, she and her family are baptized, and she constrains the Apostle and his party to abide at her house | Acts xvi. 8-15. |
|  | <span class="sc">Paul</span> exorcises a spirit of divination from a young girl, whose masters bring him and <span class="sc">Silas</span> before the magistrate; they are beaten and cast into prison, their feet being put in stocks | Acts xvi. 16-24. |
|  | At midnight the prison doors are miraculously opened and their bonds loosed; the jailer is converted; he and his family are baptized. | Acts xvi. 25-34. |
| Th'ss'l'nica | In the morning they are released, and depart from Philippi, going to Thessalonica, where <span class="sc">Paul</span> makes many converts | Acts xvi. 35-xvii. 4. |
| Berea. | <span class="sc">Paul</span> and his company go to Berea, where they are very successful | Acts xvii. 5-12. |
| 54 | Thence to Athens, where <span class="sc">Paul</span> delivers a powerful sermon, making, however, but few converts | Acts xvii. 13-34. |
| Corinth.<br>55 | He soon goes to Corinth; here preaches on the Sabbaths; among the converts, considerable in number, is <span class="sc">Crispus</span>, the chief ruler of the synagogue; the LORD appears to Paul and encourages him in his work; he remains in this city a year and a half. | Acts xviii. 1-17. |
| Eph., etc. | He goes to Ephesus; thence, touching at Cæsarea, he hastens to Jerusalem, and returns to Antioch | Acts xviii. 18-22. |
|  | After a brief rest, he makes a rapid tour (the third) through Galatia and Phrygia | Acts xviii. 23. |
| 56<br>Ephesus.<br>58 | He goes again to Ephesus, where he baptizes in JESUS’ name twelve of St. <span class="sc">John Baptist</span>’s disciples, and they receive the HOLY GHOST; he preaches upwards of two years in the school of <span class="sc">Tyrannus</span>; “GOD wrought special miracles by the hands of <span class="sc">Paul</span>;” his success here is wonderful, especially after GOD has discomfited certain vagabond Jewish exorcists who strive to work miracles similar to his | Acts xix. 1-20. |
| 59 | <span class="sc">Paul</span> leaves Ephesus and visits Macedonia and Greece; then returns to Philippi | Acts xix. 21-xx. 5. |
| 60<br>Troas.<br>Miletus. | He goes to Troas, where <span class="sc">Eutychus</span> is killed by a fall from the window of a room where <span class="sc">Paul</span> was preaching, and is restored to life by the Apostle; he sails to Miletus; here he sends for the pastors of the Church at Ephesus and delivers to them a solemn charge, moving them so that they “wept sore and fell on <span class="sc">Paul</span>’s neck and kissed him.” | Acts xx. 6-38. |
| Cæsarea. | Thence they sail to Cæsarea, where <span class="sc">Agabus</span> foretells what awaits <span class="sc">Paul</span> at Jerusalem; nevertheless he goes to Jerusalem | Acts xxi. 1-26. |
| Jerusalem. | <span class="sc">Paul</span> is seized and cast out of the Temple by a mob, but is rescued by a Roman officer; as he is being dragged to the castle, he is granted permission to speak to the multitude, and, standing on the steps, makes his defence in a speech that for grand eloquence and power has seldom been equaled | Acts xxi. 27-xxii. 29. |
|  | Being arraigned before the Sanhedrim, <span class="sc">Paul</span> skilfully sets his judges at variance, and is again taken in charge by the Roman authorities; the LORD appears to him and encourages him, telling him that he is to bear witness in Rome | Acts xxii.30-xxiii.11. |
|  | A conspiracy is entered into by certain Jews to kill <span class="sc">Paul</span>, but is frustrated by his nephew and the Roman officer, <span class="sc">Lysius</span> | Acts xxiii. 12-35. |
| Cæsarea. | He is arraigned before <span class="sc">Felix</span>; <span class="sc">Tertullus</span> makes a plausible speech of accusation, which <span class="sc">Paul</span> effectually answers; <span class="sc">Felix</span> defers the case; he keeps <span class="sc">Paul</span> a prisoner for two years, and on vacating his office leaves him bound | Acts xxiv. |
| 62 | <span class="sc">Paul</span> is arraigned successively before <span class="sc">Festus</span> and <span class="sc">Agrippa</span>; though guilty of no crime, he having appealed unto Cæsar, they cannot release him | Acts xxv., xxvi. |
| Malta. | <span class="sc">Paul</span> is sent, a prisoner, on board a ship bound for Rome; predicts the perils of the voyage; they are wrecked on Melita | Acts xxvii. |
|  | Here <span class="sc">Paul</span> is bitten by a viper without injury; cures the father of <span class="sc">Publius</span> of a fever, and heals others | Acts xxviii. 1-10. |
| 63<br>Rome. | They sail after three months’ delay for Rome, where they arrive without further incident, and <span class="sc">Paul</span> is delivered to the captain of the guard, who suffers him to live by himself with but a single soldier as guard | Acts xxviii. 11-16. |
| 65 | <span class="sc">Paul</span> preaches to the chief of the Jews, and subsequently he continues to teach in his lodging; he dwells two years in his own hired house, “preaching the kingdom of GOD and teaching those things which concern the LORD JESUS CHRIST, with all confidence, no man forbidding him | Acts xxviii. 17-31. |


Here ends St. <span class="sc">Paul</span>’s history as recorded in the <span class="sc">Acts of the Apostles</span>. There are no certain particulars of the remainder of his life. It does not appear that he was tried during this imprisonment. It is probable that, on his release in 65, he made another Missionary Tour through Greece and Asia Minor, and possibly through Spain; some have thought he also visited Britain, but though not impossible, the evidence is very unsatisfactory. Soon after this he was again carried to Rome as a prisoner, and was not treated with kindness and consideration, as during the first imprisonment, but as a felon (2 Tim. ii. 9). He was beheaded A. D. 67 or 68, during the persecutions of <span class="sc">Nero</span>, when between sixty-three and sixty-seven years of age. There has been much discussion as to the chronology of St. <span class="sc">Paul</span>’s life; without entering into the question, we have taken the dates as they appear in the standard editions of the Bible; it is possible (and we think probable) that two years should be subtracted from each date given.
