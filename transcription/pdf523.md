## Psalm CXXI.

*A Song of Degrees.*

Here (1) In the firm faith of being heard, David supplicates divine help and protection, ver. 1, 2. (2) He comforts himself and others with the assurance of God's infallible direction and protection, ver. 3-8.

¶ While I sing, let all my confidence be on the Lord. Let my soul truly wait for him, from whom cometh all my necessary and expected blessings. On him be all my burdens, all my wants, all my cares.

I<span class="sc">TO</span> the hills will lift mine eyes,
&emsp;from whence doth come mine aid.

2 My safety cometh from the Lord,
&emsp;who heav'n and earth hath made.

3 Thy foot he'll not let slide, nor will
&emsp;he slumber that thee keeps.

4 Behold, he that keeps Israel,
&emsp;he slumbers not, nor sleeps.

5 The Lord thee keeps, the Lord thy shade
&emsp;on thy right hand doth stay:

6 The moon by night thee shall not smite,
&emsp;nor yet the sun by day.

7 The Lord shall keep thy soul; he shall
&emsp;preserve thee from all ill.

8 Henceforth thy going out and in
&emsp;God keep for ever will.

## Psalm CXXII.

*A Song of Degrees of David.*

This Psalm appears to have been composed for the use of the Hebrews at their three solemn feasts, Le. xxiii. We have in it (1) Expressions of great joy in going up to Jerusalem, ver. 1, 2. (2) High commendations of the beauty, order, sanctity, and honour of Jerusalem, ver. 3-5. (3) The duty and happiness of such as are really concerned for the welfare of Jerusalem, the church of God. ver. 6-9.

¶ While I sing, let me remember the gospel church, bewail her disorders, supplicate her welfare, and set my heart on the Jerusalem which is above, into which nothing can enter that defiles or disturbs. O when shall my feet stand there! When shall I see my Jesus on his great white throne, and sit with him in his glory!

I<span class="sc">JOY'D</span> when to the house of God,
&emsp;Go up, they said to me.

2 Jerusalem, within thy gates
&emsp;our feet shall standing be.

3 Jerus'lem, as a city, is
&emsp;compactly built together:

4 Unto that place the tribes go up,
&emsp;the tribes of God go thither:
To Isr'el's testimony, there
&emsp;to God's name thanks to pay.

5 For thrones of judgment, ev'n the thrones
&emsp;of David's house, there stay.

6 Pray that Jerusalem may have
&emsp;peace and felicity:
Let them that love thee and thy peace
&emsp;have still prosperity.

7 Therefore I wish that peace may still
&emsp;within thy walls remain,
And ever may thy palaces
&emsp;prosperity retain.

8 Now, for my friends' and brethren's sakes,
&emsp;Peace be in thee, I'll say.

9 And for the house of God our Lord,
&emsp;I'll seek thy good alway.

## Psalm CXXIII.

*A Song of Degrees.*

This Psalm relates to the distressed condition of the church, and contains (1) Earnest longings for, and believing expectations of, merciful deliverances from God, ver. 1, 2. (2) An improvement of great distress, as a plea for speedy and remarkable relief, ver. 3, 4.

¶ While I sing this, let me, in the full assurance of faith, cry to God for the mercies which my own soul, my family, and the church and nation wherewith I am connected, so much need. Nor let me accept of a denial of my believing requests.

O<span class="sc">THOU</span> that dwellest in the heav'ns,
&emsp;I lift mine eyes to thee.

2 Behold, as servants' eyes do look
&emsp;their masters' hand to see,
As handmaid's eyes her mistress' hand,
&emsp;so do our eyes attend
Upon the Lord our God, until
&emsp;to us he mercy send.

3 O Lord, be gracious to us,
&emsp;unto us gracious be;
Because replenish'd with contempt
&emsp;exceedingly are we.

4 Our soul is fill'd with scorn of those
&emsp;that at their ease abide,
And with the insolent contempt
&emsp;of those that swell in pride.

## Psalm CXXIV.

*A Song of Degrees of David.*

This Psalm is highly applicable to every remarkable deliverance which God works for his church, especially to the great redemption wrought for his people by Christ. In it (1) David presents the danger they were in, ver. 1-5. (2) Ascribes the glory of their deliverance to God, ver. 1, 2, 6, 7. (3) Improves the deliverance as an encouragement to trust in God, ver. 8.

¶ Let me behold <span class="sc">Jehovah</span> as a present help in trouble. Let my waiting eyes be towards him, who, notwithstanding all the combined power and policy of hell and earth, is able and ready to pull my feet out of the net.

H<span class="sc">AD</span> not the Lord been on our side,
&emsp;may Israel now say;

2 Had not the Lord been on our side,
&emsp;when men rose us to slay;

3 They had us swallow'd quick, when as
&emsp;their wrath 'gainst us did flame:

4 Waters had cover'd us, our soul
&emsp;had sunk beneath the stream.

5 Then had the waters, swelling high,
&emsp;over our soul made way.

6 Bless'd be the Lord, who to their teeth
&emsp;us gave not for a prey.

7 Our soul's escaped, as a bird
&emsp;out of the fowler's snare;
The snare asunder broken is,
&emsp;and we escaped are.

8 Our sure and all-sufficient help
&emsp;is in <span class="sc">Jehovah's</span> name;
His name who did the heav'n create,
&emsp;and who the earth did frame.

*Another of the same.*

N<span class="sc">OW</span> Israel
&emsp;may say, and that truly,
If that the Lord
&emsp;had not our cause maintain'd;

2 If that the Lord
&emsp;had not our right sustain'd,
When cruel men
&emsp;against us furiously
Rose up in wrath,
&emsp;to make of us their prey;

3 Then certainly
&emsp;they had devour'd us all,
And swallow'd quick,
&emsp;for ought that we could deem;
Such was their rage,
&emsp;as we might well esteem.

4 And as fierce floods
&emsp;before them all things drown,
So had they brought
&emsp;our soul to death quite down.

5 The raging streams,
&emsp;with their proud swelling waves,
Had then our soul
&emsp;o'erwhelmed in the deep.

6 But bless'd be God,
&emsp;who doth us safely keep,
And hath not giv'n
&emsp;us for a living prey
Unto their teeth,
&emsp;and bloody cruelty.

7 Ev'n as a bird
&emsp;out of the fowler's snare
Escapes away,
&emsp;so is our soul set free:
Broke are their nets,
&emsp;and thus escaped we.

8 Therefore our help
&emsp;is in the Lord's great name,
Who heav'n and earth
&emsp;by his great pow'r did frame.

## Psalm CXXV.

*A Song of Degrees.*

Let me in this Psalm behold (1) The happiness of the saints in the fixedness of their new-covenant state, the safety of their condition, the seasonableness of their deliverances, and the advantage of effectual fervent prayer in their behalf, ver. 1-4. (2) The misery of the wicked, particularly of apostates from the ways of God, ver. 5.

¶ Let me then never be of them that draw back, but of them who believe, to the fixing of their heart, and the saving of their soul.

T<span class="sc">HEY</span> in the Lord that firmly trust
&emsp;shall be like Sion hill,
Which at no time can be remov'd,
&emsp;but standeth ever still.

2 As round about Jerusalem
&emsp;the mountains stand alway,
The Lord his folk doth compass so,
&emsp;from henceforth and for aye.

3 For ill men's rod upon the lot
&emsp;of just men shall not lie;
Lest righteous men stretch forth their hands
&emsp;unto iniquity.

4 Do thou to all those that be good
&emsp;thy goodness, Lord, impart;
And do thou good to those that are
&emsp;upright within their heart.

5 But as for such as turn aside
&emsp;after their crooked way,
God shall lead forth with wicked men:
&emsp;on Isr'el peace shall stay.

## Psalm CXXVI.

*A Song of Degrees.*

This Psalm relates to some remarkable deliverance of the Jewish church, perhaps from Babylon, as typical of those of the gospel church; and (1) Represents great admiration of, and thankfulness for the deliverance, ver. 1-3. (2) Contains prayers for, and encouragement of, saints exercised with tribulations, ver. 4-6.

¶ While I sing, let me admire the Lord's kindness to Britain;—to his gospel church;—to mankind;—to my soul. And in my remaining distress, personal or relative, let me sow in mourning and supplication, that I may reap joyful deliverances, and everlasting consolations, through grace.

W<span class="sc">HEN</span> Sion's bondage God turn'd back,
&emsp;as men that dream'd were we.

2 Then fill'd with laughter was our mouth,
&emsp;our tongue with melody:
They 'mong the heathen said, The Lord
&emsp;great things for them hath wrought.

3 The Lord hath done great things for us,
&emsp;whence joy to us is brought.

4 As streams of water in the south,
&emsp;our bondage, Lord, recall.

5 Who sow in tears, a reaping time
&emsp;of joy enjoy they shall.

6 That man who, bearing precious seed,
&emsp;in going forth doth mourn,
He doubtless, bringing back his sheaves,
&emsp;rejoicing shall return.

## Psalm CXXVII.

*A Song of Degrees for Solomon.*

This Psalm relates to David's orders for building the temple, 1 Ch. xxii. xxviii. or to Solomon's actual building of it, 2 Ch. ii.-vi. It teaches us constant dependence on God in all our concerns; particularly (1) In prospering our undertakings, or rearing our families, ver. 1. (2) In protecting our residence and country, ver. 1. (3) In enriching us without excessive care or labour, ver. 2. (4) In giving us agreeable heirs to possess our substance, ver. 3-5.

¶ In my undertakings, let me set the Lord always before me, and depend on his blessing as the source of my success. In all my enjoyments let my care be to enjoy God himself.

E<span class="sc">XCEPT</span> the Lord do build the house,
&emsp;the builders lose their pain,
Except the Lord the city keep,
&emsp;the watchmen watch in vain.
