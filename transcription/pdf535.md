## Paraphrase XXV.

*Isaiah liii.*

The chapter on which this paraphrase is founded is rather like a poetical history of the life of our Redeemer, than a prediction 700 years before he came in the flesh. Let me with wonder and awe reflect that Jesus should be so much unknown to the hearers of the gospel: but nothing less than almighty power can make us believe in him. How exactly verified are these predictions in the debasement and glory of the Redeemer! how ignorance and enmity lead men to despise and blaspheme him! O his infinite love in undertaking, obeying, and suffering for us! O his Father's love in transferring our sins, and with pleasure punishing them in his infinitely well-beloved Son! O how sweet, how full, how lasting a redemption it must be, that is through his blood! Let me rejoice, my Jesus shall have a glorious reward, and I and millions more shall share in his glories.

H<span class="sc">OW</span> few receive with cordial faith
&emsp;the tidings which we bring?
How few have seen the arm reveal'd
&emsp;of heav'n's eternal King?

2 The Saviour comes! no outward pomp
&emsp;bespeaks his presence nigh;
No earthly beauty shines in him
&emsp;to draw the carnal eye.

3 Fair as a beauteous tender flow'r
&emsp;amidst the desert grows,
So slighted by a rebel race
&emsp;the heav'nly Saviour rose.

4 Rejected and despis'd of men,
&emsp;behold a man of woe!
Grief was his close companion still
&emsp;through all his life below.

5 Yet all the griefs he felt were ours,
&emsp;ours were the woes he bore:
Pangs, not his own, his spotless soul
&emsp;with bitter anguish tore.

6 We held him as condemn'd by Heav'n,
&emsp;an outcast from his God,
While for our sins he groan'd, he bled,
&emsp;beneath his Father's rod.

7 His sacred blood hath wash'd our souls
&emsp;from sin's polluted stain;
His stripes have heal'd us, and his death
&emsp;reviv'd our souls again.

8 We all, like sheep, had gone astray
&emsp;in ruin's fatal road:
On him were our transgressions laid;
&emsp;he bore the mighty load.

9 Wrong'd and oppress'd, how meekly he
&emsp;in patient silence stood!
Mute, as the peaceful harmless lamb,
&emsp;when brought to shed its blood.

10 Who can his generation tell?
&emsp;from prison see him led!
With impious show of law condemn'd,
&emsp;and number'd with the dead.

11 'Midst sinners low in dust he lay;
&emsp;the rich a grave supply'd:
Unspotted was his blameless life;
&emsp;unstain'd by sin he dy'd.

12 Yet God shall raise his head on high,
&emsp;though thus he brought him low;
His sacred off'ring, when complete,
&emsp;shall terminate his woe.

13 For, saith the Lord, my pleasure then
&emsp;shall prosper in his hand;
His shall a num'rous offspring be,
&emsp;and still his honours stand.

14 His soul, rejoicing, shall behold
&emsp;the purchase of his pain;
And all the guilty whom he sav'd
&emsp;shall bless Messiah's reign.

15 He with the great shall share the spoil,
&emsp;and baffle all his foes;
Though rank'd with sinners, here he fell,
&emsp;a conqueror he rose.

16 He dy'd to bear the guilt of men,
&emsp;that sin might be forgiv'n:
He lives to bless them and defend,
&emsp;and plead their cause in heav'n.

## Paraphrase XXVI.

*Isaiah lv.*

Here we have the door of faith opened to sinful men, to Gentiles, a door that no man can shut. Let me think how all the blessings of salvation are framed to answer our need! How the most needy, nay, the most notorious sinners, are expressly, are earnestly invited to partake of blessings, which he at infinite expense provided for sinners, for his enemies, who daily contemn him and blaspheme him! Let us in the believing reception of his grace seek God and return to him; let us welcome his word, coming down as rain; and be fruitful in every good word and work.

H<span class="sc">O</span>! ye that thirst, approach the spring
&emsp;where living waters flow:
Free to that sacred fountain all
&emsp;without a price may go.

2 How long to streams of false delight
&emsp;will ye in crowds repair?
How long your strength and substance waste
&emsp;on trifles, light as air?

3 My stores afford those rich supplies
&emsp;that health and pleasure give:
Incline your ear, and come to me;
&emsp;the soul that hears shall live.

4 With you a cov'nant I will make,
&emsp;that ever shall endure;
The hope which gladden'd David's heart
&emsp;my mercy hath made sure.

5 Behold he comes! your leader comes,
&emsp;with might and honour crown'd;
A witness who shall spread my name
&emsp;to earth's remotest bound.

6 See! nations hasten to his call
&emsp;from ev'ry distant shore;
Isles, yet unknown, shall bow to him,
&emsp;and Israel's God adore.

7 Seek ye the Lord while yet his ear
&emsp;is open to your call;
While offer'd mercy still is near,
&emsp;before his footstool fall.

8 Let sinners quit their evil ways,
&emsp;their evil thoughts forego,
And God, when they to him return,
&emsp;returning grace will show.

9 He pardons with o'erflowing love:
&emsp;for, hear the voice divine!
My nature is not like to yours,
&emsp;nor like your ways are mine:

10 But far as heav'n's resplendent orbs
&emsp;beyond earth's spot extend,
As far my thoughts, as far my ways,
&emsp;your ways and thoughts transcend.

11 And as the rains from heav'n distil,
&emsp;nor thither mount again,
But swell the earth with fruitful juice,
&emsp;and all its tribes sustain:

12 So not a word that flows from me
&emsp;shall ineffectual fall;
But universal nature prove
&emsp;obedient to my call.

13 With joy and peace shall then be led
&emsp;the glad converted lands;
The lofty mountains then shall sing,
&emsp;the forests clap their hands.

14 Where briers grew 'midst barren wilds,
&emsp;shall firs and myrtles spring;
And nature, through its utmost bounds,
&emsp;eternal praises sing.

## Paraphrase XXVII.

*Isaiah lvii. 15, 16.*

Here the infinitely high and lofty Jehovah is represented seated on his throne of grace, high and lifted up, yet in great condescension towards persons destitute, distressed, and broken in spirit, intimating he will not continue his judgments till such be ruined, but that he will dwell in them by his Spirit, and revive and comfort them. Happy are those in whom he dwells amidst all their trials. Behold, my soul, how God gives grace to the humble, and God indeed dwells in man upon earth.

T<span class="sc">HUS</span> speaks the high and lofty One;
&emsp;ye tribes of earth, give ear;
The words of your Almighty King
&emsp;with sacred rev'rence hear:

2 Amidst the majesty of heav'n
&emsp;my throne is fix'd on high;
And through eternity I hear
&emsp;the praises of the sky:

3 Yet, looking down, I visit oft
&emsp;the humble hallow'd cell;
And with the penitent who mourn
&emsp;'tis my delight to dwell;

4 The downcast spirit to revive,
&emsp;the sad in soul to cheer;
And from the bed of dust the man
&emsp;of heart contrite to rear.

5 With me dwells no relentless wrath
&emsp;against the human race;
The souls which I have form'd shall find
&emsp;a refuge in my grace.

## Paraphrase XXVIII.

*Isaiah lviii. 5-9.*

These words declare the duties of a fast, and the need of great brokenness of heart for, and of reformation from sin, in our professed humiliation for it, otherwise our fast, and our demure looks, will but provoke God to abhor us. No fast is suitably observed, if we repent not of our cruel conduct with respect to the bodies and souls of others; nor is it sanctified unless our hearts and hands be opened to our poor and distressed brethren. Let me not then trust to outward expressions of sorrow, but see that my heart be rent from and for sin.

A<span class="sc">TTEND</span>, and mark the solemn fast
&emsp;which to the Lord is dear;
Disdain the false unhallow'd mask
&emsp;which vain dissemblers wear.

2 Do I delight in sorrow's dress?
&emsp;saith he who reigns above;
The hanging head and rueful look,
&emsp;will they attract my love?

3 Let such as feel oppression's load
&emsp;thy tender pity share:
And let the helpless, homeless poor,
&emsp;be thy peculiar care.

4 Go, bid the hungry orphan be
&emsp;with thy abundance blest;
Invite the wand'rer to thy gate,
&emsp;and spread the couch of rest.

5 Let him who pines with piercing cold
&emsp;by thee be warm'd and clad;
Be thine the blissful task to make
&emsp;the downcast mourner glad.

6 Then, bright as morning, shall come forth,
&emsp;in peace and joy, thy days;
And glory from the Lord above
&emsp;shall shine on all thy ways.
