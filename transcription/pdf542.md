God bends his ear to their requests,
&emsp;he loves to hear their praise.
4 Who shall the Father's record search,
&emsp;and hidden things reveal?
Behold the Son that record takes,
&emsp;and opens ev'ry seal!

5 Hark how th' adoring hosts above
&emsp;with songs surround the throne!
Ten thousand thousand are their tongues;
&emsp;but all their hearts are one.
6 Worthy the Lamb that dy'd, thy cry,
&emsp;to be exalted thus;
Worthy the Lamb, let us reply,
&emsp;for he was slain for us.

7 To him be pow'r divine ascrib'd,
&emsp;and endless blessings paid;
Salvation, glory, joy, remain
&emsp;for ever on his head!
8 Thou hast redeem'd us with thy blood,
&emsp;and set the pris'ners free;
Thou mad'st us kings and priests to God,
&emsp;and we shall reign with thee.

9 From ev'ry kindred, ev'ry tongue,
&emsp;thou brought'st thy chosen race;
And distant lands and isles have shar'd
&emsp;the riches of thy grace.
10 Let all that dwell above the sky,
&emsp;or on the earth below,
With fields, and floods, and ocean's shores,
&emsp;to thee their homage show.

11 To Him who sits upon the throne,
&emsp;the God whom we adore,
And to the Lamb that once was slain,
&emsp;be glory evermore.

## Paraphrase LXVI.

*Rev. vii. 13, to the end.*

The elder's answer to his own question is a representation chiefly of the glories of the heavenly world! How abundantly their labours and sufferings are there compensated. O their happiness in being free from sin and misery! their fulness of happiness and joy! their pure and glorious appearance! their immediate fellowship with Christ and his Father! their triumphs in his praise! Not to them, however, but to God, be the glory! Their whole salvation is owing to Jehovah's grace and Jesus' merits. Even their best works are not acceptable to God, but through the blood of his Son. Hold out, faith and patience, and I shall enter the joy of my Lord.

H<span class="sc">OW</span> bright these glorious spirits shine!
&emsp;whence all their white array?
How came they to the blissful seats
&emsp;of everlasting day?
2 Lo! these are they from suff'rings great,
&emsp;who came to realms of light,
And in the blood of Christ have wash'd
&emsp;those robes which shine so bright.

3 Now, with triumphal palms, they stand
&emsp;before the throne on high,
And serve the God they love, amidst
&emsp;the glories of the sky.
4 His presence fills each heart with joy,
&emsp;tunes ev'ry mouth to sing:
By day, by night, the sacred courts
&emsp;with glad hosannahs ring.

5 Hunger and thirst are felt no more,
&emsp;nor suns with scorching ray;
God is their sun, whose cheering beams
&emsp;diffuse eternal day.
6 The Lamb which dwells amidst the throne
&emsp;shall o'er them still preside;
Feed them with nourishment divine,
&emsp;and all their footsteps guide.

7 'Mong pastures green he'll lead his flock,
&emsp;where living streams appear;
And God the Lord from ev'ry eye
&emsp;shall wipe off ev'ry tear.

## Paraphrase LXVII.

*Rev. xxi. 1-9.*

When we peruse these lines, we see as in a figure a new heaven and a new earth, and a new Jerusalem. These represent the church in her millennial and triumphant state. Of the last, particularly, we may say, What spiritual light, glory, honour, wealth, safety, satisfaction, all meet together[?] How new all things. far surpassing anything found or imagined before! What infinite enjoyment, God to be our all for ever! and how fearful the end of the wicked! Seeing we look for these things, let us be diligent to be found in peace, without spot and blameless!

L<span class="sc">O</span>! what a glorious sight appears
&emsp;to our admiring eyes!
The former seas have pass'd away,
&emsp;the former earth and skies.
2 From heav'n the New Jerus'lem comes,
&emsp;all worthy of its Lord;
See all things now at last renew'd,
&emsp;and paradise restor'd!

3 Attending angels shout for joy,
&emsp;and the bright armies sing;
Mortals! behold the sacred seat
&emsp;of your descending King!
4 The God of glory down to men
&emsp;removes his bless'd abode;
He dwells with men; his people they,
&emsp;and he his people's God.

5 His gracious hand shall wipe the tears
&emsp;from ev'ry weeping eye;
And pains and groans, and griefs and fears,
&emsp;and death itself, shall die.
6 Behold, I change all human things!
&emsp;saith he, whose words are true;
Lo! what was old is pass'd away,
&emsp;and all things are made new!

7 I am the First, and I the Last,
&emsp;through endless years the same;
I AM, is my memorial still,
&emsp;and my eternal name.
8 Ho, ye that thirst! to you my grace
&emsp;shall hidden streams disclose,
And open full the sacred spring,
&emsp;whence life for ever flows.

9 Bless'd is the man that overcomes;
&emsp;I'll own him for a son;
A rich inheritance rewards
&emsp;the conquests he hath won.
10 But bloody hands and hearts unclean,
&emsp;and all the lying race,
The faithless, and the scoffing crew,
&emsp;who spurn at offer'd grace;

11 They, seiz'd by justice, shall be doom'd
&emsp;in dark abyss to lie,
And in the fiery burning lake
&emsp;the second death shall die.
12 O may we stand before the Lamb,
&emsp;when earth and seas are fled,
And hear the Judge pronounce our name
&emsp;with blessings on our head!
