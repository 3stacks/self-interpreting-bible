&emsp;to David I'll not lie;
36 His seed and throne shall, as the sun,
&emsp;before me last for aye.
37 It, like the moon, shall ever be
&emsp;establish'd stedfastly;

&emsp;doth witness faithfully.

&emsp;made void, and quite cast by;
Thou hast profan'd his crown, while it
&emsp;cast on the ground doth lie.

&emsp;his strong holds down hast torn.
41 He to all passers-by a spoil,
&emsp;to neighbours is a scorn.

&emsp;mad'st all his en'mies glad:

&emsp;his throne to ground down cast;

&emsp;hast made all men in vain?

&emsp;and death shall never see?

&emsp;O Lord, where be they now?

&emsp;to David sworn hast thou?

&emsp;how I in bosom bear
The scornings of the people all,
&emsp;who strong and mighty are.

51 Wherewith thy raging enemies
&emsp;reproach'd, O Lord, think on;
Wherewith they have reproach'd the steps
&emsp;of thine anointed one.
52 All blessing to the Lord our God
&emsp;let be ascribed then:
For evermore so let it be.
&emsp;Amen, yea, and amen.

## Psalm XC.

*A Prayer of Moses, the Man of God.*

This Psalm was composed by Moses, the man of God, some time after God had sentenced the Hebrews to wandering and death in the wilderness, Nu. xiv. In it, (1) Moses comforts himself and his people with the eternal and unchangeable duration of God himself, and their interest in him, ver. 1, 2. (2) He humbles himself and his people with the consideration of human frailty. ver. 3-6. (3) He submits himself and his people to the righteous sentence of God passed upon them, ver. 7-11. (4) By prayer he commits himself and his people to their gracious and merciful God, and requests the sanctified use of their awful chastisements, the averting of divine wrath, the bestowal of true comforts and joys, the returns of his favour, and the progress of his work of mercy among their children, ver. 12-17.

¶ Let me sing this as the tenant of an hour, who hath no assurance of his present life;—sing it as resigned to my lot on earth, however afflicted; and as active in preparation for death, and committing myself and family to the God of truth as our Redeemer, our God, and our everlasting <span class="sc">all</span>.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, thou hast been our dwelling-place
&emsp;in generations all.
2 Before thou ever hadst brought forth
&emsp;the mountains great or small;
Ere ever thou hadst formed the earth,
&emsp;and all the world abroad;
Ev'n thou from everlasting art
&emsp;to everlasting God.

3 Thou dost unto destruction
&emsp;man that is mortal turn;
And unto them thou say'st, Again,
&emsp;ye sons of men, return.
4 Because a thousand years appear
&emsp;no more before thy sight
Than yesterday, when it is past,
&emsp;or than a watch by night.

5 As with an overflowing flood
&emsp;thou carry'st them away:
They like a sleep are, like the grass
&emsp;that grows at morn are they.
6 At morn it flourishes and grows,
&emsp;cut down at ev'n doth fade.
7 For by thine anger we're consum'd,
&emsp;thy wrath makes us afraid.

8 Our sins thou and iniquities
&emsp;dost in thy presence place,
And sett'st our secret faults before
&emsp;the brightness of thy face.
9 For in thine anger all our days
&emsp;do pass on to an end;

&emsp;so we our years do spend.

&emsp;in some fourscore they be:
Yet doth the strength of such old men
&emsp;but grief and labour prove;
For it is soon cut off, and we
&emsp;fly hence, and soon remove.

11 Who knows the power of thy wrath?
&emsp;according to thy fear
12 So is thy wrath: Lord, teach thou us
&emsp;our end in mind to bear;
And so to count our days, that we
&emsp;our hearts may still apply

13 Turn yet again to us, O Lord,
&emsp;how long thus shall it be?

17 And let the beauty of the Lord
&emsp;our God be us upon:
Our handy-works establish thou,
&emsp;establish them each one.

## Psalm XCI.

This Psalm was perhaps penned on the occasion of the pestilence inflicted for numbering the people, 2 Sa. xxiv. In it, while the Psalmist by faith takes God for his own refuge and keeper, ver. 2. 9, he, for the encouragement of others to do the same, represents (1) The dignity, extent, and continuance of their happiness and safety who have God for their residence, ver. 1-4. (2) Their preservation from all malice and baleful influence of the powers of darkness, while multitudes are ruined thereby, ver. 3-9. (3) Their enjoyment of angels for their guard, and their triumph over devils and other opposers, ver. 10-12. (4) Their possession of special favour from, and delightful intimacy with, God here and hereafter, ver. 13-16.

¶ In singing these, let mine eyes be fixed on Jesus, the Man of God's right hand, in whom all these promises were fulfilled to the highest; and, in his name, let me depend on them to be accomplished in myself. And let all the begun experience I have had thereof, encourage me to rejoice in hope of the more abundant mercies and glory of God.
