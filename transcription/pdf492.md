## Psalm XXXVIII.

*A Psalm of David, to bring in remembrance.*

This Psalm appears to have been penned by David under some remarkable distress, attended with a deep sense of sin as the procuring cause of it. Here are (1) David's sorrowful complaints of God's sore displeasure, and of the weight of his own sins, ver. 1-5; of the sickness of his body, and distress of his mind, ver. 6-10; of the unkindness of his friends, ver. 11, and of the unprovoked injuries he received from his enemies, who were spiteful, cruel, subtle, unjust, ungrateful, impious, devilish, numerous, and powerful, ver. 12, 16-20. (2) His remarkable patience and resignation under his troubles, ver. 13-15. (3) His fervent supplications to God for the mitigation of his trouble, ver. 1; and for comfort and support under, and speedy deliverance from them, ver. 16, 21, 22: attended with candid and ingenuous acknowledgments of the sinful causes thereof, ver. 3-5, 18. ¶ In all my troubles let me search out, and by faith confess and mourn over the sinful causes of them. Let me take every distress out of God's hand, and call on him in the time thereof, that he may deliver me.

I<span class="sc">N</span> thy great indignation,
&emsp;O Lord, rebuke me not;
Nor on me lay thy chast'ning hand,
&emsp;in thy displeasure hot.

2 For in me fast thine arrows stick,
&emsp;thine hand doth press me sore:

3 And in my flesh there is no health,
&emsp;nor soundness any more.
This grief I have, because thy wrath
&emsp;is forth against me gone;
And in my bones there is no rest,
&emsp;for sin that I have done

4 Because gone up above mine head
&emsp;my great transgressions be;
And, as a weighty burden, they
&emsp;too heavy are for me.

5 My wounds do stink, and are corrupt;
&emsp;my folly makes it so.

6 I troubled am, and much bow'd down;
&emsp;all day I mourning go.

7 For a disease that loathsome is
&emsp;so fills my loins with pain,
That in my weak and weary flesh
&emsp;no soundness doth remain.

8 So feeble and infirm am I,
&emsp;and broken am so sore,
That, through disquiet of my heart,
&emsp;I have been made to roar.

9 O Lord, all that I do desire
&emsp;is still before thine eye;
And of my heart the secret groans
&emsp;not hidden are from thee.

10 My heart doth pant incessantly,
&emsp;my strength doth quite decay;
As for mine eyes, their wonted light
&emsp;is from me gone away.

11 My lovers and my friends do stand
&emsp;at distance from my sore;
And those do stand aloof that were
&emsp;kinsmen and kind before.

12 Yea, they that seek my life lay snares:
&emsp;who seek to do me wrong
Speak things mischievous, and deceits
&emsp;imagine all day long.

13 But, as one deaf, that heareth not,
&emsp;I suffer'd all to pass;
I as a dumb man did become,
&emsp;whose mouth not open'd was:

14 As one that hears not, in whose mouth
&emsp;are no reproofs at all.

15 For, Lord, I hope in thee; my God,
&emsp;thou'lt hear me when I call.

16 For I said, Hear me, lest they should
&emsp;rejoice o'er me with pride;
And o'er me magnify themselves,
&emsp;when as my foot doth slide.

17 For I am near to halt, my grief
&emsp;is still before mine eye:

18 For I'll declare my sin, and grieve
&emsp;for mine iniquity.

19 But yet mine en'mies lively are,
&emsp;and strong are they beside;
And they that hate me wrongfully
&emsp;are greatly multiply'd.

20 And they for good that render ill,
&emsp;as en'mies me withstood;
Yea, ev'n for this, because that I
&emsp;do follow what is good.

21 Forsake me not, O Lord; my God,
&emsp;far from me never be.

22 O Lord, thou my salvation art,
&emsp;haste to give help to me.

## Psalm XXXIX.

*To the chief Musician, even to Jeduthun, A Psalm of David.*

Here we have (1) Violent struggling in the Psalmist's own breast between grace and corruption, between passion and patience, ver. 1-3, 11. (2) Serious views of human frailty, shortness of life, and self-emptiness, ver. 5, 6. (3) Strong cries to God for pardon of sin, preservation from reproach, and for removal of trouble; for hearing and answering of prayer, and for lengthening out of life, till further preparation for death should be attained, ver. 7-13. ¶ While I sing. let my soul blush deep for the untenderness and want of circumspection in my life, and for my want of resignation to God's disposing will. Let me be suitably affected with the shortness, vanity, and uncertainty of my temporal life. Let me be always exercised in earnest prayer to, and believing dependence on God, as my companion and friend.

I <span class="sc">SAID</span>, I will look to my ways,
&emsp;lest with my tongue I sin:
In sight of wicked men my mouth
&emsp;with bridle I'll keep in.

2 With silence I as dumb became,
&emsp;I did myself restrain
From speaking good; but then the more
&emsp;increased was my pain.

3 My heart within me waxed hot;
&emsp;and, while I musing was,
The fire did burn; and from my tongue
&emsp;these words I did let pass:

4 Mine end, and measure of my days,
&emsp;O Lord, unto me show
What is the same; that I thereby
&emsp;my frailty well may know.
