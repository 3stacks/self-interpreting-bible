## Psalm XXXII.

*A Psalm of David, Maschil.*

Perhaps this Psalm was composed for the great day of the national atonement, on the tenth day of the seventh month, Lev. xvi. In it, observe (1) The exceeding riches of the grace of God, manifested in blessing men with forgiveness of sin, and with protection amidst dangers, and direction in duty, ver. 1, 2, 7, 8. (2) The indispensable duty of them who desire new-covenant blessings, viz.—to acknowledge their offences to God; to implore his favours which they need; to walk humbly and circumspectly before him; and to rejoice in him as God and their God, ver. 3-6, 9-11. ¶ While I am truly conscious of my sinfulness, and deeply affected therewith, let the faith and experience of Jesus' full pardon of my sins, and of the communications of his grace, melt my heart, and animate me to every commanded duty.

O <span class="sc">BLESSED</span> is the man to whom
&emsp;is freely pardoned
All the transgression he hath done,
&emsp;whose sin is covered.

2 Bless'd is the man to whom the Lord
&emsp;imputeth not his sin,
And in whose sp'rit there is no guile,
&emsp;nor fraud is found therein.

3 When as I did refrain my speech,
&emsp;and silent was my tongue,
My bones then waxed old, because
&emsp;I roared all day long.

4 For upon me both day and night
&emsp;thine hand did heavy lie,
So that my moisture turned is
&emsp;in summer's drought thereby.

5 I thereupon have unto thee
&emsp;my sin acknowledged,
And likewise mine iniquity
&emsp;I have not covered:
I will confess unto the Lord
&emsp;my trespasses, said I;
And of my sin thou freely didst
&emsp;forgive th' iniquity.

6 For this shall ev'ry godly one
&emsp;his prayer make to thee;
In such a time he shall thee seek,
&emsp;as found thou mayest be.
Surely, when floods of waters great
&emsp;do swell up to the brim,
They shall not overwhelm his soul,
&emsp;nor once come near to him.

7 Thou art my hiding-place, thou shalt
&emsp;from trouble keep me free:
Thou with songs of deliverance
&emsp;about shalt compass me.

8 I will instruct thee, and thee teach
&emsp;the way that thou shalt go;
And, with mine eye upon thee set,
&emsp;I will direction show.

9 Then be not like the horse or mule,
&emsp;which do not understand;
Whose mouth, lest they come near to thee,
&emsp;a bridle must command.

10 Unto the man that wicked is
&emsp;his sorrows shall abound;
But him that trusteth in the Lord
&emsp;mercy shall compass round.

11 Ye righteous, in the Lord be glad,
&emsp;in him do ye rejoice:
All ye that upright are in heart,
&emsp;for joy lift up your voice.

## Psalm XXXIII.

Contains a sweet but solemn summons to praise the Lord, (1) For his justice, goodness, and truth, manifested in his word and works, ver. 1-5. (2) For his power, manifested in the work of creation, and in his sovereign dominion over the world, ver. 6-11, 13-17. (3) For his special and new-covenant relations, and merciful kindness towards his chosen people, ver. 12, 18-22. ¶ While I sing, let me observe, let me admire, and be deeply affected with, the blessings of creation, providence, and redemption. And chiefly, let me remember, let me behold, let me glow with ardent desire after Him, whose bones the Lord so kept that not one of them was broken.

Y<span class="sc">E</span> righteous, in the Lord rejoice;
&emsp;it comely is and right,
That upright men, with thankful voice,
&emsp;should praise the Lord of might.

2 Praise God with harp, and unto him
&emsp;sing with the psaltery;
Upon a ten-string'd instrument
&emsp;make ye sweet melody.

3 A new song to him sing, and play
&emsp;with loud noise skilfully;

4 For right is God's word, all his works
&emsp;are done in verity.

5 To judgment and to righteousness
&emsp;a love he beareth still;
The loving-kindness of the Lord
&emsp;the earth throughout doth fill.

6 The heavens by the word of God
&emsp;did their beginning take;
And by the breathing of his mouth
&emsp;he all their hosts did make.

7 The waters of the seas he brings
&emsp;together as an heap;
And in storehouses, as it were,
&emsp;he layeth up the deep.

8 Let earth, and all that live therein,
&emsp;with rev'rence fear the Lord;
Let all the world's inhabitants
&emsp;dread him with one accord.

9 For he did speak the word, and done
&emsp;it was without delay;
Established it firmly stood,
&emsp;whatever he did say.

10 God doth the counsel bring to nought
&emsp;which heathen folk do take;
And what the people do devise
&emsp;of none effect doth make.

11 O but the counsel of the Lord
&emsp;doth stand for ever sure;
And of his heart the purposes
&emsp;from age to age endure.

12 That nation blessed is, whose God
&emsp;<span class="sc">Jehovah</span> is, and those
A blessed people are, whom for
&emsp;his heritage he chose.

13 The Lord from heav'n sees and beholds
&emsp;all sons of men full well:

14 He views all from his dwelling-place
&emsp;that in the earth do dwell.

15 He forms their hearts alike, and all
&emsp;their doings he observes.

16 Great hosts save not a king, much strength
&emsp;no mighty man preserves.

17 An horse for preservation
&emsp;is a deceitful thing;
And by the greatness of his strength
&emsp;can no deliv'rance bring.

18 Behold, on those that do him fear
&emsp;the Lord doth set his eye;
Ev'n those who on his mercy do
&emsp;with confidence rely.

19 From death to free their soul, in dearth
&emsp;life unto them to yield.

20 Our soul doth wait upon the Lord;
&emsp;he is our help and shield.

21 Sith in his holy name we trust,
&emsp;our heart shall joyful be.

22 Lord, let thy mercy be on us,
&emsp;as we do hope in thee.

## Psalm XXXIV.

*A Psalm of David, when he changed his behaviour before Abimelech, who drove him away, and he departed.*

This Psalm was composed by David when Achish or Abimelech, king of Gath, drove him from his court, as an idiot or madman, 1 Sa. xxi. 10-25. Here are (1) David's high praises to God for the favours himself and others had received, ver. 1-6. (2) His warm invitations, and strong encouragements to others, to seek after and fear the Lord, and to trust in him for all necessary supplies, in time or in eternity, ver. 7-10. (3) Familiar advices to children and others, to eschew everything sinful, and make conscience of known duty, both towards God and men, as a mean of present and future happiness, ver. 11-14. (4) A representation of the misery of the wicked, in having God against them, as a just Lawgiver, and in having ruin before then as the just reward of their sins;—and of the happiness of the godly, in having God near to them in every case, ready to hear their requests, to protect them amidst dangers, and to deliver them from enemies and trouble, ver. 15-22. ¶ While I sing, let my heart be warmed with my subject. Come, my soul, and walk in this light, in these joys of the Lord. Come, taste and see that he is good. Extol his kindness, and trust him in all things, and on every occasion.

G<span class="sc">OD</span> will I bless all times; his praise
&emsp;my mouth shall still express.

2 My soul shall boast in God: the meek
&emsp;shall hear with joyfulness.

3 Extol the Lord with me, let us
&emsp;exalt his name together.

4 I sought the Lord, he heard, and did
&emsp;me from all fears deliver.

5 They look'd to him, and lighten'd were:
&emsp;not shamed were their faces.

6 This poor man cry'd, God heard, and sav'd
&emsp;him from all his distresses.

7 The angel of the Lord encamps,
&emsp;and round encompasseth
All those about that do him fear,
&emsp;and them delivereth.

8 O taste and see that God is good:
&emsp;who trusts in him is bless'd.

9 Fear God his saints: none that him fear
&emsp;shall be with want oppress'd.

10 The lions young may hungry be,
&emsp;and they may lack their food:
But they that truly seek the Lord
&emsp;shall not lack any good.

11 O children, hither do ye come,
&emsp;and unto me give ear;
I shall you teach to understand
&emsp;how ye the Lord should fear.

12 What man is he that life desires,
&emsp;to see good would live long?

13 Thy lips refrain from speaking guile,
&emsp;and from ill words thy tongue.

14 Depart from ill, do good, seek peace,
&emsp;pursue it earnestly.

15 God's eyes are on the just; his ears
&emsp;are open to their cry.

16 The face of God is set against
&emsp;those that do wickedly,
That he may quite out from the earth
&emsp;cut off their memory.

17 The righteous cry unto the Lord,
&emsp;he unto them gives ear;
And they out of their troubles all
&emsp;by him deliver'd are.

18 The Lord is ever nigh to them
&emsp;that be of broken sp'rit;
To them he safety doth afford
&emsp;that are in heart contrite.

19 The troubles that afflict the just
&emsp;in number many be;
But yet at length out of them all
&emsp;the Lord doth set him free.
