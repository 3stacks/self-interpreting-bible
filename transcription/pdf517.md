## Psalm CVIII.

*A Psalm or Song of David.*

The first five verses of this Psalm are taken from Ps. lvii. 7-11, and contain praises and thanksgivings to God. The other eight are taken from Ps. lx. 5-12, and contain prayers for, and hopes of, promised mercies. ¶ Never scruple, my soul, to think, or to sing, or to pray over the same subjects of importance: but let there be always a nearness of affection, and newness of spiritual vigour.

M<span class="sc">Y</span> heart is fix'd, Lord; I will sing,
&emsp;and with my glory praise.

2 Awake up psaltery and harp;
&emsp;myself I'll early raise.

3 I'll praise thee 'mong the people, Lord;
&emsp;'mong nations sing will I:

4 For above heav'n thy mercy's great,
&emsp;thy truth doth reach the sky.

5 Be thou above the heavens, Lord,
&emsp;exalted gloriously;
Thy glory all the earth above
&emsp;be lifted up on high.

6 That those who thy beloved are
&emsp;delivered may be,
O do thou save with thy right hand,
&emsp;and answer give to me.

7 God in his holiness hath said,
&emsp;Herein I will take pleasure;
Shechem I will divide, and forth
&emsp;will Succoth's valley measure.

8 Gilead I claim as mine by right;
&emsp;Manasseh mine shall be;
Ephraim is of my head the strength;
&emsp;Judah gives laws for me;

9 Moab's my washing-pot; my shoe
&emsp;I'll over Edom throw;
Over the land of Palestine
&emsp;I will in triumph go.

10 O who is he will bring me to
&emsp;the city fortify'd?
O who is he that to the land
&emsp;of Edom will me guide?

11 O God, thou who hadst cast us off,
&emsp;this thing wilt thou not do?
And wilt not thou, ev'n thou, O God,
&emsp;forth with our armies go?

12 Do thou from trouble give us help,
&emsp;for helpless is man's aid.

13 Through God we shall do valiantly;
&emsp;our foes he shall down tread.

## Psalm CIX.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

Probably David penned this Psalm with a view to Doeg or Ahithophel; and hence it is very like the 41st, 52d, 55th, 120th, and 140th. But the Holy Ghost herein hath a mediate respect to Jesus and Judas the traitor, Ac. i. 20. Observe (1) The Psalmist's bitter complaints on his enemies, as spiteful, deceitful, false, restless, unjust, and ungrateful, ver. 1-5. (2) His fearful denunciations of divine justice against his distinguished foe, that he should be delivered into the hand of Satan, should have his prayers rejected, his life shortened, his substance wasted, and his posterity rendered miserable, ver. 6-20. (3) His lamentation over his inward trouble, his approaching death, his instability of condition, pining sickness, reproach of enemies, and his prayers for mercy to himself, confusion to his enemies, and glory to his God, ver. 21-29. (4) His triumph in God, as his supporter and deliverer amidst all his troubles, ver. 30, 31. ¶ While I sing, let me be deeply affected with the sufferings of my Redeemer, and with the miseries of the Jews and others who abandon themselves to be his obstinate opposers. Let me implore, and by faith foresee, the failure of all my spiritual enemies, and the full redemption of my soul, and of the whole mystical body of Christ, notwithstanding all our sinful poverty and plagues.

O <span class="sc">Thou</span> the God of all my praise,
&emsp;do thou not hold thy peace;

2 For mouths of wicked men to speak
&emsp;against me do not cease:
The mouths of vile deceitful men
&emsp;against me open'd be;
And with a false and lying tongue
&emsp;they have accused me.

3 They did beset me round about
&emsp;with words of hateful spight,
And though to them no cause I gave,
&emsp;against me they did fight.

4 They for my love became my foes,
&emsp;but I me set to pray.

5 Evil for good, hatred for love,
&emsp;to me they did repay.

6 Set thou the wicked over him;
&emsp;and upon his right hand
Give thou his greatest enemy,
&emsp;ev'n Satan, leave to stand.

7 And when by thee he shall be judg'd,
&emsp;let him condemned be;
And let his pray'r be turn'd to sin,
&emsp;when he shall call on thee.

8 Few be his days, and in his room
&emsp;his charge another take.

9 His children let be fatherless,
&emsp;his wife a widow make.

10 His children let be vagabonds,
&emsp;and beg continually;
And from their places desolate
&emsp;seek bread for their supply.

11 Let covetous extortioners
&emsp;catch all he hath away:
Of all for which he labour'd hath
&emsp;let strangers make a prey.

12 Let there be none to pity him,
&emsp;let there be none at all
That on his children fatherless
&emsp;will let his mercy fall.

13 Let his posterity from earth
&emsp;cut off for ever be,
And in the foll'wing age their name
&emsp;be blotted out by thee.

14 Let God his father's wickedness
&emsp;still to remembrance call;
And never let his mother's sin
&emsp;be blotted out at all.

15 But let them all before the Lord
&emsp;appear continually,
That he may wholly from the earth
&emsp;cut off their memory.

16 Because he mercy minded not,
&emsp;but persecuted still
The poor and needy, that he might
&emsp;the broken-hearted kill.

17 As he in cursing pleasure took,
&emsp;so let it to him fall;
As he delighted not to bless,
&emsp;so bless him not at all.

18 As cursing he like clothes put on,
&emsp;into his bowels so,
Like water, and into his bones,
&emsp;like oil, down let it go.

19 Like to the garment let it be
&emsp;which doth himself array,
And for a girdle, wherewith he
&emsp;is girt about alway.

20 From God let this be their reward
&emsp;that en'mies are to me,
And their reward that speak against
&emsp;my soul maliciously.

21 But do thou, for thine own name's sake,
&emsp;O God the Lord, for me:
Sith good and sweet thy mercy is,
&emsp;from trouble set me free.

22 For I am poor and indigent,
&emsp;afflicted sore am I,
My heart within me also is
&emsp;wounded exceedingly.

23 I pass like a declining shade,
&emsp;am like the locust tost:

24 My knees through fasting weaken'd are,
&emsp;my flesh hath fatness lost.

25 I also am a vile reproach
&emsp;unto them made to be;
And they that did upon me look
&emsp;did shake their heads at me.

26 O do thou help and succour me,
&emsp;who art my God and Lord:
And, for thy tender mercy's sake,
&emsp;safety to me afford;

27 That thereby they may know that this
&emsp;is thy almighty hand;
And that thou, Lord, hast done the same,
&emsp;they may well understand.

28 Although they curse with spite, yet, Lord,
&emsp;bless thou with loving voice:
Let them asham'd be when they rise;
&emsp;thy servant let rejoice.

29 Let thou mine adversaries all
&emsp;with shame be clothed over;
And let their own confusion
&emsp;them, as a mantle, cover.

30 But as for me, I with my mouth
&emsp;will greatly praise the Lord;
And I among the multitude
&emsp;his praises will record.

31 For he shall stand at his right hand
&emsp;who is in poverty,
To save him from all those that would
&emsp;condemn his soul to die.

## Psalm CX.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm relates wholly to Jesus Christ: and represents him (1) In his glorious offices, as a Prophet, gathering the nations by the proclamation of his gospel as the rod of his strength, ver. 2, 3; as our great High-priest, ver. 4; and as our exalted, victorious, and governing King, ver. 2, 3, 5, 6. (2) In his diversified states of debasement and exaltation, ver. 7, 1, 5. ¶ While I sing, let mine eyes be fixed, wholly fixed on my glorious Redeemer. Behold! how, having by himself purged our sins, he sat down on the right hand of the Majesty on high! Behold him, by the word of his grace, winning the race of sinful men to himself. Behold him able to save to the uttermost, because he ever liveth to make intercession for us. And let my mouth be filled with Hallelujahs and Hosannas in the highest, to Him who, by water and by blood, cometh in the name of the Lord to save me.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> Lord did say unto my Lord,
&emsp;Sit thou at my right hand,
Until I make thy foes a stool,
&emsp;whereon thy feet may stand.
