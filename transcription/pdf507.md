## Psalm LXXX.

*To the chief Musician, upon Shoshannim-Eduth, A Psalm of Asaph.*

This Psalm relates to the distressed condition of the Hebrew church and nation, perhaps during the Syrian, Assyrian, or Chaldean invasions, 2 Ki. xii. xiii. xv. xvii. xviii. xix. xxiv. xxv. Here are (1) Earnest supplications for God's special presence and favour, ver. 1-3, 7, 19. (2) Mournful complaints of the lasting appearances of God's anger; of the overflowings of their grief; of abuse and derision by their neighbours; and of the unhinging of their constitution, which God himself had fixed when he brought them out of Egypt, ver. 4-13. (3) Further supplications, that God would consider and pity his vineyard, their church and nation; assist their king, his vine-dresser; and convert them to, and quicken them in his service, ver. 14-19. ¶ While I sing, let me be affected with the distresses of God's church; let me pant for the fullest manifestations of his glory, and the blessings of his presence; and let me insist that his Spirit may glorify Jesus Christ, the great *Man of his right hand,* in taking what is his, and showing it unto me.

H<span class="sc">EAR</span>, Isr'el's Shepherd! like a flock
&emsp;thou that dost Joseph guide;
Shine forth, O thou that dost between
&emsp;the cherubim abide
2 In Ephraim's and Benjamin's,
&emsp;and in Manasseh's sight,
O come for our salvation;
&emsp;stir up thy strength and might.

3 Turn us again, O Lord our God,
&emsp;and upon us vouchsafe
To make thy countenance to shine,
&emsp;and so we shall be safe.
4 O Lord of hosts, almighty God,
&emsp;how long shall kindled be
Thy wrath against the prayer made
&emsp;by thine own folk to thee?

5 Thou tears of sorrow giv'st to them
&emsp;instead of bread to eat;
Yea, tears instead of drink thou giv'st
&emsp;to them in measure great.
6 Thou makest us a strife unto
&emsp;our neighbours round about;
Our enemies among themselves
&emsp;at us do laugh and flout.

7 Turn us again, O God of hosts,
&emsp;and upon us vouchsafe
To make thy countenance to shine,
&emsp;and so we shall be safe.
8 A vine from Egypt brought thou hast,
&emsp;by thine outstretched hand;
And thou the heathen out didst cast,
&emsp;to plant it in their land.

9 Before it thou a room didst make,
&emsp;where it might grow and stand;
Thou causedst it deep root to take,
&emsp;and it did fill the land.
10 The mountains vail'd were with its shade,
&emsp;as with a covering;

&emsp;which out from it did spring.

11 Upon the one hand to the sea
&emsp;her boughs she did out send;
On th' other side unto the flood
&emsp;her branches did extend.
12 Why hast thou then thus broken down
&emsp;and ta'en her hedge away?

&emsp;and make of her a prey.

13 The boar who from the forest comes
&emsp;doth waste it at his pleasure;
The wild beast of the field also
&emsp;devours it out of measure.
14 O God of hosts, we thee beseech,
&emsp;return now unto thine;
Look down from heav'n in love, behold,
&emsp;and visit this thy vine:

15 This vineyard, which thine own right hand
&emsp;hath planted us among;
And that same branch, which for thyself
&emsp;thou hast made to be strong.
16 Burnt up it is with flaming fire,
&emsp;it also is cut down:
They utterly are perished
&emsp;when as thy face doth frown.

17 O let thy hand be still upon
&emsp;the Man of thy right hand,

&emsp;thou madest strong to stand.
18 So henceforth we will not go back,
&emsp;nor turn from thee at all:
O do thou quicken us, and we
&emsp;upon thy name will call.

19 Turn us again, Lord God of hosts,
&emsp;and upon us vouchsafe

&emsp;and so we shall be safe.

## Psalm LXXXI.

*To the chief Musician, upon Gittith, A Psalm of Asaph.*

This Psalm was probably composed for the feast of trumpets on the first day of the 7th month, Le. xxiii. 24. Here are (1) Solemn exhortations to praise God for what he is to his people, and for what he hath done for them, ver. 1-7. (2) Instructions concerning God's new-covenant grant of himself, and his benefits to men; their ungrateful refusal thereof; and the misery they incur, and the happiness they lose thereby, ver. 8-16. ¶ While I sing, let my soul consider what God is to, hath done for, and given to <span class="sc">men</span>—to <span class="sc">me</span>; and let all my inward powers steadfastly believe his declarations, and eagerly embrace his offers. Let my heart be filled with grief that ever I refused to hear my own gracious God speaking from heaven, and offering to <span class="sc">me</span> all the unsearchable riches of Christ.

S<span class="sc">ING</span> loud to God our strength; with joy
&emsp;to Jacob's God do sing.
2 Take up a psalm, the pleasant harp,
&emsp;timbrel and psalt'ry bring.

3 Blow trumpets at new-moon, what day
&emsp;our feast appointed is:
4 For charge to Isr'el, and a law
&emsp;of Jacob's God was this.

5 To Joseph this a testimony
&emsp;he made, when Egypt land
He travell'd through, where speech I heard
&emsp;I did not understand.
6 His shoulder I from burdens took,
&emsp;his hands from pots did free.
7 Thou didst in trouble on me call,
&emsp;and I deliver'd thee:

In secret place of thundering
&emsp;I did thee answer make;
And at the streams of Meribah
&emsp;of thee a proof did take.
8 O thou, my people, give an ear,
&emsp;I'll testify to thee;
To thee, O Isr'el, if thou wilt
&emsp;but hearken unto me.

9 In midst of thee there shall not be
&emsp;any strange god at all;
Nor unto any god unknown
&emsp;thou bowing down shalt fall.
10 I am the Lord thy God, which did
&emsp;from Egypt land thee guide;
I'll fill thy mouth abundantly,
&emsp;do thou it open wide.

11 But yet my people to my voice
&emsp;would not attentive be;
And ev'n my chosen Israel
&emsp;he would have none of me.
12 So to the lust of their own hearts
&emsp;I them delivered;
And then in counsels of their own
&emsp;they vainly wandered.

13 O that my people had me heard,
&emsp;Isr'el my ways had chose!
14 I had their en'mies soon subdu'd,
&emsp;my hand turn'd on their foes.
15 The haters of the Lord to him
&emsp;submission should have feign'd;
But as for them, their time should have
&emsp;for evermore remain'd.

16 He should have also fed them with
&emsp;the finest of the wheat;
Of honey from the rock thy fill
&emsp;I should have made thee eat.

## Psalm LXXXII.

*A Psalm of Asaph.*

This Psalm was probably penned for the direction and warning of the Jewish courts of judicature. It represents (1) The dignity of magistrates, and their dependence on God as their sovereign, governor, and judge, ver. 1, 6. (2) Their duty, ver. 3, 4. (3) Their too frequent degeneracy and mischievous corruption, and the just punishment thereof, ver. 2, 5, 7. (4) The saints' request for the establishment of God's kingdom in the world, ver. 8. ¶ While I sing, let me stand in awe of <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>'s authority and presence. Let me remember I must be answerable to him for all my conduct. Let me be affected with my meanness and corruption. Let me revere magistrates as the deputies of God on earth. And in every station in which God places me, let my care be, in all things to live honestly, and to cry mightily that the kingdoms of this world may quickly be made the kingdoms of my Lord and of his Christ.

I<span class="sc">N</span> gods' assembly God doth stand;
&emsp;he judgeth gods among.
2 How long, accepting persons vile,
&emsp;will ye give judgment wrong?
3 Defend the poor and fatherless;
&emsp;to poor oppress'd do right.
4 The poor and needy ones set free;
&emsp;rid them from ill men's might.

5 They know not, nor will understand;
&emsp;in darkness they walk on.
All the foundations of the earth
&emsp;out of their course are gone.
