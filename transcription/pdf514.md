13 Thou shalt arise, and mercy yet
Thou to mount Sion shalt extend:
Her time for favour which was set,
Behold, is now come to an end.
14 Thy saints take pleasure in her stones,
Her very dust to them is dear.
15 All heathen lands and kingly thrones
On earth thy glorious name shall fear.

16 God in his glory shall appear,
When Sion he builds and repairs.
17 He shall regard and lend his ear
Unto the needy's humble pray'rs:
Th' afflicted's pray'r he will not scorn.
18 All times this shall be on record:
And generations yet unborn
Shall praise and magnify the Lord.

19 He from his holy place look'd down,
The earth he view'd from heav'n on high,
20 To hear the pris'ner's mourning groan,
And free them that are doom'd to die;
21 That Sion, and Jerus'lem too,
His name and praise may well record,
22 When people and the kingdoms do
Assemble all to praise the Lord.

23 My strength he weaken'd in the way,
My days of life he shortened.
24 My God, O take me not away
In mid-time of my days, I said:
Thy years throughout all ages last.
25 Of old thou hast established
The earth's foundation firm and fast:
Thy mighty hands the heav'ns have made,

26 They perish shall, as garments do,
But thou shalt evermore endure;
As vestures, thou shalt change them so;
And they shall all be changed sure:
27 But from all changes thou art free;
Thy endless years do last for aye.
28 Thy servants, and their seed who be,
Establish'd shall before thee stay.

## Psalm CIII.

*A Psalm of David.*

Here (1) The Psalmist earnestly stirs up himself to praise God for favours to himself in particular; for pardon of sins, healing of diseases, redemption from death, ver. 1-5; and for his mercies to his people, in revealing his mind to them, pardoning their sins, sympathizing with their weakness, pitying their afflictions, and perpetuating his new-covenant mercy towards them and their seed, ver. 6-18. (2) Conscious of his own insufficiency, he requests the assistance of angels, and of all the works of God to praise him, who is their Sovereign, ver. 19-22. ¶ Thus, my soul, let all my inward powers be stirred up to praise and thank my Redeemer, for all that he hath done for me,—for Britain, and for his church on earth.

O <span class="sc">Thou</span> my soul, bless God the Lord;
&emsp;and all that in me is
Be stirred up his holy name
&emsp;to magnify and bless.

2 Bless, O my soul, the Lord thy God,
&emsp;and not forgetful be
Of all his gracious benefits
&emsp;he hath bestow'd on thee.

3 All thine iniquities who doth
&emsp;most graciously forgive:
Who thy diseases all and pains
&emsp;doth heal, and thee relieve;
4 Who doth redeem thy life, that thou
&emsp;to death may'st not go down;
Who thee with loving-kindness doth
&emsp;and tender mercies crown:

5 Who with abundance of good things
&emsp;doth satisfy thy mouth;
So that, ev'n as the eagle's age,
&emsp;renewed is thy youth.
6 God righteous judgment executes
&emsp;for all oppressed ones.
7 His ways to Moses, he his acts
&emsp;made known to Isr'el's sons.

8 The Lord our God is merciful,
&emsp;and he is gracious,
Long-suffering, and slow to wrath,
&emsp;in mercy plenteous.
9 He will not chide continually,
&emsp;nor keep his anger still.
10 With us he dealt not as we sinn'd,
&emsp;nor did requite our ill.

11 For as the heaven in its height
&emsp;the earth surmounteth far:
So great to those that do him fear
&emsp;his tender mercies are:
12 As far as east is distant from
&emsp;the west, so far hath he
From us removed, in his love,
all our iniquity.

13 Such pity as a father hath
&emsp;unto his children dear;
Like pity shows the Lord to such
&emsp;as worship him in fear,
14 For he remembers we are dust,
&emsp;and he our frame well knows.
15 Frail man, his days are like the grass,
&emsp;as flow'r in field he grows:

16 For over it the wind doth pass,
&emsp;and it away is gone;
And of the place where once it was
&emsp;it shall no more be known.
17 But unto them that do him fear
&emsp;God's mercy never ends;
And to their children's children still
&emsp;his righteousness extends:

18 To such as keep his covenant,
&emsp;and mindful are alway
Of his most just commandements,
&emsp;that they may them obey.
19 The Lord prepared hath his throne
&emsp;in heavens firm to stand;
And ev'ry thing that being hath
&emsp;his kingdom doth command.

20 O ye his angels, that excel
&emsp;in strength, bless ye the Lord;
Ye who obey what he commands,
&emsp;and hearken to his word.
21 O bless and magnify the Lord,
&emsp;ye glorious hosts of his;
Ye ministers, that do fulfil
&emsp;whate'er his pleasure is.

22 O bless the Lord, all ye his works,
&emsp;wherewith the world is stor'd
In his dominions ev'ry where.
&emsp;My soul, bless thou the Lord.

## Psalm CIV.

This Psalm was perhaps composed along with the preceding one; and contains (1) High celebrations of God as the God of nature; particularly of his appearance in his heavenly palaces, ver. 1-4; of his creation of the sea and dry land, ver. 5-9; of the provision he makes for his creatures according to their respective needs, ver. 10-28; and of his sovereign dominion and influence over them, ver. 29-32. (2) Holy resolutions to meditate on and praise the Lord in the view of his defeating of the wicked, ver. 83-85. ¶ Lo! how stretched heavens, how refulgent luminaries, how fertile fields, how towering woods, how roaring seas, how ranging brutes, how labouring men, how flaming seraphs—unite their force to awaken my soul, to tune my heart to the high praises of her <span class="sc">Redeemer</span>! Let me meet my God, my Christ in every view. In every sound let me hear his voice. In every form let me discern how great is his goodness, and how great is his beauty! Nor let creation's lower plains detain my heart. Let new-covenant relations to my unbounded <span class="sc">all</span>; let redemption through his blood fix all my thoughts, enrapture all my inward powers, that I many sing anthems, all on fire, to Him who loved me and gave himself for me.

B<span class="sc">LESS</span> God, my soul. O Lord my God,
&emsp;thou art exceeding great;
With honour and with majesty
&emsp;thon clothed art in state.
2 With light, as with a robe, thyself
&emsp;thou coverest about;
And, like unto a curtain, thou
&emsp;the heavens stretchest out.

3 Who of his chambers doth the beams
&emsp;within the waters lay;
Who doth the clouds his chariot make,
&emsp;on wings of wind make way.
4 Who flaming fire his ministers,
&emsp;his angels spirits, doth make:
5 Who earth's foundations did lay,
&emsp;that it should never shake.

6 Thou didst it cover with the deep,
&emsp;as with a garment spread:
The waters stood above the hills,
&emsp;when thou the word but said.
7 But at the voice of thy rebuke
&emsp;they fled, and would not stay;
They at thy thunder's dreadful voice
&emsp;did haste them fast away.

8 They by the mountains do ascend,
&emsp;and by the valley-ground
Descend, unto that very place
&emsp;which thou for them didst found,
9 Thou hast a bound unto them set,
&emsp;that they may not pass over,
That they do not return again
&emsp;the'face of earth to cover.

10 He to the valleys sends the springs
&emsp;which run among the hills:
11 They to all beasts of field give drink,
&emsp;wild asses drink their fills.

12 By them the fowls of heav'n shall have
&emsp;their habitation,
Which do among the branches sing
&emsp;with delectation.

13 He from his chambers watereth
&emsp;the hills, when they are dry'd:
With fruit and increase of thy works
&emsp;the earth is satisfy'd.

14 For cattle he makes grass to grow,
&emsp;he makes the herb to spring
For th' use of man, that food to him
&emsp;he from the earth may bring;

15 And wine, that to the heart of man
&emsp;doth cheerfulness impart,
Oil that his face makes shine, and bread
&emsp;that strengtheneth his heart.

16 The trees of God are full of sap;
&emsp;the cedars that do stand
In Lebanon, which planted were
&emsp;by his almighty hand.

17 Birds of the air upon their boughs
&emsp;do choose their nests to make;
As for the stork, the fir-tree she
&emsp;doth for her dwelling take.
18 The lofty mountains for wild goats
&emsp;a place of refuge be;
The conies also to the rocks
&emsp;do for their safety flee.

19 He sets the moon in heav'n, thereby
&emsp;the seasons to discern:
From him the sun his certain time
&emsp;of going down doth learn.
20 Thou darkness mak'st, 'tis night, then beasts
&emsp;of forests creep abroad.
21 The lions young roar for their prey,
&emsp;and seek their meat from God.

22 The sun doth rise, and home they flock,
&emsp;down in their dens they lie.
23 Man goes to work, his labour he
&emsp;doth to the ev'ning ply.
24 How manifold, Lord, are thy works!
&emsp;in wisdom wonderful
Thou ev'ry one of them hast made;
&emsp;earth's of thy riches full:

25 So is this great and spacious sea,
&emsp;wherein things creeping are,
Which number'd cannot be; and beasts
&emsp;both great and small are there.
26 There ships go; there thou mak'st to play
&emsp;that leviathan great.
27 These all wait on thee, that thou may'st
&emsp;in due time give them meat.
