But all obedience to God's law must be immediately founded on what God is, and is to us, and hath done for us. And, oh! what great pleasure God takes in granting himself to be our God in Christ! Indeed, every peculiar circumstance which men can be in hath its own peculiar enforcements of duty to God and our neighbour. In what a solemn manner was God's law delivered! and it is a mercy that we have his word written for us, for the greater certainty and the better spreading thereof. But it is still a greater mercy that we have one Mediator between God and men—the man Christ Jesus; and so receive the law as fulfilled in Christ: and it is most acceptable to God when sinful men, terrified by the holy law, kindly flee to Jesus and his mediation; when our hearts are right with him; and especially when the views of his promise, his law, and the mediation of Christ and our own vows, concur in animating us to the greatest exactness in all holy obedience.

## Chapter VI.
[Ver. 4. *Hear, O Israel.* This is one of the passages the Jews inscribe on their phylacteries, and which they regard as one of the most pregnant with divine truth and instruction. The words are spoken not merely as instructions from Moses to Israel, but as a confession of faith common to Moses and Israel: 'Hear, O Israel, <span class="sc">Jehovah</span> *our Elohim* is one <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>.' The words assert most emphatically the *unity* of the Godhead in the singular—<span class="sc">Jehovah</span>; while they equally assert the *plurality* of the persons by the plural—<span class="sc">Elohim</span>. The *unity* of the Godhead is asserted in opposition to 'the gods many and lords many' of the heathen; while the *plurality* of persons is asserted as the foundation and key to the great mystery of redemption.
