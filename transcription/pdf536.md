## Paraphrase XXIX.

*Lam. iii. 37-40.*

The design of the Prophet in the words paraphrased is to reconcile us to the afflictions of life Under them let us look up to God's sovereignty and his agency; in all that happens us, let us remember our sins are the cause of all the evils we suffer, and our reformation the end God intends: let us complain to God, but never of him. O my soul, despise not thou the chastening of the Lord, nor faint when thou art rebuked of him.

A<span class="sc">MIDST</span> the mighty, where is he
&emsp;who saith, and it is done?
Each varying scene of changeful life
&emsp;is from the Lord alone.

2 He gives in gladsome bow'rs to dwell,
&emsp;or clothes in sorrow's shroud;
His hand hath form'd the light, his hand
&emsp;hath form'd the dark'ning cloud.

3 Why should a living man complain
&emsp;beneath the chast'ning rod?
Our sins afflict us; and the cross
&emsp;must bring us back to God.

4 O sons of men! with anxious care
&emsp;your hearts and ways explore;
Return from paths of vice to God:
&emsp;return, and sin no more!

## Paraphrase XXX.

*Hosea vi. 1-4.*

Here are God's gracious directions how to act and speak in true repentance, and great encouragement to repent. While I sing these lines, let me think how ruinous sin is, and how necessary a gospel repentance for it is. Nothing more effectually leads to this than a firm faith of God's gospel grant of himself as our God. How marvellous that God should instruct what to pray for, as well as readily grant our requests! How happy and delightful to deal with a God of infinite mercy and goodness! How answerable his kind promises to the needs and prayers of those whom he instructs! O the marvellous change made on persons and societies by his all-powerful grace! Lord, under the influence of thy grace, may I grow up to him who is the head in all things, and be a good savour of Christ to all amongst whom I dwell.

C<span class="sc">OME</span>, let us to the Lord our God
&emsp;with contrite hearts return;
Our God is gracious, nor will leave
&emsp;the desolate to mourn.

2 His voice commands the tempest forth,
&emsp;and stills the stormy wave;
And though his arm be strong to smite,
&emsp;'tis also strong to save.

3 Long hath the night of sorrow reign'd;
&emsp;the dawn shall bring us light:
God shall appear, and we shall rise
&emsp;with gladness in his sight.

4 Our hearts, if God we seek to know,
&emsp;shall know him, and rejoice;
His coming like the morn shall be,
&emsp;like morning songs his voice.

5 As dew upon the tender herb,
&emsp;diffusing fragrance round;
As show'rs that usher in the spring,
&emsp;and cheer the thirsty ground:

6 So shall his presence bless our souls,
&emsp;and shed a joyful light;
That hallow'd morn shall chase away
&emsp;the sorrows of the night.

## Paraphrase XXXI.

*Micah vi. 6-9.*

In this passage God represents to men the vain methods they would take to recommend themselves to his favour, and then shows them the true method to enjoy this singular blessing. Think, my soul, what anxious inquiries after peace and pardon, deep convictions of guilt, or fondness of an enterprise, may occasion! By what costly expedients they would willingly clear their way. Not all the inventions of men can recommend us to God; and faith in the atonement, and in God as ours in Christ, true repentance and real holiness, are preferable to every ceremonial service. O the infinite kindness of God, in teaching us both by his word and rod!

T<span class="sc">HUS</span> speaks the heathen: How shall man
&emsp;the Pow'r Supreme adore?
With what accepted off'rings come
&emsp;his mercy to implore?

2 Shall clouds of incense to the skies
&emsp;with grateful odour speed?
Or victims from a thousand hills
&emsp;upon the altar bleed?

3 Does justice nobler blood demand
&emsp;to save the sinner's life?
Shall, trembling, in his offspring's side
&emsp;the father plunge the knife?

4 No: God rejects the bloody rites
&emsp;which blindfold zeal began;
His oracles of truth proclaim
&emsp;the message brought to man.

5 He what is good hath clearly shown,
&emsp;O favour'd race! to thee;
And what doth God require of those
&emsp;who bend to him the knee?

6 Thy deeds, let sacred justice rule;
&emsp;thy heart, let mercy fill;
And, walking humbly with thy God,
&emsp;to him resign thy will.

## Paraphrase XXXII.

*Hab. iii. 17, 18.*

In this passage the prophet seems, under a view of the Chaldean invasion, to have supposed they would destroy every outward comfort in the country, so declares it as his resolution that he will take God as his all, and rejoice in him. O my soul, let me, like many of the best of men, under the want of all things, when all around is dark and dreary, claim God as mine, and rejoice in him, and particularly in the assured hope, that in the better world I shall hunger no more, nor thirst any more; but the Lamb who is in the midst of the throne shall lead me to fountains of living waters.

W<span class="sc">HAT</span> though no flow'rs the fig-tree clothe,
&emsp;though vines their fruit deny,
The labour of the olive fail,
&emsp;and fields no meat supply?

2 Though from the fold, with sad surprise,
&emsp;my flock cut off I see;
Though famine pine in empty stalls,
&emsp;where herds were wont to be?

3 Yet in the Lord will I be glad,
&emsp;and glory in his love:
In him I'll joy, who will the God
&emsp;of my salvation prove.

4 He to my tardy feet shall lend
&emsp;the swiftness of the roe;
Till, rais'd on high, I safely dwell
&emsp;beyond the reach of woe.

5 God is the treasure of my soul,
&emsp;the source of lasting joy;
A joy which want shall not impair,
&emsp;nor death itself destroy.

## Paraphrase XXXIII.

*Mat. vi. 9-14.*

The prayer paraphrased here is at once the best form and the best model of prayer: and, short as it is, it is a summary of almost everything a Christian may or ought to seek from God. We are taught in it, in all our prayers to view God in Christ as our Father, and to prefer the concerns of his glory to our comfort We are further taught to ask for ourselves a competent portion of temporal things with God's blessing, the remission of our sins, our preservation from temptation, and our final deliverance from all sin and all misery; and withal, the conclusion contains an ascription of glory to God, and intimates we must draw our encouragement in prayer from nothing but from God himself. Let us say with the disciples, Lord, teach us to pray; and with the apostle, We know not what we should pray for as we ought; by thy Spirit make intercession for us.

F<span class="sc">ATHER</span> of all! we bow to thee,
&emsp;who dwell'st in heav'n ador'd;
But present still through all thy works,
&emsp;the universal Lord.

2 For ever hallow'd be thy name
&emsp;by all beneath the skies;
And may thy kingdom still advance,
&emsp;till grace to glory rise.

3 A grateful homage may we yield,
&emsp;with hearts resign'd to thee;
And as in heav'n thy will is done,
&emsp;on earth so let it be.

4 From day to day we humbly own
&emsp;the hand that feeds us still:
Give us our bread, and teach to rest
&emsp;contented in thy will.

5 Our sins before thee we confess;
&emsp;O may they be forgiv'n!
As we to others mercy show,
&emsp;we mercy beg from Heav'n.

6 Still let thy grace our life direct;
&emsp;from evil guard our way;
And in temptation's fatal path
&emsp;permit us not to stray.

7 For thine the pow'r, the kingdom thine;
&emsp;all glory's due to thee:
Thine from eternity they were,
&emsp;and thine shall ever be.

## Paraphrase XXXIV.

*Mat. xi. 25, to the end.*

Here we have first a prayer by the Redeemer to his Father, acquiescing in the awful and gracious determinations of his high decree, and one of the most eminent calls of the gospel, with its foundation. While I sing this, let me think of the unbounded and delightful fitness and fulness of Christ to save sinners, even the chief: and not by natural qualifications, or works of righteousness, but according to his own and his Father's rich and sovereign grace. How tender, extensive, and unhampered his invitations of self destroyed, restless slaves of Satan! How full his assurance of salvation directed to them! How pleasant the views of his love and influences of his Spirit render their service; and how easy his yoke of holiness and sufferings, in their way to their glorious crown!

T<span class="sc">HUS</span> spoke the Saviour of the world,
&emsp;and rais'd his eyes to heav'n:
To thee, O Father! Lord of all,
&emsp;eternal praise be giv'n.

2 Thou to the pure and lowly heart
&emsp;hast heav'nly truth reveal'd;
Which from the self-conceited mind
&emsp;thy wisdom hath conceal'd.

3 Ev'n so! thou, Father, hast ordain'd
&emsp;thy high decree to stand;
Nor men nor angels may presume
&emsp;the reason to demand.

4 Thou only know'st the Son: from thee
&emsp;my kingdom I receive;
And none the Father know but they
&emsp;who in the Son believe.

5 Come then to me, all ye who groan,
&emsp;with guilt and fears opprest;
Resign to me the willing heart,
&emsp;and I will give you rest.

6 Take up my yoke, and learn of me
&emsp;the meek and lowly mind;
And thus your weary troubled souls
&emsp;repose and peace shall find.

7 For light and gentle is my yoke;
&emsp;the burden I impose
Shall ease the heart, which groan'd before
&emsp;beneath a load of woes.

## Paraphrase XXXV.

*Mat. xxvi. 26-29.*

Jesus, having loved his own which were in the world, loved them to the end. On this account, on the last night of his life, he instituted the sacramental supper, to be the perpetual memorial of his dying love. The bread represents him as God-man, chosen, appointed, and furnished by his Father, and suffering unto death for the redemption of his people. The wine represents his fulfilling all righteousness for the bringing and securing of all promised new-covenant blessings, particularly the full remission of sins to all who believe. Let a man examine himself, and so let him eat.

'T<span class="sc">WAS</span> on that night, when doom'd to know
The eager rage of ev'ry foe,
That night in which he was betray'd,
The Saviour of the world took bread:

2 And, after thanks and glory giv'n
To him that rules in earth and heav'n,
That symbol of his flesh he broke,
And thus to all his foll'wers spoke:

3 My broken body thus I give
For you, for all; take, eat, and live;
And oft the sacred rite renew,
That brings my wondrous love to view.

4 Then in his hands the cup he rais'd,
And God anew he thank'd and prais'd;
While kindness in his bosom glow'd,
And from his lips salvation flow'd.

5 My blood I thus pour forth, he cries,
To cleanse the soul in sin that lies;
In this the covenant is seal'd,
And Heav'n's eternal grace reveal'd.
