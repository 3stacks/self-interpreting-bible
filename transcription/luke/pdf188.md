19:1 And *Jesus* entered and passed through {a}Jericho.
19:2 And, behold, *there was* a man named Zacchæus, {b}which was the chief among the publicans, and he was rich.{1}
19:3 And he {c}sought to see Jesus, who he was; and could not for the press, because he was little of stature.
19:4 And he ran before, and climbed up into a {d}sycamore-tree to see him: for he was to pass that *way*.
19:5 And when Jesus came to the place, he looked{e} up, and saw him, and said unto him, Zacchæus, {g}make haste, and come down; for to-day {h}I must abide at thy house.
19:6 And{i} he made haste, and came down, and received him joyfully.
19:7 And when they saw *it*, {j}they all murmured, saying, That he was gone to be guest with a man that is a sinner.
19:8 And{k} Zacchæus stood, and said unto the Lord, Behold, Lord, the half of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken anything from any man by false accusation, I restore *him* fourfold.{2}
19:9 And Jesus said unto him, This day is {l}salvation come to this house, forsomuch as he also is{n} a son of Abraham.{3}
19:10 For{o} the Son of man is come to seek and to save that which was lost.
19:11 ¶ And as they heard these things, he added and spake a parable,{4} because he was nigh to Jerusalem, and because {p}they thought that the kingdom of God should immediately appear.
19:12 He{q} said therefore, A certain nobleman went into {r}a far country, {s}to receive for himself a kingdom,{5} and {t}to return.
19:13 And he called his ten servants, and {u}delivered them ten pounds,{6} and said unto them, Occupy till I come.
19:14 But his citizens{7} hated him, and sent a message after him, saying, We will not have this *man* to reign over us.
19:15 And it came to pass, that when he was returned, having received the kingdom, {v}then he commanded these servants to be called unto him, to whom he had given the money,{8} that he might know how much every man had gained by trading.
19:16 Then came the first, saying, Lord, {x}thy pound hath gained ten pounds.
19:17 And he said unto him, {y}Well, thou good servant: because thou hast been faithful in a very little, have thou authority over ten cities.
19:18 And the second came, saying, Lord, {z}thy pound hath gained five pounds.
19:19 And he said likewise to him, {a}Be thou also over five cities.
19:20 And another came, saying, {b}Lord, behold, *here is* thy pound, which I have kept laid up in a napkin:
19:21 For I {c}feared thee, {d}because thou art an austere man: thou takest up that thou layedst not down, and reapest that thou didst not sow.
19:22 And he saith unto him, {e}Out of thine own mouth will I judge thee, *thou* wicked servant. Thou knewest{9} that I was an austere man, taking up that I laid not down, and reaping that I did not sow:
19:23 Wherefore{g} then gavest not thou my money into the bank, that at my coming I might have required mine own with usury?
19:24 And he said unto them that stood by, Take from him the pound, and give *it* to him that hath ten pounds.
19:25 (And they said unto him, Lord, {i}he hath ten pounds.){1}
19:26 For I say unto you, {j}That unto every one which hath shall be given; {k}and from him that hath not, even that he hath shall be taken away from him.
19:27 But{2} those mine enemies, which would not that I should reign over them, bring hither, and slay *them* before me.
19:28 ¶ And when he had thus spoken, {l}he went before, ascending up to Jerusalem.{3}
19:29 And{n} it came to pass, when he was come nigh to Bethphage and Bethany, at the mount called *the mount* of Olives, he sent two of his disciples,{4}
19:30 Saying, Go ye into the village over against *you*; in the which, at your entering, ye shall find a colt tied, whereon yet never man sat: loose him, and bring *him hither*.
19:31 And if any man ask you, Why do ye loose *him*? thus shall ye say unto him, Because {o}the Lord hath need of him.
19:32 And they that were sent went their way, and found even as he had said unto them.

19:28 3 | The whole route from Jericho to Jerusalem is a continual ascent. 19:12 † | The parable is probably an allusion to the well-known story of Herod, or his son Archelaus. Herod went to Rome to obtain the investiture of the kingdom of Judea from Antony. Yea, everything we have ought to be at Jesus' call; and it is better to give to pious uses while we live than defer it to be given after our death.—His free grace is sufficient both to seek and save us, be our guilt or malady what it will. And abundant are the gifts and privileges which Jesus hath given to the ministers and members of his gospel church. His exaltation to glory issues in blessings to men. But all that is received must be improved and accounted for. Happy are they who, with deep humility and dependence on Christ, improve it well; and infinitely glorious, as well as free, their eternal reward. But fearful is their punishment who, through hard thoughts of God, abandon themselves to sinful inactivity. Nowhere is sloth more criminal and ruinous than in the church of God. If men will not improve God's gifts, they must be deprived of them. And if Jews and others dare to reject an ascended Redeemer, they must be destroyed in the most public and fearful manner.
