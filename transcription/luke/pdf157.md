<!-- illustration: DISTANT VIEW OF THE MOUNT OF BEATITUDES. [Luke, vi:20.]—We have in this picture a different view of the Mount of Beatitudes from the one we gave in connection with the Sermon on the Mount, as reported by Matthew, v, vi, vii. This is an interesting height, not simply because it is supposed that Christ there uttered his memorable sermon, but also because it is thought by some to be the mount to which He retired after the resurrection. If Jesus Christ was not the son of God, is it not an unaccountable thing that He should have, nearly 2,000 years ago, uttered the words which, by general consent, are looked upon as the sublimest that have ever fallen from the lips of man? It is inconceivable that a Galilean peasant, with no help from a divine source, could have expressed himself in such a discourse. -->
