## Psalm XIII.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

Here we have the Psalmist sowing in tears, but reaping in joy. Observe (1) His extreme distress, occasioned by God's apparent unkindness, and by inward anguish of soul, and insolence of enemies, ver. 1, 2. (2) His fervent supplications that God would consider his case, strengthen his faith, direct his goings, and comfort his heart, ver. 3, 4. (3) Sudden deliverance, flowing from a fixed trust in God, and inducing to a triumphant joy in him, ver. 5, 6.

¶ Think, my soul, how divine withdrawments, sensibly perceived, sting a renewed heart! How changeable the saints' spiritual frames are while they continue in this world. Weeping endures for a night, but joy cometh in the morning. And, behold, how quickly the prayers of faith are answered, and its expectations fulfilled.

H<span class="sc">OW</span> long wilt thou forget me, Lord?
&emsp;shall it for ever be?
O how long shall it be that thou
&emsp;wilt hide thy face from me?
2 How long take counsel in my soul,
&emsp;still sad in heart, shall I?
How long exalted over me
&emsp;shall be mine enemy?

3 O Lord my God, consider well,
&emsp;and answer to me make:
Mine eyes enlighten, lest the sleep
&emsp;of death me overtake:
4 Lest that mine enemy should say,
&emsp;against him I prevail'd;
And those that trouble me rejoice,
&emsp;when I am mov'd and fail'd.

5 But I have all my confidence
&emsp;thy mercy set upon;
My heart within me shall rejoice
&emsp;in thy salvation.
6 I will unto the Lord my God
&emsp;sing praises cheerfully,
Because he hath his bounty shown
&emsp;to me abundantly.

## Psalm XIV.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm represents the fearful sinfulness and misery of mankind, and the marvellous method of deliverance therefrom. Behold here, (1) All mankind fearfully infected with the God-dishonouring, the soul-debasing and defiling, the world-disturbing malady of sinful tendencies in the heart and life, ver. 1-3. (2) Warm expostulations for the conviction, awakening, and reclaiming of wicked men, ver. 4-6. (3) Consolations exhibited to the righteous, from God's being among them, and being a refuge to them, and from his sending his Son to save and redeem them, ver. 5-7.

¶ While I sing these lines, let me have a deep sense of my corruptions. Let me be ashamed of them before God. Let me, with a broken heart and weeping eye, confess my filthiness and crimes. Let me groan after and flee to Jesus, the Saviour, the salvation of God. O wretched man that I am! who shall deliver me from this abominable life: from this inward body of death!—Thanks to the Redeemer, who is come to Zion to turn away ungodliness from Jacob! Let therefore Jacob rejoice, and the Gentiles sing.

T<span class="sc">HAT</span> there is not a God, the fool
&emsp;doth in his heart conclude:
They are corrupt, their works are vile;
&emsp;not one of them doth good.
2 Upon men's sons the Lord from heav'n
&emsp;did cast his eyes abroad,
To see if any understood,
&emsp;and did seek after God.

3 They altogether filthy are,
&emsp;they all aside are gone;
And there is none that doeth good,
&emsp;yea, sure there is not one.
4 These workers of iniquity
&emsp;do they not know at all,
That they my people eat as bread,
&emsp;and on God do not call?

5 There fear'd they much; for God is with
&emsp;the whole race of the just.
6 You shame the counsel of the poor,
&emsp;because God is his trust.
7 Let Isr'el's help from Sion come:
&emsp;when back the Lord shall bring
His captives, Jacob shall rejoice,
&emsp;and Israel shall sing.

## Psalm XV.

*A Psalm of David.*

Here let me (1) With wonder, observe the happy state of present fellowship with God, in his church on earth, and of everlasting fellowship with him in the temple above; and how fixed the saints are in this their blessed state, ver. 1, 5. (2) Let me, with self-examination, remark the discriminating characters of such as are admitted to that happy state. They are sincere, upright, and honest in all their ways; are kind to neighbours; they esteem others in proportion to the appearance of Christ's image on them; and they prefer a good conscience to every earthly advantage, ver. 2-5.

¶ While I sing, let me think of my perfect, and all-perfecting Saviour, who is passed into the heavens—is entered for me as the forerunner. And having received him into my heart as my sanctifying furniture, let me walk in him as my way, and follow him as my pattern, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, and living soberly, righteously, and godly, in this present world. Thus shall the Father and the Son come unto me on earth, and take up their abode with me: and so shall I in heaven be for ever with the Lord.

W<span class="sc">ITHIN</span> thy tabernacle, Lord,
&emsp;who shall abide with thee?
And in thy high and holy hill
&emsp;who shall a dweller be?
2 The man that walketh uprightly,
&emsp;and worketh righteousness,
And as he thinketh in his heart,
&emsp;so doth he truth express.

3 Who doth not slander with his tongue,
&emsp;nor to his friend doth hurt;
Nor yet against his neighbour doth
&emsp;take up an ill report.
4 In whose eyes vile men are despis'd;
&emsp;but those that God do fear
He honoureth; and changeth not,
&emsp;though to his hurt he swear.

5 His coin puts not to usury,
&emsp;nor take reward will he
Against the guiltless. Who doth thus
&emsp;shall never moved be.

## Psalm XVI.

*Michtam of David.*

This Psalm is indeed a Michtam, a golden Psalm. Behold the man according to God's heart (1) Committing himself to God as his Preserver, ver. 1. (2) Avowing his endearing love to the people, and strict adherence to the worship of God, ver. 3, 4. (3) With great confidence and joy claiming God for his satisfying portion, ver. 2, 5-7. (4) Comforting himself in, and blessing God for, his present intimacy with him, and granted direction to him, and for his certain prospect of the eternal enjoyment of him, ver. 8-11.

¶ But chiefly, Behold here Jesus, the man of God's right hand, surrendering himself up to his Father's service, in room of, and for the everlasting advantage of the world! Behold him taking out his new-covenant claim to God, as his God and portion for ever, and as our God and portion in him! Behold how, supported of God in his holy manhood, he suffers unto a tremendous death and debased burial; but being raised again, he is crowned with everlasting glory and honour! Behold how the agonies of suffering, and the pains of death are succeeded with fulness of joy, and rivers of life and pleasures at God's right hand for evermore. Looking to him, let me with patience run the race that is set before me, living on, and rejoicing in, God as my <span class="sc">all</span> and <span class="sc">in all</span>; and looking for the blessed hope and glorious appearing of the great God my Saviour. Through him, neither sin, nor devils, nor death, nor hell shall be able to prevent my immediate and everlasting enjoyment of <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>, as my infinite portion and <span class="sc">exceeding joy</span>.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, keep me; for I trust in thee.
2 To God thus was my speech,
&emsp;Thou art my Lord; and unto thee
&emsp;my goodness doth not reach:
3 To saints on earth, to th' excellent,
&emsp;where my delight's all plac'd.
4 Their sorrows shall be multiply'd
&emsp;to other gods that haste:

Of their drink-offerings of blood
&emsp;I will no off'ring make;
Yea, neither I their very names
&emsp;up in my lips will take.
5 God is of mine inheritance
&emsp;and cup the portion;
The lot that fallen is to me
&emsp;thou dost maintain alone.

6 Unto me happily the lines
&emsp;in pleasant places fell;
Yea, the inheritance I got
&emsp;in beauty doth excel.
7 I bless the Lord, because he doth
&emsp;by counsel me conduct;
And in the seasons of the night
&emsp;my reins do me instruct.

8 Before me still the Lord I set:
&emsp;sith it is so that he
Doth ever stand at my right hand,
&emsp;I shall not moved be.
9 Because of this my heart is glad,
&emsp;and joy shall be exprest
Ev'n by my glory; and my flesh
&emsp;in confidence shall rest.

10 Because my soul in grave to dwell
&emsp;shall not be left by thee;
Nor wilt thou give thine Holy One
&emsp;corruption to see.
11 Thou wilt me show the path of life:
&emsp;of joys there is full store
Before thy face; at thy right hand
&emsp;are pleasures evermore.

## Psalm XVII.

*A Prayer of David.*

Here, in the depth of distress, (1) David appeals to God concerning the integrity of his heart, and the justness of his cause, ver. 1-4. (2) He prays for establishment in grace within; for tokens of divine favour from above; and for preservation from his carnal, malicious, and insolent, his restless and powerful enemies around, ver. 5-14. (3) He triumphs in the assured faith of his everlasting enjoyment of, and conformity to God, ver. 15.

¶ Let me also here look after Jesus, in whose heart was no deceit, and in whose mouth was no guile:—Jesus, who fulfilled all righteousness for me; and who, notwithstanding all the rage of devils and men, hath sat down at the right hand of God, crowned with glory and honour. Let me remember, that he, as the forerunner, is for me entered into the mansions of bliss; and hath left me an example that I should follow his steps.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, hear the right, attend my cry,
&emsp;unto my pray'r give heed,
That doth not in hypocrisy
&emsp;from feigued lips proceed.
2 And from before thy presence forth
&emsp;my sentence do thou send:
Toward these things that equal are
&emsp;do thou thine eyes intend.

3 Thou prov'dst mine heart, thou visit'dst me
&emsp;by night, thou didst me try,
Yet nothing found'st; for that my mouth
&emsp;shall not sin, purpos'd I.
4 As for men's works, I, by the word
&emsp;that from thy lips doth flow,
Did me preserve out of the paths
&emsp;wherein destroyers go.

5 Hold up my goings, Lord, me guide
&emsp;in those thy paths divine,
So that my footsteps may not slide
&emsp;out of those ways of thine.
6 I called have on thee, O God,
&emsp;because thou wilt me hear:
That thou may'st hearken to my speech,
&emsp;to me incline thine ear.

7 Thy wondrous loving-kindness show,
&emsp;thou that, by thy right hand,
Sav'st them that trust in thee from those
&emsp;that up against them stand.
8 As th' apple of the eye me keep;
&emsp;in thy wings shade me close
9 From lewd oppressors, compassing
&emsp;me round, as deadly foes.

10 In their own fat they are inclos'd;
&emsp;their mouth speaks loftily.
11 Our steps they compass'd; and to ground
&emsp;down bowing set their eye.
12 He like unto a lion is
&emsp;that's greedy of his prey,
Or lion young, which lurking doth
&emsp;in Secret places stay.
