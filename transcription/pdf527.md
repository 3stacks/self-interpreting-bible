## Psalm CXLI.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was likewise composed under distress and persecution. In it David prays (1) For God's kind acceptance of his prayers, ver. 1, 2. (2) For his powerful assistance in keeping his tongue, his heart, and his hand in the way of duty, ver 3, 4. (3) That others might be helpful to him with their seasonable reproofs, and he to them with his prayers, ver. 5, 6. (4) That when he and his friends were brought to the last extremity, God would graciously appear for their relief, ver. 7-10. ¶ Whenever I am afflicted, let me pray. Let me highly prize and kindly receive Christian reproof, and earnestly improve it to my spiritual advantage. Nor let me ever despair on account of the great distress of my soul or of the church. Is anything too hard for the Lord?

O <span class="sc">Lord</span>, I unto thee do cry,
&emsp;do thou make haste to me,
&emsp;And give an ear unto my voice,
&emsp;when I cry unto thee.
2 As incense let my prayer be
&emsp;directed in thine eyes;
&emsp;And the uplifting of my hands
&emsp;as th' ev'ning sacrifice.

3 Set, Lord, a watch before my mouth,
&emsp;keep of my lips the door.
4 My heart incline thou not unto
&emsp;the ills I should abhor,
&emsp;To practise wicked works with men
&emsp;that work iniquity;
&emsp;And with their delicates my taste
&emsp;let me not satisfy.

5 Let him that righteous is me smite,
&emsp;it shall a kindness be;
&emsp;Let him reprove, I shall it count
&emsp;a precious oil to me:
&emsp;Such smiting shall not break my head;
&emsp;for yet the time shall fall,
&emsp;When I in their calamities
&emsp;to God pray for them shall.

6 When as their judges down shall be
&emsp;in stony places cast,
&emsp;Then shall they hear my words; for they
&emsp;shall sweet be to their taste.
7 About the grave's devouring mouth
&emsp;our bones are scatter'd round,
&emsp;As wood which men do cut and cleave
&emsp;lies scatter'd on the ground.

8 But unto thee, O God the Lord,
&emsp;mine eyes uplifted be:
&emsp;My soul do not leave destitute;
&emsp;my trust is set on thee.
9 Lord, keep me safely from the snares
&emsp;which they for me prepare;
&emsp;And from the subtile gins of them
&emsp;that wicked workers are.

10 Let workers of iniquity
&emsp;into their own nets fall,
&emsp;Whilst I do, by thine help, escape
&emsp;the danger of them all.

## Psalm CXLII.

*Maschil of David; A Prayer when he was in the Cave.*

This Psalm was framed by David when obliged, by Saul's persecution, to hide himself in a cave, 1 Sa. xxii. 1, or xxiv. 3, and contains (1) Bitter complaints of the subtlety, strength, and malice of his enemies, and of the coldness and indifference of his friends, ver. 1-4. 6. (2) The comfort he took in God's knowing his way, and in recognizing his claim to God as his portion, ver. 3-5. (3) His pleasant expectations that God would deliver him, and that his fellow saints would join with him in thanksgiving for his deliverance, ver. 6, 7. ¶ If all men forsake me, if all men harass me, let me cast my burden on the Lord, that he may sustain me. Let me renew my believing claims to him as my Lord, my God, my Refuge, and my Portion. Let me rest firmly on his characters and promises for his sympathy, help, and deliverance.

I <span class="sc">With</span> my voice cry'd to the Lord,
&emsp;with it made my request:
2 Pour'd out to him my plaint, to him
&emsp;my trouble I exprest.
3 When in me was o'erwhelm'd my sp'rit,
&emsp;then well thou knew'st my way;
&emsp;Where I did walk a snare for me
&emsp;they privily did lay.

4 I look'd on my right hand, and view'd,
&emsp;but none to know me were;
&emsp;All refuge failed me, no man
&emsp;did for my soul take care.
5 I cry'd to thee; I said, Thou art
&emsp;my refuge, Lord, alone;
&emsp;And in the land of those that live
&emsp;thou art my portion.

6 Because I am brought very low,
&emsp;attend unto my cry:
&emsp;Me from my persecutors save,
&emsp;who stronger are than I.
7 From prison bring my soul, that I
&emsp;thy name may glorify:
&emsp;The just shall compass me, when thou
&emsp;with me deal'st bounteously.

## Psalm CXLIII.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was probably penned by David during the unnatural rebellion of his son Absalom, as Ps. iii. xlii. xliii. It contains (1) Sad complaints of trouble, and of the sinking of his spirit under it, ver. 3-5, 7. (2) Fervent supplications that God would hear his prayer, ver. 1, 7; forgive his sins, ver. 2; manifest his favours, ver. 6-8; direct him in the way of duty, ver. 8-10; quicken him in it, ver. 11; deliver him out of trouble, ver. 9, 11; and, in fine, punish his persecutors, ver. 12. ¶ What time my heart is overwhelmed, let God lead me to the Rock that is higher than I; and let me study to have my sin removed first, in order that my troubles may remove in due order, and with a rich blessing.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, hear my pray'r, attend my suits;
&emsp;and in thy faithfulness
&emsp;Give thou an answer unto me,
&emsp;and in thy righteousness.
2 Thy servant also bring thou not
&emsp;in judgment to be try'd:
&emsp;Because no living man can be
&emsp;in thy sight justify'd.

3 For th' en'my hath pursu'd my soul,
&emsp;my life to ground down tread:
&emsp;In darkness he hath made me dwell,
&emsp;as who have long been dead.
4 My sp'rit is therefore overwhelm'd
&emsp;in me perplexedly;
&emsp;Within me is my very heart
&emsp;amazed wondrously.

5 I call to mind the days of old,
&emsp;to meditate I use
&emsp;On all thy works; upon the deeds
&emsp;I of thy hands do muse.
6 My hands to thee I stretch; my soul
&emsp;thirsts, as dry land, for thee.
7 Haste, Lord, to hear, my spirit fails:
&emsp;hide not thy face from me;
&emsp;Lest like to them I do become
&emsp;that go down to the dust.
8 At morn let me thy kindness hear;
&emsp;for in thee do I trust.
&emsp;Teach me the way that I should walk:
&emsp;I lift my soul to thee.
9 Lord, free me from my foes; I flee
&emsp;to thee to cover me.

10 Because thou art my God, to do
&emsp;thy will do me instruct:
&emsp;Thy Sp'rit is good, me to the land
&emsp;of uprightness conduct.
11 Revive and quicken me, O Lord,
&emsp;ev'n for thine own name's sake:
&emsp;And do thou, for thy righteousness,
&emsp;my soul from trouble take.

12 And of thy mercy slay my foes;
&emsp;let all destroyed be
&emsp;That do afflict my soul: for I
&emsp;a servant am to thee.

*Another of the same.*

O<span class="sc">H</span>, hear my prayer, Lord,
&emsp;And unto my desire
&emsp;To bow thine ear accord,
&emsp;I humbly thee require;
&emsp;And, in thy faithfulness,
&emsp;Unto me answer make,
&emsp;And, in thy righteousness,
&emsp;Upon me pity take.

2 In judgment enter not
&emsp;With me thy servant poor;
&emsp;For why, this well I wot,
&emsp;No sinner can endure
&emsp;The sight of thee, O God:
&emsp;If thou his deeds shalt try,
&emsp;He dare make none abode
&emsp;Himself to justify.

3 Behold, the cruel foe
&emsp;Me persecutes with spite,
&emsp;My soul to overthrow:
&emsp;Yea, he my life down quite
&emsp;Unto the ground hath smote,
&emsp;And made me dwell full low
&emsp;In darkness, as forgot,
&emsp;Or men dead long ago.

4 Therefore my sp'rit much vex'd,
&emsp;O'erwhelm'd is me within;
&emsp;My heart right sore perplex'd
&emsp;And desolate hath been.
5 Yet I do call to mind
&emsp;What ancient days record,
&emsp;Thy works of ev'ry kind
&emsp;I think upon, O Lord.

6 Lo, I do stretch my hands
&emsp;To thee, my help alone;
&emsp;For thou well understands
&emsp;All my complaint and moan:
&emsp;My thirsting soul desires,
&emsp;And longeth after thee,
&emsp;As thirsty ground requires
&emsp;With rain refresh'd to be.

7 Lord, let my pray'r prevail,
&emsp;To answer it make speed;
&emsp;For, lo, my sp'rit doth fail:
&emsp;Hide not thy face in need:
&emsp;Lest I be like to those
&emsp;That do in darkness sit,
&emsp;Or him that downward goes
&emsp;Into the dreadful pit.

8 Because I trust in thee,
&emsp;O Lord, cause me to hear
&emsp;Thy loving-kindness free,
&emsp;When morning doth appear:
&emsp;Cause me to know the way
&emsp;Wherein my path should be:
&emsp;For why, my soul on high
&emsp;I do lift up to thee.

9 From my fierce enemy
&emsp;In safety do me guide,
&emsp;Because I flee to thee,
&emsp;Lord, that thou may'st me hide.
10 My God alone art thou,
&emsp;Teach me thy righteousness:
&emsp;Thy Sp'rit's good, lead me to
&emsp;The land of uprightness.

11 O Lord, for thy name's sake,
&emsp;Be pleas'd to quicken me;
&emsp;And, for thy truth, forth take
&emsp;My soul from misery.
12 And of thy grace destroy
&emsp;My foes, and put to shame
&emsp;All who my soul annoy;
&emsp;For I thy servant am.
