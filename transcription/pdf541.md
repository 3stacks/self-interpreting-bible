## Paraphrase LX.

*Heb. xiii. 20, 21.*

In these words we have the object of prayer in his new covenant character, and also the matter of prayer. What a mercy that a reconciled God of grace, and a risen Saviour and chief Shepherd, have, by the new covenant, secured to us gracious influences, to make us completely holy. To him let us ascribe all the honour, and always pray and never faint; in everything by prayer and supplication let our requests be made known to God.

&emsp;we nearer still may rise,

&emsp;be pleasing in thine eyes.

## Paraphrase LXI.

*1 Peter i. 3-5.*

The verses paraphrased lead us to exclaim, according to what unbounded mercy, and by what infinite power, we are endowed with new principles of grace, to the praise and honour of God! And amidst all trials may God's people triumph in the assured views and hopes founded on a risen Saviour, of their bright glories and complete salvation at the final appearing of Jesus Christ. O what power must be exerted to keep the best safe to the heavenly kingdom. Let my treasure be in heaven, and my heart there, set on things above, and not on things of the earth.

B<span class="sc">LESS'D</span> be the everlasting God,
&emsp;the Father of our Lord;
Be his abounding mercy prais'd,
&emsp;his majesty ador'd.
2 When from the dead he rais'd his Son,
&emsp;and call'd him to the sky,
He gave our souls a lively hope
&emsp;that they should never die.

3 To an inheritance divine
&emsp;he taught our hearts to rise;
'Tis uncorrupted, undefil'd,
&emsp;unfading in the skies.
4 Saints by the pow'r of God are kept
&emsp;till the salvation come:
We walk by faith as strangers here;
&emsp;but Christ shall call us home.

## Paraphrase LXII.

*2 Peter iii. 3-14.*

The words of Peter, as inspired by God, here lead us to say, How precisely the Scripture foretells the conduct of its adversaries, our own Deists not excepted, and thereby renders their wicked opposition a proof of its own divine inspiration! In what tremendous manner will Jesus come at last! What perishing of heavens, melting of elements, and burning of the earth, will attend it! How terrible for carnal men, to have that world they have chosen for their portion, universally kindled into flames to give them a final adieu, and fearful introduction into hell! What a blessed change will ensue! new heavens and a new earth wherein dwelleth righteousness! In the contemplation and faith of these glorious events, how circumspect, holy, and religious we ought to be!

L<span class="sc">O</span>! in the last of days behold
&emsp;a faithless race arise;
Their lawless lust their only rule;
&emsp;and thus the scoffer cries;
2 Where is the promise, deem'd so true,
&emsp;that spoke the Saviour near?
E'er since our fathers slept in dust,
&emsp;no change has reach'd our ear.

3 Years roll'd on years successive glide,
&emsp;since first the world began,
And on the tide of time still floats,
&emsp;secure, the bark of man.
4 Thus speaks the scoffer; but his words
&emsp;conceal the truth he knows,
That from the waters' dark abyss
&emsp;the earth at first arose.

5 But when the sons of men began
&emsp;with one consent to stray,
At Heav'n's command a deluge swept
&emsp;the godless race away.
6 A diff'rent fate is now prepar'd
&emsp;for Nature's trembling frame;
Soon shall her orbs be all enwrapt
&emsp;in one devouring flame.

7 Reserv'd are sinners for the hour
&emsp;when to the gulf below,
Arm'd with the hand of sov'reign pow'r,
&emsp;the Judge consigns his foe.
8 Though now, ye just! the time appears
&emsp;protracted, dark, unknown,

&emsp;to heav'n's great Lord are one.

9 Still all may share his sov'reign grace,
&emsp;in ev'ry change secure;
The meek, the suppliant contrite race,
&emsp;shall find his mercy sure.

&emsp;forbids the suppliant's fall;

&emsp;and watch o'er ev'ry deed;
14 Expecting calm th' appointed hour,
&emsp;when, Nature's conflict o'er,
A new and better world shall rise,
&emsp;where sin is known no more.

## Paraphrase LXIII.

*1 John iii. 1-4.*

In singing these lines let me reflect how astonishing the love of God in making sinful men, children of Satan, his children by repentance and faith, notwithstanding of their mean, contemptible, and unknown appearance on earth. How great shall be their glory and happiness in the last judgment and in the eternal state! How powerfully evangelic, well-founded hopes of everlasting happiness influence to the study of holiness in heart and life! While I admire grace-bringing salvation, let me live soberly, righteously, in this present world.

B<span class="sc">EHOLD</span> th' amazing gift of love
&emsp;the Father hath bestow'd
On us, the sinful sons of men,
&emsp;to call us sons of God!
2 Conceal'd as yet this honour lies,
&emsp;by this dark world unknown,
A world that knew not when he came,
&emsp;ev'n God's eternal Son.

3 High is the rank we now possess;
&emsp;but higher we shall rise;
Though what we shall hereafter be
&emsp;is hid from mortal eyes:
4 Our souls, we know, when he appears,
&emsp;shall bear his image bright;
For all his glory, full disclos'd,
&emsp;shall open to our sight.

5 A hope so great, and so divine,
&emsp;may trials well endure;
And purge the soul from sense and sin,
&emsp;as Christ himself is pure.

## Paraphrase LXIV.

*Rev. i. 5-9.*

The words paraphrased are an anthem of praise to our divine Redeemer. O how glorious and honoured these songsters, when by his love washed from the guilt, dominion, and filth of their sin in Jesus' blood, and made kings unto God and his Father! How terrible the condition, and dreadful the sentence and fate of Jesus' enemies, when he appears in his majesty and glory at the last judgment! Let me think what shall be my lot at that great and terrible day. If I have lived without preparation for it, let me, without a moment's farther delay, receive and rest on the Saviour for salvation. Now is the accepted time, and now is the day of salvation!

T<span class="sc">O</span> him that lov'd the souls of men,
&emsp;and wash'd us in his blood,
To royal honours rais'd our head,
&emsp;and made us priests to God;
2 To him let ev'ry tongue be praise,
&emsp;and ev'ry heart be love!
All grateful honours paid on earth,
&emsp;and nobler songs above!

3 Behold, on flying clouds he comes!
&emsp;his saints shall bless the day;
While they that pierc'd him sadly mourn
&emsp;in anguish and dismay.
4 I am the First, and I the Last;
&emsp;time centres all in me;
Th' Almighty God, who was, and is,
&emsp;and evermore shall be.

## Paraphrase LXV.

*Rev. v. 6, to the end.*

In this paraphrase we have a glorious and pleasant representation of the dignity of the Saviour, and of the employments and dignity of the heavenly state. May not I reflect how transcendently wonderful and glorious the character of Jesus! at once infinitely majestic, and yet endearingly meek, and marked with an atoning death for our sins? How extensive his power and authority, his wisdom and knowledge! How unbounded his fulness of the Holy Ghost, and his power of sending him to men! How delightfully ministers and saints, and even angels, triumph in, and praise him for his redemption work equally as his Father! God forbid my soul should be silent, when so many praise him!

B<span class="sc">EHOLD</span> the glories of the Lamb
&emsp;amidst his Father's throne;
Prepare new honours for his name,
&emsp;and songs before unknown.
2 Lo! elders worship at his feet;
&emsp;the church adores around,
With vials full of odours rich,
&emsp;and harps of sweetest sound.
