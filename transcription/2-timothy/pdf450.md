## Concluding Remarks on the Second Epistle to Timothy.

This, though not the last in order of position, is probably the last the apostle ever wrote. About this time Nero, an emperor proverbially cruel, had set fire to Rome, and, in order to divert the public odium from himself, charged the crime against the Christians, and had them condemned to death in great numbers; some were crucified; others being covered with combustibles and burned as night-lights in his gardens; others covered with skins of wild beasts and exposed to dogs; others cast to the lions (Tacitus, *Annales*, lib. xv. cap. 44). Under these circumstances, and in prospect of some such death, the apostle wrote. And certainly it thus serves in an especial manner 'to show with what composure a Christian can die.' *C.*
