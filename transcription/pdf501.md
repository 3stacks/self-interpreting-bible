## Psalm LXVI.

*To the chief Musician, A Song or Psalm.*

In this Psalm of thanksgiving, in which the fate of Israel is to be considered as typical of that of the gospel church and people of God, we have (1) David, as the messenger of God, calling all the nations of mankind to praise God for the manifestation of his sovereign dominion in his works, so wonderful in themselves, terrible to his enemies, comfortable to his people, and commanding and awful to all men, ver. 1-7; and for trying his people with afflictions, for supporting under them, protecting amidst them, and delivering from them at last, ver. 8-12. (2) David, as the servant of God, exemplifying his own exhortations in honouring God by costly oblations, and by thankful declarations of what God had done for his soul, particularly in answer to his prayers, ver. 13-20.

A<span class="sc">LL</span> lands to God, in joyful sounds,
&emsp;aloft your voices raise.

2 Sing forth the honour of his name,
&emsp;and glorious make his praise.

3 Say unto God, How terrible
&emsp;in all thy works art thou!
Through thy great pow'r thy foes to thee
&emsp;shall be constrain'd to bow.

4 All on the earth shall worship thee,
&emsp;they shall thy praise proclaim
In songs: they shall sing cheerfully
&emsp;unto thy holy name.

5 Come, and the works that God hath wrought
&emsp;with admiration see:
In's working to the sons of men
&emsp;most terrible is he.

6 Into dry land the sea he turn'd,
&emsp;and they a passage had;
Ev'n marching through the flood on foot,
&emsp;there we in him were glad.

7 He ruleth ever by his pow'r;
&emsp;his eyes the nations see:
O let not the rebellious ones
&emsp;lift up themselves on high.

8 Ye people, bless our God; aloud
&emsp;the voice speak of his praise:

9 Our soul in life who safe preserves,
&emsp;our foot from sliding stays.

10 For thou didst prove and try us, Lord,
&emsp;as men do silver try;

11 Brought'st us into the net, and mad'st
&emsp;bands on our loins to lie.

12 Thou hast caus'd men ride o'er our heads;
&emsp;and though that we did pass
Through fire and water, yet thou brought'st
&emsp;us to a wealthy place.

13 I'll bring burnt-off'rings to thy house;
&emsp;to thee my vows I'll pay,

14 Which my lips utter'd, my mouth spake,
&emsp;when trouble on me lay.

15 Burnt-sacrifices of fat rams
&emsp;with incense I will bring;
Of bullocks and of goats I will
&emsp;present an offering.

16 All that fear God, come, hear, I'll tell
&emsp;what he did for my soul.

17 I with my mouth unto him cry'd,
&emsp;my tongue did him extol.

18 If in my heart I sin regard,
&emsp;the Lord me will not hear:

19 But surely God me heard, and to
&emsp;my prayer's voice gave ear.

20 O let the Lord, our gracious God,
&emsp;for ever blessed be,
Who turned not my pray'r from him,
&emsp;nor yet his grace from me.

## Psalm LXVII.

*To the chief Musician on Neginoth, A Psalm or Song.*

Here we have (1) David's prayer for the church of Israel, ver. 1. (2) His supplication for the spread of the gospel among the Gentiles, as the ground of great consolation and praise, ver. 2-5. (3) His believing prospect of the divine blessings and true piety which should attend the same, ver. 6, 7.

¶ While I sing, let me request the salvation of Israel. Let me supplicate the gathering of the nations to <span class="sc">Shiloh</span>. Let me praise the Lord that already this great work is begun, and that the Lord, who is mighty, shall finish it in his time.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, bless and pity us,
&emsp;shine on us with thy face:

2 That th' earth thy way, and nations all
&emsp;may know thy saving grace.

3 Let people praise thee, Lord;
&emsp;let people all thee praise.

4 O let the nations be glad,
&emsp;in songs their voices raise:
Thou'lt justly people judge,
&emsp;on earth rule nations all.

5 Let people praise thee, Lord; let them
&emsp;praise thee, both great and small.

6 The earth her fruit shall yield,
&emsp;our God shall blessing send.

7 God shall us bless; men shall him fear
&emsp;unto earth's utmost end.

*Another of the same.*

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, unto us be merciful,
&emsp;do thou us also bless;
And graciously cause shine on us
&emsp;the brightness of thy face:

2 That so thy way upon the earth
&emsp;to all men may be known;
Also among the nations all
&emsp;thy saving health be shown.

3 O let the people praise thee, Lord;
&emsp;let people all thee praise.

4 O let the nations be glad,
&emsp;and sing for joy always:
For rightly thou shalt people judge,
&emsp;and nations rule on earth.

5 Let people praise thee, Lord; let all
&emsp;the folk praise thee with mirth.

6 Then shall the earth yield her increase;
&emsp;God, our God, bless us shall.

7 God shall us bless; and of the earth
&emsp;the ends shall fear him all.

## Psalm LXVIII.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm or Song of David.*

Perhaps this Psalm was composed on the same occasion as the 24th and 47th, when David brought up the ark of God to that tabernacle which he had pitched for it in Zion, 2 Sa. vi. We have it in (1) Fervent supplications that God would scatter and defeat the projects of his enemies, ver. 1, 2. (2) High praises to him for his infinite greatness and grace; his righteous relieving of the afflicted and oppressed; his directing of Israel in the Arabian desert; his manifesting of his glory at Sinai; his comfortable providing for his people in the wilderness and in Canaan, ver. 3-10; for the easy conquest of their Canaanitish enemies; his fixing his temple on Mount Zion; for the ascension of Christ to glory, to receive gifts for men; and for the spread of the gospel among Jews and Gentiles by means of the apostles; while the obstinate Jews are severely punished, ver. 11-32; and, in fine, for his supreme dominion, his awful majesty, his mighty power, and for the glory of his sanctuary, and the grace he bestows on his people, ver. 33-35.

¶ While I sing, let me behold, let me admire what God is, and hath done for and to my soul, and to the church of God of which I am a member. Let me behold what God hath done, in instances unnumbered, in prosecution of the promise he made by his Eternal Son.

L<span class="sc">ET</span> God arise, and scattered
&emsp;let all his en'mies be;
And let all those that do him hate
&emsp;before his presence flee.

2 As smoke is driv'n, so drive thou them;
&emsp;as fire melts wax away,
Before God's face let wicked men
&emsp;so perish and decay.

3 But let the righteous be glad:
&emsp;let them before God's sight
Be very joyful; yea, let them
&emsp;rejoice with all their might.

4 To God sing, to his name sing praise:
&emsp;extol him with your voice,
That rides on heav'n, by his name <span class="sc">Jah</span>,
&emsp;before his face rejoice.

5 Because the Lord a father is
&emsp;unto the fatherless;
God is the widow's judge, within
&emsp;his place of holiness.

6 God doth the solitary set
&emsp;in fam'lies: and from bands
The chain'd doth free; but rebels do
&emsp;inhabit parched lands.

7 O God, what time thou didst go forth
&emsp;before thy people's face;
And when through the great wilderness
&emsp;thy glorious marching was;

8 Then at God's presence shook the earth,
&emsp;then drops from heaven fell;
This Sinai shook before the Lord,
&emsp;the God of Israel.

9 O God, thou to thine heritage
&emsp;didst send a plenteous rain,
Whereby thou, when it weary was,
&emsp;didst it refresh again.

10 Thy congregation then did make
&emsp;their habitation there:
Of thine own goodness for the poor,
&emsp;O God, thou didst prepare.

11 The Lord himself did give the word,
&emsp;the word abroad did spread;
Great was the company of them
&emsp;the same who published.

12 Kings of great armies foiled were,
&emsp;and forc'd to flee away;
And women, who remain'd at home,
&emsp;did distribute the prey.

13 Though ye have lien among the pots,
&emsp;like doves ye shall appear,
Whose wings with silver, and with gold
&emsp;whose feathers cover'd are.

14 When there th' Almighty scatter'd kings,
&emsp;like Salmon's snow 'twas white.

15 God's hill is like to Bashan hill,
&emsp;like Bashan hill for height.

16 Why do ye leap, ye mountains high?
&emsp;this is the hill where God
Desires to dwell; yea, God in it
&emsp;for aye will make abode.

17 God's chariots twenty thousand are,
&emsp;thousands of angels strong;
In's holy place God is, as in
&emsp;mount Sinai, them among.

18 Thou hast, O Lord, most glorious,
&emsp;ascended up on high;
And in triumph victorious led
&emsp;captive captivity:
Thou hast received gifts for men,
&emsp;for such as did rebel;
Yea, ev'n for them, that God the Lord
&emsp;in midst of them might dwell.

19 Bless'd be the Lord, who is to us
&emsp;of our salvation God;
Who daily with his benefits
&emsp;us plenteously doth load.

20 He of salvation is the God,
&emsp;who is our God most strong;
And unto God the Lord from death
&emsp;the issues do belong.

21 But surely God shall wound the head
&emsp;of those that are his foes;
The hairy scalp of him that still
&emsp;on in his trespass goes.

22 God said, My people I will bring
&emsp;again from Bashan hill;
Yea, from the sea's devouring depths
&emsp;them bring again I will;

23 That in the blood of enemies
&emsp;thy foot imbru'd may be,
And of thy dogs dipp'd in the same
&emsp;the tongues thou mayest see.

24 Thy goings they have seen, O God;
&emsp;the steps of majesty
Of my God, and my mighty King,
&emsp;within the sanctuary.
