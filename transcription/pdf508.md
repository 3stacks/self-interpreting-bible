## Psalm LXXXIII.

*A Song or Psalm of Asaph.*

This Psalm relates to some combination of the heathens around against the Hebrews, either in the days of David, 2 Sa. viii. or x.; or of Jehoshaphat, 2 Ch. xx. And contains (1) A solemn remonstrance to God concerning their malicious designs against his church and honour, ver. 1-8. (2) Fervent supplications to God, that he would defeat these attempts; protect and preserve his church; humble his enemies; and glorify himself in the world, ver. 9-18. ¶ While I sing, let me be affected with the inward combinations of my own lusts with Satan and the world, and with the joint endeavours of open enemies and naughty professors against the church and interests of Christ; and commit the cause to God who judgeth righteously.

K<span class="sc">EEP</span> not, O God, we thee entreat,
&emsp;O keep not silence now:
Do thou not hold thy peace, O God,
&emsp;and still no more be thou.
2 For, lo, thine enemies a noise
&emsp;tumultuously have made;
And they that haters are of thee
&emsp;have lifted up the head.

3 Against thy chosen people they
&emsp;do crafty counsel take;
And they against thy hidden ones
&emsp;do consultations make.
4 Come, let us cut them off, said they,
&emsp;from being a nation,
That of the name of Isr'el may
&emsp;no more be mention.
5 For with joint heart they plot, in league
&emsp;against thee they combine.
6 The tents of Edom, Ishm'elites,
&emsp;Moab's, and Hagar's line;
7 Gebal, and Ammon, Amalek,
&emsp;Philistines, those of Tyre;
8 And Assur join'd with them, to help
&emsp;Lot's children they conspire.
9 Do to them as to Midian,
&emsp;Jabin at Kison strand;
10 And Sis'ra, which at En-dor fell,
&emsp;as dung to fat the land.
11 Like Oreb and like Zeeb make
&emsp;their noble men to fall;
Like Zeba and Zalmunna like,
&emsp;make thou their princes all;

12 Who said, For our possession
&emsp;let us God's houses take.
13 My God, them like a wheel, as chaff
&emsp;before the wind, them make.
14 As fire consumes the wood, as flame
&emsp;doth mountains set on fire,
15 Chase and affright them with the storm
&emsp;and tempest of thine ire.
16 Their faces fill with shame, O Lord,
&emsp;that they may seek thy name.
17 Let them confounded be, and vex'd,
&emsp;and perish in their shame:
18 That men may know that thou, to whom
&emsp;alone doth appertain
The name <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>, dost most high
&emsp;o'er all the earth remain.

## Psalm LXXXIV.

*To the chief Musician, upon Gittith, A Psalm for the sons of Korah.*

This Psalm is very similar to the 27th, 42d, 43d, and 68d, and might be composed on the same occasion with the former, when David was banished from Jerusalem by Absalom his son, 2 Sa. xv. xvi. We have here (1) David's ardent affection towards the public ordinances of God, and sense of their happiness who enjoyed them, ver. 1-7, 10. (2) His heart-burning desire to the God of ordinances, ver. 8, 9. (3) His assured faith of God's kindness and persuasion of the happiness of such as trust in him, ver. 11, 12. ¶ So let my heart cry out for God, the living God. So let me covet earnestly intimate fellowship with him in his ordinances. So let me praise his name, and hold on in his way, till I arrive at the Sion above. So let God be my Friend, my Protector, my Supplier, my Store, and the everlasting Rock of my rest.

H<span class="sc">OW</span> lovely is thy dwelling-place,
&emsp;O Lord of hosts, to me!
The tabernacles of thy grace
&emsp;how pleasant, Lord, they be!
2 My thirsty soul longs vel'mently,
&emsp;yea faints, thy courts to see:
My very heart and flesh cry out,
&emsp;O living God, for thee.

3 Behold, the sparrow findeth out
&emsp;an house wherein to rest;
The swallow also for herself
&emsp;hath purchased a nest;
Ev'n thine own altars,<sup>1</sup> where she safe
&emsp;her young ones forth may bring,
O thou almighty Lord of hosts,
&emsp;who art my God and King.
4 Bless'd are they in thy house that dwell,
&emsp;they ever give thee praise.
5 Bless'd is the man whose strength thou art,
&emsp;in whose heart are thy ways:
6 Who passing thorough Baca's vale,
&emsp;therein do dig up wells;
Also the rain that falleth down
&emsp;the pools with water fills.
7 So they from strength unwearied go
&emsp;still forward unto strength,
Until in Sion they appear
&emsp;before the Lord at length.
8 Lord God of hosts, my prayer hear;
&emsp;O Jacob's God, give ear.
9 See God our shield, look on the face
&emsp;of thine Anointed dear.

10 For in thy courts one day excels
&emsp;a thousand; rather in
My God's house will I keep a door,
&emsp;than dwell in tents of sin.
11 For God the Lord's a sun and shield:
&emsp;he'll grace and glory give;
And will withhold no good from them
&emsp;that uprightly do live.
12 O thou that art the Lord of hosts,
&emsp;that man is truly blest,
Who by assured confidence
&emsp;on thee alone doth rest.

## Psalm LXXXV.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm for the sons of Korah.*

This Psalm relates to some remarkable deliverance of the Jewish nation; but whether that effected by the advancement of David to the throne, or that from the Assyrian invasion, 2 Ki. xix. or from the Chaldean captivity, Ezr. i. is uncertain. It contains (1) Thankful acknowledgments of divine favours received, ver. 1-3. (2) Supplications that further favours and deliverances may be speedily bestowed, ver. 4-7. (3) Firm expectations of a gracious answer, in the coming, mediation, and blessings of the Messiah, ver. 8-13. ¶ While I sing, let my soul be affected with the mercies which I, which Britain, which the church of God have enjoyed. Let me be thereby encouraged to plead for more; and let me, without doubting, credit the promises, and expect supply from the fulness of Christ.

O <span class="sc">Lord</span>, thou hast been favourable
&emsp;to thy beloved land:
Jacob's captivity thou hast
&emsp;recall'd with mighty hand.

<sup>1</sup> To me it is inconceivable how sparrows or swallows could fix their nests in the altars of God, which were of brass or rough stones, and had a fire perpetually burning upon them, and multitudes of priests and Levites crowding around them. God did not allow any trees to be planted near them. I cannot believe God's tabernacle or temple was polluted with the nests and ordure of birds in the manner of our ruinous churches. Nor can I see this idea answerable to the context, or scope of this Psalm. Might not the verse be rather translated, *As the sparrow findeth the house, and the swallow the nest for herself, where she hath put her young ones, my soul findeth thine altars, O Lord of hosts, my King and my God,* i. e. with inexpressible ardour I long for, and desire them, and with ineffable pleasure I approach them, in order to enjoy intimate fellowship with my God. Compare ver. 1, 2, 10 of this Psalm with Psalms xlii. 1, 2; xliii. 3, 4.

2 Thou pardoned thy people hast
&emsp;all their iniquities;
Thou all their trespasses and sins
&emsp;hast cover'd from thine eyes.
3 Thou took'st off all thine ire, and turn'dst
&emsp;from thy wrath's furiousness.
4 Turn us, God of our health, and cause
&emsp;thy wrath 'gainst us to cease.
5 Shall thy displeasure thus endure
&emsp;against us without end?
Wilt thou to generations all
&emsp;thine anger forth extend?

6 That in thee may thy people joy,
&emsp;wilt thou not us revive?
7 Show us thy mercy, Lord, to us
&emsp;do thy salvation give.
8 I'll hear what God the Lord will speak:
&emsp;to his folk he'll speak peace,
And to his saints; but let them not
&emsp;return to foolishness.

9 To them that fear him surely near
&emsp;is his salvation;
That glory in our land may have
&emsp;her habitation.
10 Truth met with mercy, righteousness
&emsp;and peace kiss'd mutually:
11 Truth springs from earth, and righteousness
&emsp;looks down from heaven high.

12 Yea, what is good the Lord shall give;
&emsp;our land shall yield increase:
13 Justice, to set us in his steps,
&emsp;shall go before his face.

## Psalm LXXXVI.

*A Prayer of David.*

This Psalm contains (1) David's fervent supplications that God would hear his prayers, ver. 1, 6, 7, mercifully preserve and save him, ver. 2, 3, 16, and afford him joy, strength, and honour, ver. 4, 11, 17. (2) The pleas wherewith he enforces his prayers, drawn from the goodness of God, ver. 5, 13, 15; his own relation to, and trust in God, ver. 2, 4, 16; his former experience of God's kindness, ver. 17; and from the malice of his enemies, ver. 14. (3) His ascription of praise to God, as matchless in his nature and work; as the sole object of worship; and as great and infinitely gracious, ver. 8-10, 12, 13. ¶ While I sing, let me remember that the Psalmist hath left me an example that I should walk in his steps.

O <span class="sc">Lord</span>, do thou bow down thine ear
&emsp;and hear me graciously;
Because I sore afflicted am,
&emsp;and am in poverty.
2 Because I'm holy, let my soul
&emsp;by thee preserved be:
O thou my God, thy servant save,
&emsp;that puts his trust in thee.

3 Sith unto thee I daily cry,
&emsp;be merciful to me.
4 Rejoice thy servant's soul; for, Lord,
&emsp;I lift my soul to thee.
5 For thou art gracious, O Lord,
&emsp;and ready to forgive;
And rich in mercy, all that call
&emsp;upon thee to relieve.

6 Hear, Lord, my pray'r; unto the voice
&emsp;of my request attend:
7 In troublous times I'll call on thee;
&emsp;for thou wilt answer send.
8 Lord, there is none among the gods
&emsp;that may with thee compare;
And like the works which thou hast done,
&emsp;not any work is there.

9 All nations whom thou mad'st shall come
&emsp;and worship rev'rently
Before thy face; and they, O Lord,
&emsp;thy name shall glorify.
10 Because thou art exceeding great,
&emsp;and works by thee are done
Which are to be admir'd, and thou
&emsp;art God thyself alone.
