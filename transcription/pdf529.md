## Psalm CXLVII.

This Psalm was probably penned by David while he repaired and fortified Jerusalem, 2 Sa. v., and contains (1) Solemn calls to praise God, ver. 1, 7, 12, 20. (2) Weighty reasons for praising God, viz. 1. That as the God of nature he is infinitely intelligent and great, and the sovereign Manager of all creatures, ver. 4, 5, 8, 9, 15-18. 2. As the God of grace, he tenderly comforts, and affectionately delights in his people, ver. 3, 6, 10, 11. 3. As the God of Israel, Jerusalem, and Sion, he settles their civil and religious state, ver. 2, 13, 14, 19, 20. ¶ When God manifests himself in so many things, be ashamed, my soul, that I discern him in so few. O to see God's power and glory in every work of his hand, and especially in every word of his mouth! and to be so affected with what he is, and hath done, and will do, as in everything to give thanks.

## Psalm CXLVIII.

Here the Psalmist, overwhelmed with enrapturing views of the glory, and sense of the goodness of the Lord, sends forth a solemn call to all his fellow-creatures to assist him in his songs of praise. (1) To the celestial creatures above, whether intellectual beings or not, ver. 1-6. (2) To the terrestrial creatures below, whether irrational, ver. 7-10; or rational, ver. 11-13; but chiefly to his chosen people, ver. 14. ¶ While angels and other rational beings actively trumpet forth his honours, and irrational creatures praise him objectively, in manifesting his excellencies marked on them, let my soul be ravished with his glory, amazed with his grace, and all inflamed with love, in uttering his praise, who, in Christ, is <span class="sc">my God</span> and my <span class="sc">exceeding joy</span>. Cry out, all my powers, My God, who is like unto thee! Is there any work like unto thy work!

## Psalm CXLIX.

This Psalm was perhaps penned by David when he took the stronghold of Sion from the Jebusites, and represents (1) Abundant joys to the people of God, in his relation to, delight in, and kindness towards them, ver. 1-5. (2) Abundance of terror to the proudest of their enemies, in their victory and power over them, ver. 6-9. ¶ While I sing, let mine eyes be fixed on Jesus Christ going forth, in his chariots of salvation, subduing his enemies to himself, by the gospel rod of his strength, in the apostolic and millennial period, Re. xx. 2; and, in the chariots of his power defeating his incorrigible opposers in the triumph of his law.

P<span class="sc">RAISE</span> ye the Lord: unto him sing
&emsp;a new song, and his praise
&emsp;In the assembly of his saints
&emsp;in sweet psalms do ye raise.
2 Let Isr'el in his Maker joy,
&emsp;and to him praises sing:
&emsp;Let all that Sion's children are
&emsp;be joyful in their King.
3 O let them unto his great name
&emsp;give praises in the dance;
&emsp;Let them with timbrel and with harp
&emsp;in songs his praise advance.
4 For God doth pleasure take in those
&emsp;that his own people be;
&emsp;And he with his salvation
&emsp;the meek will beautify.
5 And in his glory excellent,
&emsp;let all his saints rejoice:
&emsp;Let them to him upon their beds
&emsp;aloud lift up their voice.
6 Let in their mouth aloft be rais'd
&emsp;the high praise of the Lord,
&emsp;And let them have in their right hand
&emsp;a sharp two-edged sword;
7 To execute the vengeance due
&emsp;upon the heathen all,
&emsp;And make deserved punishment
&emsp;upon the people fall.

&emsp;their kings that them command;
&emsp;Yea, and with iron fetters strong,
&emsp;the nobles of their land.
9 On them the judgment to perform
&emsp;found written in his word:
&emsp;This honour is to all his saints.
&emsp;O do ye praise the Lord.

## Psalm CL.

This Psalm contains thirteen calls to praise God. It directs (1) Who ought to praise him, ver. 6. (2) Why they should praise him, viz. that he dwells with men; and for his mighty acts, and his excellent greatness, ver. 1, 2. And (3) How he ought to be praised,—in a public, earnest, and skilful manner, ver. 1, 3-5. ¶ Dare not, my soul, to finish thy notes, and to conclude the Book, without commencing heartily in the work. Awake then, my inward powers, let me even here begin my high hallelujahs and hosannas to him who loved me, and gave himself for me.

P<span class="sc">RAISE</span> ye the Lord. God's praise within
&emsp;his sanctuary raise;
&emsp;And to him in the firmament
&emsp;of his pow'r give ye praise.
2 Because of all his mighty acts,
&emsp;with praise him magnify:
&emsp;O praise him, as he doth excel
&emsp;in glorious majesty.
3 Praise him with trumpet's sound; his praise
&emsp;with psaltery advance:
4 With timbrel, harp, string'd instruments,
&emsp;and organs, in the dance.
5 Praise him on cymbals loud; him praise
&emsp;on cymbals sounding high.
6 Let each thing breathing praise the Lord.
&emsp;Praise to the Lord give ye.
