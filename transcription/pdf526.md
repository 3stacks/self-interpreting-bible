## Psalm CXXXVII.

This Psalm was probably composed in Chaldea during the captivity, and contains (1) The Jews grievously bewailing of their distress, contempt, and reproach, ver. 1-4. (2) Their tender and affectionate remembrance of, and concern for Jerusalem, the church, and the ordinances of God, ver. 5, 6. (3) Denunciations of destruction to the Edomites who had promoted, and the Chaldeans who had effected their distress and captivity, ver. 7-9. ¶ While I sing, let me bless the Lord for what mercy is in my lot above that of many others. Let the welfare of God's church lie near my heart. Let me earnestly desire, and firmly expect the ruin of all her and my spiritual foes.

B<span class="sc">Y</span> Babel's streams we sat and wept,
&emsp;when Sion we thought on.
2 In midst thereof we hang'd our harps
&emsp;the willow-trees upon.
3 For there a song required they,
&emsp;who did us captive bring:
&emsp;Our spoilers call'd for mirth, and said,
&emsp;A song of Sion sing.

4 O how the Lord's song shall we sing
&emsp;within a foreign land?
5 If thee, Jerus'lem, I forget,
&emsp;skill part from my right hand.
6 My tongue to my mouth's roof let cleave,
&emsp;if I do thee forget,
&emsp;Jerusalem, and thee above
&emsp;my chief joy do not set.

7 Remember Edom's children, Lord,
&emsp;who in Jerus'lem's day,
&emsp;Ev'n unto its foundation,
&emsp;Raze, raze it quite, did say.
8 O daughter thou of Babylon,
&emsp;near to destruction;
&emsp;Bless'd shall he be that thee rewards,
&emsp;as thou to us hast done.

9 Yea, happy surely shall he be
&emsp;thy tender little ones
&emsp;Who shall lay hold upon, and them
&emsp;shall dash against the stones.

## Psalm CXXXVIII.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was perhaps composed by David when he was newly advanced to his throne, 2 Sa. v. 1 Ch. xii. In it (1) He looks back with thankfulness upon the experience which he had of God's goodness to him, ver. 1-3. (2) He looks forward with comfort, in hopes, 1, That others would go on to praise God like him, ver. 4, 5. 2, That God would continue to do good to him, ver. 6-8. ¶ While I sing these lofty lines, let the sense of the unnumbered mercies I have received from God, and the hope of his everlasting loving-kindness, tune and animate my heart.

T<span class="sc">HEE</span> will I praise with all my heart,
&emsp;I will sing praise to thee
2 Before the gods: And worship will
&emsp;toward thy sanctuary.
&emsp;I'll praise thy name, ev'n for thy truth,
&emsp;and kindness of thy love;
&emsp;For thou thy word hast magnify'd
&emsp;all thy great name above.

3 Thou didst me answer in the day
&emsp;when I to thee did cry;
&emsp;And thou my fainting soul with strength
&emsp;didst strengthen inwardly.
4 All kings upon the earth that are
&emsp;shall give thee praise, O Lord;
&emsp;When as they from thy mouth shall hear
&emsp;thy true and faithful word.

5 Yea, in the righteous ways of God
&emsp;with gladness they shall sing:
&emsp;For great's the glory of the Lord,
&emsp;who doth for ever reign.
6 Though God be high, yet he respects
&emsp;all those that lowly be;
&emsp;Whereas the proud and lofty ones
&emsp;afar off knoweth he.

7 Though I in midst of trouble walk,
&emsp;I life from thee shall have:
&emsp;'Gainst my foes' wrath thou'lt stretch thine hand,
&emsp;thy right hand shall me save.
8 Surely that which concerneth me
&emsp;the Lord will perfect make:
&emsp;Lord, still thy mercy lasts; do not
&emsp;thine own hands' works forsake.

## Psalm CXXXIX.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was probably penned by David when he was loaded with some vile reproaches, and contains (1) His celebration of, 1, The omniscient knowledge, ver. 1-6; 2, The omnipresence, ver. 7-12; and 3, The creating kindness of God, ver. 13-16. (2) His improvement of these, 1, In devout meditation on God. ver. 17, 18; 2, In detestation of wicked men, ver. 19-22; 3, In solemn profession of his uprightness before God, ver. 23, 24. ¶ Let me sing, let me pray, and let me live always under the deepest impressions of God's eye upon me; his presence with me; his formation of me; his new-covenant marriage to me; and his everlasting redemption of my soul. Let my nights and my days be filled up with precious, with fixed, with pleasant thoughts concerning these, and holy improvements of them. Let me never make the Lord's enemies my intimates. Let a thorough purgation from sin, and progress in holiness, be the delight, the earnest desire of my soul.

O <span class="sc">Lord</span>, thou hast me search'd and known.
2 Thou know'st my sitting down,
&emsp;And rising up; yea, all my thoughts
&emsp;afar to thee are known.
3 My footsteps, and my lying down,
&emsp;thou compassest always;
&emsp;Thou also most entirely art
&emsp;acquaint with all my ways.

4 For in my tongue, before I speak,
&emsp;not any word can be,
&emsp;But altogether, lo, O Lord,
&emsp;it is well known to thee.
5 Behind, before, thou hast beset,
&emsp;and laid on me thine hand.
6 Such knowledge is too strange for me,
&emsp;too high to understand.

7 From thy Sp'rit whither shall I go?
&emsp;or from thy presence fly?
8 Ascend I heav'n, lo, thou art there;
&emsp;there, if in hell I lie.
9 Take I the morning wings, and dwell
&emsp;in th' utmost parts of sea;
10 Ev'n there, Lord, shall thy hand me lead,
&emsp;thy right hand hold shall me.

11 If I do say that darkness shall
&emsp;me cover from thy sight,
&emsp;Then surely shall the very night
&emsp;about me be as light.
12 Yea, darkness hideth not from thee,
&emsp;but night doth shine as day:
&emsp;To thee the darkness and the light
&emsp;are both alike alway.

13 For thou possessed hast my reins,
&emsp;and thou hast cover'd me,
&emsp;When I within my mother's womb
&emsp;enclosed was by thee.
14 Thee will I praise; for fearfully
&emsp;and strangely made I am;
&emsp;Thy works are marv'llous, and right well
&emsp;my soul doth know the same.

15 My substance was not hid from thee,
&emsp;when as in secret I
&emsp;Was made; and in earth's lowest parts
&emsp;was wrought most curiously.
16 Thine eyes my substance did behold,
&emsp;yet being unperfect;
&emsp;And in the volume of thy book
&emsp;my members all were writ;

&emsp;Which after in continuance
&emsp;were fashion'd ev'ry one,
&emsp;When as they yet all shapeless were,
&emsp;and of them there was none.
17 How precious also are thy thoughts,
&emsp;O gracious God, to me!
&emsp;And in their sum how passing great
&emsp;and numberless they be!

18 If I should count them, than the sand
&emsp;they more in number be:
&emsp;What time soever I awake,
&emsp;I ever am with thee.
19 Thou, Lord, wilt sure the wicked slay:
&emsp;hence from me bloody men.
20 Thy foes against thee loudly speak,
&emsp;and take thy name in vain.

21 Do not I hate all those, O Lord,
&emsp;that hatred bear to thee?
&emsp;With those that up against thee rise
&emsp;can I but grieved be?
22 With perfect hatred them I hate,
&emsp;my foes I them do hold.
23 Search me, O God, and know my heart,
&emsp;try me, my thoughts unfold;

24 And see if any wicked way
&emsp;there be at all in me;
&emsp;And in thine everlasting way
&emsp;to me a leader be.

## Psalm CXL.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was probably penned by David when persecuted by Saul; and perhaps partly relates to Doeg the Edomite, as Ps. lii. and cxx. In it (1) David complains of the malice of his enemies, and supplicates God's preservation from them, ver. 1-5. (2) He encourages himself in God as his God, ver. 6, 7. (3) He prays for, and prophecies the destruction of all his enemies, ver. 8-11. (4) He assures himself, and other saints, that all their troubles shall end happily, ver. 12, 13. ¶ Let my troubles stir me up to fervent prayer, and to an earnest believing on God as my God. Thus shall I suck honey out of the flinty rock; and the malice, as well as the hate of mine enemies, shall work for me.

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, from the ill and froward man
&emsp;give me deliverance,
&emsp;And do thou safe preserve me from
&emsp;the man of violence:
2 Who in their heart mischievous things
&emsp;are meditating ever;
&emsp;And they for war assembled are
&emsp;continually together.

3 Much like unto a serpent's tongue
&emsp;their tongues they sharp do make;
&emsp;And underneath there lips there lies
&emsp;the poison of a snake.
4 Lord, keep me from the wicked's hands,
&emsp;from violent men me save;
&emsp;Who utterly to overthrow
&emsp;my goings purpos'd have.

5 The proud for me a snare have hid,
&emsp;and cords; yea, they a net
&emsp;Have by the way-side for me spread;
&emsp;they gins for me have set.
6 I said unto the Lord, Thou art
&emsp;my God: unto the cry
&emsp;Of all my supplications,
&emsp;Lord, do thine ear apply.

7 O God the Lord, who art the strength
&emsp;of my salvation:
&emsp;A cov'ring in the day of war
&emsp;my head thou hast put on.
8 Unto the wicked man, O Lord,
&emsp;his wishes do not grant;
&emsp;Nor further thou his ill device,
&emsp;lest they themselves should vaunt.

9 As for the head and chief of those
&emsp;about that compass me,
&emsp;Ev'n by the mischief of their lips
&emsp;let thou them cover'd be.
10 Let burning coals upon them fall,
&emsp;them throw in fiery flame,
&emsp;And in-deep pits, that they no more
&emsp;may rise out of the same.

11 Let not an evil speaker be
&emsp;on earth established:
&emsp;Mischief shall hunt the violent man,
&emsp;till he be ruined.
