# Hymns

## Hymn I.

W<span class="sc">HEN</span> all thy mercies, O my God!
&emsp;my rising soul surveys,
Transported with the view, I'm lost
&emsp;in wonder, love, and praise.

2 O how shall words, with equal warmth,
&emsp;the gratitude declare
That glows within my ravish'd heart!
&emsp;but Thou canst read it there.

3 Thy Providence my life sustain'd,
&emsp;and all my wants redrest,
When in the silent womb I lay,
&emsp;and hung upon the breast.

4 To all my weak complaints and cries
&emsp;thy mercy lent an ear,
Ere yet my feeble thoughts had learn'd
&emsp;to form themselves in pray'r.

5 Unnumber'd comforts to my soul
&emsp;thy tender care bestow'd,
Before my infant heart conceiv'd
&emsp;from whom these comforts flow'd.

6 When in the slipp'ry paths of youth
&emsp;with heedless steps I ran;
Thine arm, unseen, convey'd me safe,
&emsp;and led me up to man:

7 Through hidden dangers, toils, and deaths,
&emsp;it gently clear'd my way;
And through the pleasing snares of vice,
&emsp;more to be fear'd than they.

8 When worn with sickness, oft hast thou
&emsp;with health renew'd my face;
And, when in sins and sorrows sunk,
&emsp;reviv'd my soul with grace.

9 Thy bounteous hand with worldly bliss
&emsp;hath made my cup run o'er;
And, in a kind and faithful friend,
&emsp;hath doubled all my store.

10 Ten thousand thousand precious gifts
&emsp;my daily thanks employ;
Nor is the least a cheerful heart,
&emsp;that tastes these gifts with joy.

11 Through ev'ry period of my life
&emsp;thy goodness I'll proclaim;
And after death, in distant worlds,
&emsp;resume the glorious theme.

12 When nature fails, and day and night
&emsp;divide thy works no more,
My ever grateful heart, O Lord,
&emsp;thy mercy shall adore.

13 Through all eternity to thee
&emsp;a joyful song I'll raise;
For, oh! eternity's too short
&emsp;to utter all thy praise.

## Hymn II.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> spacious firmament on high,
With all the blue ethereal sky,
And spangled heav'ns, a shining frame,
Their great Original proclaim.

2 Th' unweary'd sun, from day to day,
Does his Creator's pow'r display;
And publishes to ev'ry land
The work of an Almighty hand.

3 Soon as the ev'ning shades prevail,
The moon takes up the wondrous tale,
And, nightly to the list'ning earth,
Repeats the story of her birth;

4 While all the stars that round her burn.
And all the planets in their turn,
Confirm the tidings as they roll,
And spread the truth from pole to pole.

5 What though in solemn silence all
Move round the dark terrestrial ball?
What though no real voice, nor sound,
Amidst their radiant orbs be found?

6 In Reason's ear they all rejoice,
And utter forth a glorious voice;
For ever singing, as they shine,
"The hand that made us is divine."

## Hymn III.

W<span class="sc">HEN</span> rising from the bed of death,
&emsp;o'erwhelm'd with guilt and fear,
I see my Maker face to face,
&emsp;O how shall I appear!

2 If yet while pardon may be found,
&emsp;and mercy may be sought,
My heart with inward horror shrinks,
&emsp;and trembles at the thought;

3 When thou, O Lord! shalt stand disclos'd
&emsp;in majesty severe,
And sit in judgment on my soul,
&emsp;O how shall I appear!

4 But thou hast told the troubled mind,
&emsp;who doth her sins lament,
That timely grief for errors past
&emsp;shall future woe prevent.

5 Then see the sorrows of my heart,
&emsp;ere yet it be too late,
And hear my Saviour's dying groans,
&emsp;to give those sorrows weight.

6 For never shall my soul despair
&emsp;of mercy at thy throne,
Who knows thine only Son has dy'd
&emsp;thy justice to atone.

## Hymn IV.

B<span class="sc">LEST</span> morning! whose first dawning ray,
&emsp;beheld the Son of God
Arise triumphant from the grave,
&emsp;and leave his dark abode.

2 Wrapt in the silence of the tomb
&emsp;the great Redeemer lay,
Till the revolving skies had brought
&emsp;the third, th' appointed day.

3 Hell and the grave combin'd their force
&emsp;to hold our Lord in vain;
Sudden the Conqueror arose,
&emsp;and burst their feeble chain.

4 To thy great name, Almighty Lord!
&emsp;we sacred honours pay,
And loud hosannahs shall proclaim
&emsp;the triumphs of the day.

5 Salvation and immortal praise
&emsp;to our victorious King!
Let heav'n and earth, and rocks and seas,
&emsp;with glad hosannahs ring.

6 To Father, Son, and Holy Ghost,
&emsp;the God whom we adore,
Be glory, as it was, and is,
&emsp;and shall be evermore.

## Hymn V.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> hour of my departure's come;
I hear the voice that calls me home
At last, O Lord! let trouble cease,
And let thy servant die in peace.

2 The race appointed I have run;
The combat's o'er, the prize is won;
And now my witness is on high,
And now my record's in the sky.

3 Not in mine innocence I trust;
I bow before thee in the dust;
And through my Saviour's blood alone
I look for mercy at thy throne.

4 I leave the world without a tear,
Save for the friends I held so dear;
To heal their sorrows, Lord, descend,
And to the friendless prove a friend.

5 I come, I come, at thy command,
I give my spirit to thy hand;
Stretch forth thine everlasting arms,
And shield me in the last alarms.

6 The hour of my departure's come;
I hear the voice that calls me home;
Now, O my God! let trouble cease;
Now let thy servant die in peace.
