## Paraphrase VI.

*Job viii. 11-22.*

O what a common sin among professors is hypocrisy! what a sad cause of it is forgetfulness of God! what a fearful cheat the hope of the hypocrite puts upon himself! though it flourish a while, it perisheth at last. What important admonitions the things of nature around us suggest, did we but consider them aright! O my soul, let me never be a harsh judge of others, and never too mild to myself. Wretched man that I am, who shall deliver me from this hypocritical heart. Blessed God, examine me and prove me, try my reins and my heart!

T<span class="sc">HE</span> rush may rise where waters flow,
&emsp;and flags beside the stream;
But soon their verdure fades and dies
&emsp;before the scorching beam:
2 So is the sinner's hope cut off;
&emsp;or, if it transient rise,
'Tis like the spider's airy web,
&emsp;from ev'ry breath that flies.

3 Fix'd on his house he leans; his house
&emsp;and all its props decay:
He holds it fast; but, while he holds,
&emsp;the tott'ring frame gives way.
4 Fair, in his garden, to the sun,
&emsp;his boughs with verdure smile;
And, deeply fix'd, his spreading roots
&emsp;unshaken stand a while.

5 But forth the sentence flies from Heav'n,
&emsp;that sweeps him from his place;
Which then denies him for its lord,
&emsp;nor owns it knew his face.
6 Lo! this the joy of wicked men,
&emsp;who Heav'n's high laws despise:
They quickly fall; and in their room
&emsp;as quickly others rise.

7 But, for the just, with gracious care,
&emsp;God will his pow'r employ;
He'll teach their lips to sing his praise,
&emsp;and fill their hearts with joy.

## Paraphrase VII.

*Job ix. 2-10.*

While I sing these words, think, my soul, on the infinite greatness of God! What is man, so mean, so sinful, that he should be mindful of him! how incapable to advance a justifying righteousness before him! how dreadful the case of those who harden themselves against him. O how plainly are his excellencies marked in his works of creation and providence. Happy they whose God is Jehovah! and inexpressibly miserable are his most powerful and proud opposers. Kiss the Son, my soul, lest God be angry and I perish from the way.

H<span class="sc">OW</span> should the sons of Adam's race
&emsp;be pure before their God?
If he contends in righteousness,
&emsp;we sink beneath his rod.
2 If he should mark my words and thoughts
&emsp;with strict inquiring eyes,
Could I for one of thousand faults
&emsp;the least excuse devise?

3 Strong is his arm, his heart is wise;
&emsp;who dares with him contend?
Or who, that tries th' unequal strife,
&emsp;shall prosper in the end?
4 He makes the mountains feel his wrath,
&emsp;and their old seats forsake;
The trembling earth deserts her place,
&emsp;and all her pillars shake.

5 He bids the sun forbear to rise;
&emsp;th' obedient sun forbears:
His hand with sackcloth spreads the skies,
&emsp;and seals up all the stars.
6 He walks upon the raging sea;
&emsp;flies on the stormy wind:
None can explore his wondrous way,
&emsp;or his dark footsteps find.

## Paraphrase VIII.

*Job xiv. 1-15.*

Think, my soul, if human life is so short, so sinful, so wretched[?]; if death and eternity be so near, why should any be proud[?]? why unconcerned about eternal happiness? why not live[?] on that gracious God who has a feeling of our infirmities, and look and long for that rest that remaineth for the people of God? How comfortable, but heart awing, yet heart-composing, are believing views of death and the resurrection from the jaws of the grave! How pleasant to think that while my body, as precious dust, sleeps in Jesus' arms of kindly care, my soul shall retire with him on his throne, and there wait till my last, my great change come. The hope of a glorious resurrection is enough to support me under every earthly trouble.

F<span class="sc">EW</span> are thy days, and full of woe,
&emsp;O man, of woman born!
Thy doom is written, "Dust thou art,
&emsp;"and shalt to dust return."
2 Behold the emblem of thy state
&emsp;in flow'rs that bloom and die,
Or in the shadow's fleeting form,
&emsp;that mocks the gazer's eye.

3 Guilty and frail, how shalt thou stand
&emsp;before thy sov'reign Lord?
Can troubled and polluted springs
&emsp;a hallow'd stream afford?
4 Determin'd are the days that fly
&emsp;successive o'er thy head;
The number'd hour is on the wing
&emsp;that lays thee with the dead.

5 Great God! afflict not in thy wrath
&emsp;the short allotted span,
That bounds the few and weary days
&emsp;of pilgrimage to man.
6 All nature dies, and lives again:
&emsp;the flow'r that paints the field,
The trees that crown the mountain's brow,
&emsp;and boughs and blossoms yield,

7 Resign the honours of their form
&emsp;at Winter's stormy blast,
And leave the naked leafless plain
&emsp;a desolated waste.
8 Yet soon reviving plants and flow'rs
&emsp;anew shall deck the plain;
The woods shall hear the voice of Spring,
&emsp;and flourish green again.

9 But man forsakes this earthly scene,
&emsp;ah! never to return:
Shall any foll'wing spring revive
&emsp;the ashes of the urn?
10 The mighty flood that rolls along
&emsp;its torrents to the main,
Can ne'er recall its waters lost
&emsp;from that abyss again.

11 So days, and years, and ages past,
&emsp;descending down to night,
Can henceforth never more return
&emsp;back to the gates of light;
12 And man, when laid in lonesome grave,
&emsp;shall sleep in Death's dark gloom,
Until th' eternal morning wake
&emsp;the slumbers of the tomb.

13 O may the grave become to me
&emsp;the bed of peaceful rest,
Whence I shall gladly rise at length,
&emsp;and mingle with the blest!
14 Cheer'd by this hope, with patient mind,
&emsp;I'll wait Heav'n's high decree,
Till the appointed period come,
&emsp;when death shall set me free.

## Paraphrase IX.

*Job xxvi. 6, to the end.*

How plainly the perfections of Deity are marked in all his works of nature and grace! How well everything is done, where God is the sole or principal agent! Let me therefore in all behold his hand and face. If he conceals the glories of his throne from this lower world, let me walk by faith, not by sight. If his perfections are unsearchable, let me always admire and adore.

W<span class="sc">HO</span> can resist th' Almighty arm
&emsp;that made the starry sky?
Or who elude the certain glance
&emsp;of God's all-seeing eye?
2 From him no cov'ring vails our crimes;
&emsp;hell opens to his sight;
And all Destruction's secret snares
&emsp;lie full disclos'd in light.

3 Firm on the boundless void of space
&emsp;he pois'd the steady pole,
And in the circle of his clouds
&emsp;bade secret waters roll.
4 While nature's universal frame
&emsp;its Maker's pow'r reveals,
His throne, remote from mortal eyes,
&emsp;an awful cloud conceals.

5 From where the rising day ascends,
&emsp;to where it sets in night,
He compasses the floods with bounds,
&emsp;and checks their threat'ning might.
6 The pillars that support the sky
&emsp;tremble at his rebuke;
Through all its caverns quakes the earth,
&emsp;as though its centre shook.

7 He brings the waters from their beds,
&emsp;although no tempest blows,
And smites the kingdom of the proud
&emsp;without the hand of foes.
8 With bright inhabitants above
&emsp;he fills the heav'nly land,
And all the crooked serpent's breed
&emsp;dismay'd before him stand.
9 Few of his works can we survey;
&emsp;these few our skill transcend:
But the full thunder of his pow'r
&emsp;what heart can comprehend?

## Paraphrase X.

*Prov. i. 20-31.*

This translation is peculiarly adapted to public ordinances: Jesus Christ addresses sinners as the Wisdom of God, particularly fools and scorners. Let me reflect how great is the compassion of Jesus to rebellious sinners: in the most earnest and open manner he pleads with them in the ordinances of his grace to promote their eternal salvation; condescending are his offers and invitations of mercy pointed to the most unworthy and wicked, and precious the benefits which he offers; but inexcusable is their guilt, and dreadful their ruin, who obstinately refuse them. Rejected of God in their misery, even their ease and prosperity harden and ensnare them.

I<span class="sc">N</span> streets, and op'nings of the gates,
&emsp;where pours the busy crowd,
Thus heav'nly Wisdom lifts her voice,
&emsp;and cries to men aloud:
2 How long, ye scorners of the truth,
&emsp;scornful will ye remain?
How long shall fools their folly love,
&emsp;and hear my words in vain?

3 O turn, at last, at my reproof!
&emsp;and, in that happy hour,
His bless'd effusions on your heart
&emsp;my Spirit down shall pour.
4 But since so long, with earnest voice,
&emsp;to you in vain I call,
Since all my counsels and reproofs
&emsp;thus ineffectual fall;

5 The time will come, when humbled low,
&emsp;in Sorrow's evil day,
Your voice by anguish shall be taught,
&emsp;but taught too late, to pray.
6 When, like the whirlwind, o'er the deep
&emsp;comes Desolation's blast.
Pray'rs then extorted shall be vain,
&emsp;the hour of mercy past.

7 The choice you made has fix'd your doom;[?]
&emsp;for this is Heav'n's decree,
That with the fruits of what he sow'd
&emsp;the sinner fill'd shall be.

## Paraphrase XI.

*Prov. iii. 13-17.*

These verses exhibit the true advantages of early piety. Real religion is truly man's first concern, his highest, his only happiness. By this he gets durable and unsearchable riches; he attains true honour, is a crown of glory in the hand of the Lord. And O how it guards him against these dangers to which sin exposes us! It guides the young in youth's slippery paths, and it throws a lustre around old age, and makes it truly respectable. What pleasure, rest, and joy, it gives to all, especially the young: they sing in the ways of the Lord. O let children come to Jesus, and he will teach them the fear of the Lord.

O <span class="sc">HAPPY</span> is the man who hears
&emsp;Instruction's warning voice;
And who celestial Wisdom makes
&emsp;his early, only choice.
2 For she has treasures greater far
&emsp;than east or west unfold;
And her rewards more precious are
&emsp;than all their stores of gold.
