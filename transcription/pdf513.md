## Psalm CI.

*A Psalm of David.*

This Psalm was probably composed when David assumed the government of his kingdom; and contains his holy resolutions (1) To make God's mercies and judgments the connected matter of his song; and to walk circumspectly before his family through the assisting presence of God, ver. 1, 2. (2) To purge his family and kingdom; discouraging and punishing persons mischievous and wicked, slanderers, liars, and deceitful workers; and encouraging the pious, ver. 3-8.

¶ While I sing, let me remember my faults; and in the strength of the grace that is in Christ Jesus, form the like holy resolutions.

I <span class="sc">Mercy</span> will and judgment sing,
&emsp;Lord, I will sing to thee.
2 With wisdom in a perfect way
&emsp;shall my behaviour be.
O when, in kindness unto me,
&emsp;wilt thou be pleas'd to come?
I with a perfect heart will walk
&emsp;within my house at home.

3 I will endure no wicked thing
&emsp;before mine eyes to be:
I hate their work that turn aside,
&emsp;it shall not cleave to me.
4 A stubborn and a froward heart
&emsp;depart quite from me shall;
A person giv'n to wickedness
&emsp;I will not know at all.

5 I'll cut him off that slandereth
&emsp;his neighbour privily:
The haughty heart I will not bear,
&emsp;nor him that looketh high.
6 Upon the faithful of the land
&emsp;mine eyes shall be, that they
May dwell with me: he shall me serve
&emsp;that walks in perfect way.

7 Who of deceit a worker is
&emsp;in my house shall not dwell;
And in my presence shall he not
&emsp;remain that lies doth tell.
8 Yea, all the wicked of the land
&emsp;early destroy will I;
All from God's city to cut off
&emsp;that work iniquity.

## Psalm CII.

*A Prayer of the afflicted when he is overwhelmed, and poureth out his complaint before the Lord.*

This Psalm relates to some grievous distress. Wherein are observable (1) Bitter complaints of divine hidings and wrath, of sore bodily troubles, of inward griefs and perplexities, of calumny and reproach, of sudden changes of condition, and of apparent nearness to death, ver. 1-11. (2) Seasonable comforts against these grievances, drawn from the eternity and unchangeableness of God, ver. 12, 24, 27; from the deliverances, spread, and establishment, which he will grant to his church in answer to the prayers, and for the comfort of his afflicted people, ver. 13-28.

¶ While I sing, think, my soul, if I am exercised by my troubles; if those of the inner man bear heaviest on my heart; and if I am a cordial partaker with Sion in her joys and griefs.

O <span class="sc">Lord</span>, unto my pray'r give ear,
&emsp;my cry let come to thee;
2 And in the day of my distress
&emsp;hide not thy face from me.
Give ear to me; what time I call,
&emsp;to answer me make haste:
3 For, as an hearth, my bones are burnt,
&emsp;my days, like smoke, do waste.

4 My heart within me smitten is,
&emsp;and it is withered
Like very grass; so that I do
&emsp;forget to eat my bread.
5 By reason of my groaning voice
&emsp;my bones cleave to my skin.
6 Like pelican in wilderness
&emsp;forsaken I have been:

I like an owl in desert am,
&emsp;that nightly there doth moan;
7 I watch, and like a sparrow am
&emsp;on the house-top alone.
8 My bitter en'mies all the day
&emsp;reproaches cast on me;
And, being mad at me, with rage
&emsp;against me sworn they be.

9 For why? I ashes eaten have
&emsp;like bread, in sorrows deep;
My drink I also mingled have
&emsp;with tears that I did weep.
10 Thy wrath and indignation
&emsp;did cause this grief and pain;
For thou hast lift me up on high,
&emsp;and cast me down again.

11 My days are like unto a shade,
&emsp;which doth declining pass;
And I am dry'd and withered,
&emsp;ev'n like unto the grass.
12 But thou, Lord, everlasting art,
&emsp;and thy remembrance shall
Continually endure, and be
&emsp;to generations all.

13 Thou shalt arise, and mercy have
&emsp;upon thy Sion yet;
The time to favour her is come,
&emsp;the time that thou hast set.
14 For in her rubbish and her stones
&emsp;thy servants pleasure take;
Yea, they the very dust thereof
&emsp;do favour for her sake.

15 So shall the heathen people fear
&emsp;the Lord's most holy name;
And all the kings on earth shall dread
&emsp;thy glory and thy fame.
16 When Sion by the mighty Lord
&emsp;built up again shall be,
In glory then and majesty
&emsp;to men appear shall he.

17 The prayer of the destitute
&emsp;he surely will regard;
Their prayer will he not despise,
&emsp;by him it shall be heard.
18 For generations yet to come
&emsp;this shall be on record:
So shall the people that shall be
&emsp;created praise the Lord.

19 He from his sanctuary's height
&emsp;hath downward cast his eye;
And from his glorious throne in heav'n
&emsp;the Lord the earth did spy;
20 That of the mournful prisoner
&emsp;the groanings he might hear,
To set them free that unto death
&emsp;by men appointed are:

21 That they in Sion may declare
&emsp;the Lord's most holy name,
And publish in Jerusalem
&emsp;the praises of the same;
22 When as the people gather shall
&emsp;in troops with one accord,
When kingdoms shall assembled be
&emsp;to serve the highest Lord.

23 My wonted strength and force he hath
&emsp;abated in the way,
And he my days hath shortened:
24 Thus therefore did I say,
My God, in mid-time of my days
&emsp;take thou me not away:
From age to age eternally
&emsp;thy years endure and stay.

25 The firm foundation of the earth
&emsp;of old time thou hast laid;
The heavens also are the work
&emsp;which thine own hands have made.
26 Thou shalt for evermore endure,
&emsp;but they shall perish all;
Yea, ev'ry one of them wax old,
&emsp;like to a garment, shall:
Thou, as a vesture, shalt them change,
&emsp;and they shall changed be:

27 But thou the same art, and thy years
&emsp;are to eternity.
28 The children of thy servants shall
&emsp;continually endure;
And in thy sight, O Lord, their seed
&emsp;shall be establish'd sure.

*Another of the same.*

L<span class="sc">ORD</span>, hear my pray'r, and let my cry
Have speedy access unto thee;
2 In day of my calamity
O hide not thou thy face from me.
Hear when I call to thee; that day
An answer speedily return:
3 My days, like smoke, consume away,
And, as an hearth, my bones do burn.

4 My heart is wounded very sore,
And withered, like grass doth fade:
I am forgetful grown therefore
To take and eat my daily bread.
5 By reason of my smart within,
And voice of my most grievous groans,
My flesh consumed is, my skin,
All parch'd, doth cleave unto my bones.

6 The pelican of wilderness,
The owl in desert, I do match;
And, sparrow-like, companionless,
Upon the house's top, I watch.
8 I all day long am made a scorn,
Reproach'd by my malicious foes:
The madmen are against me sworn,
The men against me that arose.

9 For I have ashes eaten up,
To me as if they had been bread;
And with my drink I in my cup
Of bitter tears a mixture made.
10 Because thy wrath was not appeas'd,
And dreadful indignation:
Therefore it was that thou me rais'd,
And thou again didst cast me down.

11 My days are like a shade alway,
Which doth declining swiftly pass;
And I am withered away,
Much like unto the fading grass.
12 But thou, O Lord, shalt still endure,
From change and all mutation free,
And to all generations sure
Shall thy remembrance ever be.
