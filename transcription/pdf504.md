3 For I envious was, and grudg'd
&emsp;the foolish folk to see,
When I perceiv'd the wicked sort
&emsp;enjoy prosperity.

4 For still their strength continueth firm;
&emsp;their death of bands is free.
5 They are not toil'd like other men,
&emsp;nor plagu'd, as others be.

6 Therefore their pride, like to a chain,
&emsp;them compasseth about;
And, as a garment, violence
&emsp;doth cover them throughout.

7 Their eyes stand out with fat; they have
&emsp;more than their hearts could wish.
8 They are corrupt; their talk of wrong
&emsp;both lewd and lofty is.

9 They set their mouth against the heav'ns
&emsp;in their blasphemous talk;
And their reproaching tongue throughout
&emsp;the earth at large doth walk.

10 His people oftentimes for this
&emsp;look back, and turn about;
Sith waters of so full a cup
&emsp;to these are poured out.

11 And thus they say, How can it be
&emsp;that God these things doth know?
Or, Can there in the Highest be
&emsp;knowledge of things below?

12 Behold, these are the wicked ones,
&emsp;yet prosper at their will
In worldly things; they do increase
&emsp;in wealth and riches still.

13 I verily have done in vain
&emsp;my heart to purify;
To no effect in innocence
&emsp;washed my hands have I.

14 For daily, and all day throughout,
&emsp;great plagues I suffer'd have;
Yea, ev'ry morning I of new
&emsp;did chastisement receive.

15 If in this manner foolishly
&emsp;to speak I would intend,
Thy children's generation,
&emsp;behold, I should offend.

16 When I this thought to know, it was
&emsp;too hard a thing for me;
17 Till to God's sanctuary I went,
&emsp;then I their end did see.

18 Assuredly thou didst them set
&emsp;a slipp'ry place upon;
Them suddenly thou castedst down
&emsp;into destruction.

19 How in a moment suddenly
&emsp;to ruin brought are they!
With fearful terrors utterly
&emsp;they are consum'd away.

20 Ev'n like unto a dream, when one
&emsp;from sleeping doth arise;
So thou, O Lord, when thou awak'st,
&emsp;their image shalt despise.

21 Thus grieved was my heart in me,
&emsp;and me my reins opprest:
22 So rude was I, and ignorant,
&emsp;and in thy sight a beast.

23 Nevertheless continually,
&emsp;O Lord, I am with thee:
Thou dost me hold by my right hand,
&emsp;and still upholdest me.

24 Thou, with thy counsel, while I live,
&emsp;wilt me conduct and guide;
And to thy glory afterward
&emsp;receive me to abide.

25 Whom have I in the heavens high
&emsp;but thee, O Lord, alone?
And in the earth whom I desire
&emsp;besides thee there is none.

26 My flesh and heart doth faint and fail,
&emsp;but God doth fail me never:
For of my heart God is the strength
&emsp;and portion for ever.

27 For, lo, they that are far from thee
&emsp;for ever perish shall;
Them that a whoring from thee go
&emsp;thou hast destroyed all.

28 But surely it is good for me
&emsp;that I draw near to God:
In God I trust, that all thy works
&emsp;I may declare abroad.

## Psalm LXXIV.

*Maschil of Asaph.*

This Psalm relates to the destruction of the city and temple of Jerusalem by the Chaldeans, or to some similar disaster. In it we have (1) The church's bitter complaints of God's displeasure; of the outrage of their enemies; and of their apparent hopeless situation, ver. 1-11. (2) Strong and heart-encouraging pleadings with God upon the account of his relation to them, and of the great things he had done for them; that he was at once their God and the God of nature; and that therefore he would remember to execute just rewards upon his sworn enemies, and grant help and relief to his covenant-people, ver. 12-23.

¶ While I sing this, let me admire the sovereignty and holiness of God in so severely correcting his own people. And let the broken, the deserted condition of the church in this land, deeply affect my heart, and excite my earnest prayers for her restoration.

O <span class="sc">GOD</span>, why hast thou cast us off?
&emsp;is it for evermore?
Against thy pasture-sheep why doth
&emsp;thine anger smoke so sore?

2 O call to thy rememberance
&emsp;thy congregation,
Which thou hast purchased of old;
&emsp;still think the same upon:
The rod of thine inheritance,
&emsp;which thou redeemed hast,
This Sion hill, wherein thou hadst
&emsp;thy dwelling in times past.

3 To these long desolations
&emsp;thy feet lift, do not tarry;
For all the ills thy foes have done
&emsp;within thy sanctuary.

4 Amidst thy congregations
&emsp;thine enemies do roar:
Their ensigns they set up for signs
&emsp;of triumph thee before.

5 A man was famous, and was had
&emsp;in estimation,
According as he lifted up
&emsp;his axe thick trees upon.

6 But all at once with axes now
&emsp;and hammers they go to,
And down the carved work thereof
&emsp;they break, and quite undo.

7 They fired have thy sanctuary,
&emsp;and have defil'd the same,
By casting down unto the ground
&emsp;the place where dwelt thy name.

8 Thus said they in their hearts, Let us
&emsp;destroy them out of hand:
They burnt up all the synagogues
&emsp;of God within the land.

9 Our signs we do not now behold:
&emsp;there is not us among
A prophet more, nor any one
&emsp;that knows the time how long.

10 How long, Lord, shall the enemy
&emsp;thus in reproach exclaim?
And shall the adversary thus
&emsp;always blaspheme thy name?

11 Thy hand, ev'n thy right hand of might,
&emsp;why dost thou thus draw back?
O from thy bosom pluck it out
&emsp;for our deliv'rance' sake.

12 For certainly God is my King,
&emsp;ev'n from the times of old,
Working in midst of all the earth
&emsp;salvation manifold.

13 The sea, by thy great pow'r, to part
&emsp;asunder thou didst make:
And thou the dragons' heads, O Lord,
&emsp;within the waters brake.

14 The leviathan's head thou brak'st
&emsp;in pieces, and didst give
Him to be meat unto the folk
&emsp;in wilderness that live.

15 Thou clav'st the fountain and the flood,
&emsp;which did with streams abound:
Thou dry'dst the mighty waters up
&emsp;unto the very ground.

16 Thine only is the day, O Lord,
&emsp;thine also is the night;
And thou alone prepared hast
&emsp;the sun and shining light.

17 By thee the borders of the earth
&emsp;were settled ev'ry where:
The summer and the winter both
&emsp;by thee created were.

18 That th' enemy reproached hath,
&emsp;O keep it in record;
And that the foolish people have
&emsp;blasphem'd thy name, O Lord.

19 Unto the multitude do not
&emsp;thy turtle's soul deliver:
The congregation of thy poor
&emsp;do not forget for ever.

20 Unto thy cov'nant have respect:
&emsp;for earth's dark places be
Full of the habitations
&emsp;of horrid cruelty.

21 O let not those that be oppress'd
&emsp;return again with shame:
Let those that poor and needy are
&emsp;give praise unto thy name.

22 Do thou, O God, arise and plead
&emsp;the cause that is thine own:
Remember how thou art reproach'd
&emsp;still by the foolish one.

23 Do not forget the voice of those
&emsp;that are thine enemies:
Of those the tumult ever grows
&emsp;that do against thee rise.

## Psalm LXXV.

*To the chief Musician, Al-taschith, A Psalm or Song of Asaph.*

Here (1) David returns thanks to God for advancing him to the throne of Israel, and resolves to act for the public welfare, ver. 1-3, 9, 10. (2) He rebukes the insolence of such as opposed his advancement, which sprung from the sovereign disposal of God the Judge of the world, and denounces their destruction, ver. 4-8.

¶ While I sing. let me think of Jesus, whom God hath crowned with glory and honour, and of the infinite danger of opposing his government. And if he exalt me to the spiritual honours of his kingdom, let it be my care to glorify him, and to profit his people.

T<span class="sc">O</span> thee, O God, do we give thanks,
&emsp;we do give thanks to thee;
Because thy wondrous works declare
&emsp;thy great name near to be.

2 I purpose, when I shall receive
&emsp;the congregation,
That I shall judgment uprightly
&emsp;render to ev'ry one.

3 Dissolved is the land, with all
&emsp;that in the same do dwell;
But I the pillars thereof do
&emsp;bear up, and stablish well.

4 I to the foolish people said,
&emsp;Do not deal foolishly;
And unto those that wicked are,
&emsp;Lift not your horn on high.

5 Lift not your horn on high, nor speak
6 &emsp;with stubborn neck. But know,
That not from east, nor west, nor south,
&emsp;promotion doth flow.

7 But God is judge; he puts down one,
&emsp;and sets another up.
