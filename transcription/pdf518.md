2 The Lord shall out of Sion send
&emsp;the rod of thy great pow'r:
In midst of all thine enemies
&emsp;be thou the governor.

3 A willing people in thy day
&emsp;of pow'r shall come to thee,
In holy beauties from morn's womb;
&emsp;thy youth like dew shall be.
4 The Lord himself hath made an oath,
&emsp;and will repent him never,
Of th' order of Melchisedec
&emsp;thou art a priest for ever.

5 The glorious and mighty Lord,
&emsp;that sits at thy right hand,
Shall, in his day of wrath, strike through
&emsp;kings that do him withstand.
6 He shall among the heathen judge,
&emsp;he shall with bodies dead
The places fill: o'er many lands
&emsp;he wound shall ev'ry head.

7 The brook that runneth in the way
&emsp;with drink shall him supply;
And, for this cause, in triumph he
&emsp;shall lift his head on high.

## Psalm CXI.

In this Psalm, every sentence of which begins with a new letter of the Hebrew alphabet, we have (1) The praising of God inculcated and exemplified, ver. 1. (2) The grounds of praise exhibited, viz. that God himself is gracious, full of compassion, and ever mindful of his covenant; and that his works are magnificent, pleasant, righteous, wonderful, perfect, kind, prudent, and durable, and all conformed to his promise and covenant, ver. 2-9. (3) A recommendation of a holy and reverential obedience to God, as the beginning of wisdom, ver. 10. ¶ While I sing, let my heart be all inflamed with the believing consideration of the marvellous works of redemption, as wrought for and on my soul,—all filled with that love which casteth out slavish fear, but powerfully promotes the filial reverence and awe of God.

P<span class="sc">RAISE</span> ye the Lord: with my whole heart
&emsp;I will God's praise declare,
Where the assemblies of the just
&emsp;and congregations are.
2 The whole works of the Lord our God
&emsp;are great above all measure,
Sought out they are of ev'ry one
&emsp;that doth therein take pleasure.

3 His work most honourable is,
&emsp;most glorious and pure,
And his untainted righteousness
&emsp;for ever doth endure.
4 His works most wonderful he hath
&emsp;made to be thought upon:
The Lord is gracious, and he is
&emsp;full of compassion.

5 He giveth meat unto all those
&emsp;that truly do him fear;
And evermore his covenant
&emsp;he in his mind will bear.
6 He did the power of his works
&emsp;unto his people show,
When he the heathen's heritage
&emsp;upon them did bestow.

7 His handy-works are truth and right;
&emsp;all his commands are sure:
8 And, done in truth and uprightness,
&emsp;they evermore endure.
9 He sent redemption to his folk;
&emsp;his covenant for aye
He did command: holy his name
&emsp;and rev'rend is alway.

10 Wisdom's beginning is God's fear;
&emsp;good understanding they
Have all that his commands fulfil;
&emsp;his praise endures for aye.

## Psalm CXII.

This Psalm is also alphabetically composed, and contains (1) Invitations to praise God, ver. 1. (2) A delineation of the saints' character and blessedness; that they are well principled, honest, sincere, and kind; shall be blessed with prosperity outward and inward, with comfort amidst griefs, wisdom amidst perplexity, and with honour and stability; and that their posterity shall be blessed after them, ver. 2-9. (3) The misery of the wicked, ver. 10. ¶ While I sing this, let me try my character, and rejoice in hope of being blessed in Christ Jesus, with all spiritual, temporal, and everlasting blessings.

P<span class="sc">RAISE</span> ye the Lord. The man is bless'd
&emsp;that fears the Lord aright,
He who in his commandements
&emsp;doth greatly take delight.
2 His seed and offspring powerful
&emsp;shall be the earth upon:
Of upright men blessed shall be
&emsp;the generation.

3 Riches and wealth shall ever be
&emsp;within his house in store;
And his unspotted righteousness
&emsp;endures for evermore.
4 Unto the upright light doth rise,
&emsp;though he in darkness be:
Compassionate, and merciful,
&emsp;and righteous, is he.

5 A good man doth his favour show,
&emsp;and doth to others lend:
He with discretion his affairs
&emsp;will guide unto the end.
6 Surely there is not any thing
&emsp;that ever shall him move:
The righteous man's memorial
&emsp;shall everlasting prove.

7 When he shall evil tidings hear,
&emsp;he shall not be afraid:
His heart is fix'd, his confidence
&emsp;upon the Lord is stay'd.
8 His heart is firmly stablished,
&emsp;afraid he shall not be,
Until upon his enemies
&emsp;he his desire shall see.

9 He hath dispers'd, giv'n to the poor;
&emsp;his righteousness shall be
To ages all; with honour shall
&emsp;his horn be raised high.
10 The wicked shall it see, and fret,
&emsp;his teeth gnash, melt away:
What wicked men do most desire
&emsp;shall utterly decay.

## Psalm CXIII.

This Psalm, with the five immediately following, were called the great <span class="sc">Hallelujah</span>, and used to be sung at the passover supper, Mat. xxvi. 30. Here (1) We are solemnly called to celebrate the praises of God, ver. 1-3. (2) We are plentifully furnished with matter of praise, viz. the *heights* of his glory and greatness, and the *depths* of his condescension and grace, ver. 4-9. ¶ Think, O my soul, what the Lord hath done for me! and for mine! and for mankind!

P<span class="sc">RAISE</span> God: ye servants of the Lord,
&emsp;O praise, the Lord's name praise.
2 Yea, blessed be the name of God
&emsp;from this time forth always.
3 From rising sun to where it sets,
&emsp;God's name is to be prais'd.
4 Above all nations God is high,
&emsp;'bove heav'ns his glory rais'd.

5 Unto the Lord our God that dwells
&emsp;on high, who can compare?
6 Himself that humbleth things to see
&emsp;in heav'n and earth that are
7 He from the dust doth raise the poor,
&emsp;that very low doth lie;
And from the dunghill lifts the man
&emsp;oppress'd with poverty;

8 That he may highly him advance,
&emsp;and with the princes set;
With those that of his people are
&emsp;the chief, ev'n princes great.
9 The barren woman house to keep
&emsp;he maketh, and to be
Of sons a mother full of joy.
&emsp;Praise to the Lord give ye.

## Psalm CXIV.

In this Psalm are celebrated (1) God's bringing Israel out of their Egyptian slavery, ver. 1. (2) His setting up his tabernacle among them, and taking them for his peculiar people, ver. 2. (3) His dividing the Red Sea and Jordan before them, ver. 3, 5. (4) His awful descent on Mount Sinai to give them his law, ver. 4, 6, 7. (5) His providing them with water from the flinty rocks, ver. 8. ¶ While I sing, let my soul be affected with the grace, the glory, and the power of my God; with the mighty things he hath done for men, chiefly in the work of our redemption; and be encouraged to trust him in the greatest straits.

W<span class="sc">HEN</span> Isr'el out of Egypt went,
&emsp;and did his dwelling change,
When Jacob's house went out from those
&emsp;that were of language strange,
2 He Judah did his sanctuary,
&emsp;his kingdom Isr'el make:
3 The sea it saw, and quickly fled,
&emsp;Jordan was driven back.

4 Like rams the mountains, and like lambs
&emsp;the hills skipp'd to and fro.
5 O sea, why fledd'st thou? Jordan, back
&emsp;why wast thou driven so?
6 Ye mountains great, wherefore was it
&emsp;that ye did skip like rams?
And wherefore was it, little hills,
&emsp;that ye did leap like lambs?

7 O at the presence of the Lord,
&emsp;earth, tremble thou for fear,
While as the presence of the God
&emsp;of Jacob doth appear:
8 Who from the hard and stony rock
&emsp;did standing water bring;
And by his pow'r did turn the flint
&emsp;into a water-spring.

## Psalm CXV.

This Psalm instructs us, (1) To whom glory and praise ought to be ascribed: not to ourselves, but to God, for his mercy and truth, ver. 1: not-to lifeless senseless idols, but to God, whose throne is in the heavens, and who doth whatever is best, ver. 2-8, 15, 16. (2) How we are to glorify God, 1. By trusting in him, crediting his promise, and receiving his blessings, ver. 9-15. 2. By blessing him while we live, ver. 17, 18. ¶ Learn, my soul, to deny thyself; to take up thy cross and follow Christ; and to keep thyself from idols. But make <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>, as reconciled in Christ, thy portion, thy trust, thy help, thy shield, thy blessedness, thy comfort, thy praise; and consider thine earthly enjoyments as the gracious gifts of thy God, and an earnest of thy far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory.

N<span class="sc">OT</span> unto us, Lord, not to us,
&emsp;but do thou glory take
Unto thy name, ev'n for thy truth,
&emsp;and for thy mercy's sake.
2 O wherefore should the heathen say,
&emsp;Where is their God now gone?
3 But our God in the heavens is,
&emsp;what pleas'd him he hath done.

4 Their idols silver are and gold,
&emsp;work of men's hands they be.
5 Mouths have they, but they do not speak;
&emsp;and eyes, but do not see;
6 Ears have they, but they do not hear;
&emsp;noses, but savour not;
7 Hands, feet, but handle not, nor walk;
&emsp;nor speak they through their throat.

8 Like them their makers are, and all
&emsp;on them their trust that build.
9 O Isr'el, trust thou in the Lord,
&emsp;he is their help and shield.
10 O Aaron's house, trust in the Lord,
&emsp;their help and shield is he.
11 Ye that fear God, trust in the Lord,
&emsp;their help and shield he'll be.
