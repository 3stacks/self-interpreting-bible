## Psalm LVIII.

*To the chief Musician, Al-taschith, Michtam of David.*

This Psalm was probably composed by David when Saul carried on some kind of legal prosecution against him, which is not mentioned in the history of his reign. Here (1) He describes the corruption in the government of these judges, in neglecting to do justice, and in readiness to do injustice; and the corruption of their nature with malice, falsehood, and obstinate untractableness, ver. 1-5. (2) He prays that God would disable them to perpetrate mischief, would defeat their projects, and weaken their influence, ver. 6-8. (3) He predicts their ruin, as calculated to promote the comfort of the godly, and the conviction of sinners, ver. 9-11.

¶ While I sing, let me search out my corruptions, and bewail my obstinate refusals of Jesus Christ, and the counsels of his Word. Let me dread the speedy but awful wrath of God, if I regard iniquity in my heart, or indulge it in my life.

D<span class="sc">O</span> ye, O congregation,
&emsp;indeed speak righteousness?
O ye that are the sons of men,
&emsp;judge ye with uprightness?
2 Yea, ev'n within your very hearts
&emsp;ye wickedness have done;
And ye the vi'lence of your hands
&emsp;do weigh the earth upon.

3 The wicked men estranged are,
&emsp;ev'n from the very womb;
They, speaking lies, do stray as soon
&emsp;as to the world they come.
4 Unto a serpent's poison like
&emsp;their poison doth appear;
Yea, they are like the adder deaf,
&emsp;that closely stops her ear:

5 That so she may not hear the voice
&emsp;of one that charm her would,
No, not though he most cunning were,
&emsp;and charm most wisely could.
6 Their teeth, O God, within their mouth
&emsp;break thou in pieces small;
The great teeth break thou out, O Lord,
&emsp;of these young lions all.

7 Let them like waters melt away,
&emsp;which downward still do flow:
In pieces cut his arrows all,
&emsp;when he shall bend his bow.
8 Like to a snail that melts away,
&emsp;let each of them be gone;
Like woman's birth untimely, that
&emsp;they never see the sun.

9 He shall them take away before
&emsp;your pots the thorns can find,
Both living, and in fury great,
&emsp;as with a stormy wind.
10 The righteous, when he vengeance sees,
&emsp;he shall be joyful then;
The righteous one shall wash his feet
&emsp;in blood of wicked men.

11 So men shall say, The righteous man
&emsp;reward shall never miss:
And verily upon the earth
&emsp;a God to judge there is.

## Psalm LIX.

*To the chief Musician, Al-taschith, Michtam of David, when Saul sent, and they watched the house to kill him.*

This Psalm was penned by David when Saul sent messengers to watch his house in order to kill him, 1 Sa. xix. In it, as in the seven immediately preceding, we have (1) David's bitter complaints of his enemies, as wicked, barbarous, malicious. and atheistical, ver. 1-7. (2) His predictory prayers, that God would expose these enemies to contempt and derision; make them standing monuments of his just indignation, deal with them according to their sins; consume them in his wrath; and even render their sin their punishment, ver 8. 10-15. (3) His holy resolutions to wait upon God till his judgments should be executed on them, and then to praise him for his favours, ver. 9, 10, 16, 17.

¶ While I sing, let me think of the opposition made to David's Lord. and of the undying justice which hath overtaken his enemies, Jewish and heathen. and shall overtake his antichristian and other opposers. While his honours are trampled under foot. let me wait on and trust in God, that at last I may join in the <span class="sc">Hallelujahs</span> of his people, when the devices of his enemies shall fail for ever and ever.

M<span class="sc">Y</span> God, deliver me from those
&emsp;that are mine enemies;
And do thou me defend from those
&emsp;that up against me rise.
2 Do thou deliver me from them
&emsp;that work iniquity;
And give me safety from the men
&emsp;of bloody cruelty.

3 For, lo, they for my soul lay wait:
&emsp;the mighty do combine
Against me, Lord; not for my fault,
&emsp;nor any sin of mine.
4 They run, and, without fault in me,
&emsp;themselves do ready make:
Awake to meet me with thy help;
&emsp;and do thou notice take.

5 Awake therefore, Lord God of hosts,
&emsp;thou God of Israel,
To visit heathen all: spare none
&emsp;that wickedly rebel.
6 At ev'ning they go to and fro;
&emsp;they make great noise and sound,
Like to a dog, and often walk
&emsp;about the city round.

7 Behold, they belch out with their mouth,
&emsp;and in their lips are swords:
For they do say thus, Who is he
&emsp;that now doth hear our words?
8 But thou, O Lord, shalt laugh at them,
&emsp;and all the heathen mock.
9 While he's in pow'r I'll wait on thee;
&emsp;for God is my high rock.

10 He of my mercy that is God
&emsp;betimes shall me prevent;
Upon mine en'mies God shall let
&emsp;me see mine heart's content.
11 Them slay not, lest my folk forget;
&emsp;but scatter them abroad
By thy strong pow'r; and bring them down,
&emsp;O thou our shield and God.

12 For their mouth's sin, and for the words
&emsp;that from their lips do fly,
Let them be taken in their pride:
&emsp;because they curse and lie.
13 In wrath consume them, them consume,
&emsp;that so they may not be:
And that in Jacob God doth rule
&emsp;to th' earth's ends let them see.

14 At ev'ning let thou them return,
&emsp;making great noise and sound,
Like to a dog, and often walk
&emsp;about the city round.
15 And let them wander up and down,
&emsp;in seeking food to eat;
And let them grudge when they shall not
&emsp;be satisfy'd with meat.

16 But of thy pow'r I'll sing aloud;
&emsp;at morn thy mercy praise:
For thou to me my refuge wast,
&emsp;and tow'r, in troublous days.
17 O God, thou art my strength, I will
&emsp;sing praises unto thee;
For God is my defence, a God
&emsp;of mercy unto me.

## Psalm LX.

*To the chief Musician upon Shushan-eduth, Michtam of David, to teach, when he strove with Aram-naharaim and with Aram-zobah, when Joab returned, and smote of Edom in the valley of Salt twelve thousand.*

This Psalm was penned by David during his wars with the Syrians and Edomites, 2 Sa. viii. Here (1) He bewails the tokens of God's displeasure with Israel, in the breaking and disjointing of the nation under Saul and Ishbosheth, ver. 1-3. (2) In thankful contemplation of the late revival he had given to their affairs by his own accession to the throne, and victories over the Philistines, Moabites, &c., he beseeches God to grant them victory over. and rest from all their enemies, ver. 4, 5. (3) In the confident expectation of God's fulfilment of his promises, along with importunate supplication, he expresses his triumphant hopes that God would quickly subdue every enemy, the fortified cities of Edom not excepted, ver. 6-12.

¶ In all my distressed cases, let me rejoice in the <span class="sc">Banner</span>, the <span class="sc">Ensign</span> given and displayed to the nations; and in the faith of God's promise, let me firmly expect victory over every spiritual foe and rejoice in hope of the glory of God, the full enjoyment of the Canaan that is above.

O <span class="sc">LORD</span>, thou hast rejected us,
&emsp;and scatter'd us abroad;
Thou justly hast displeased been;
&emsp;return to us, O God.
2 The earth to tremble thou hast made;
&emsp;therein didst breaches make:
Do thou thereof the breaches heal,
&emsp;because the land doth shake.

3 Unto thy people thou hard things
&emsp;hast show'd, and on them sent;
And thou hast caused us to drink
&emsp;wine of astonishment.
4 And yet a banner thou hast giv'n
&emsp;to them who thee do fear;
That it by them, because of truth,
&emsp;displayed may appear.

5 That thy beloved people may
&emsp;deliver'd be from thrall,
Save with the pow'r of thy right hand,
&emsp;and hear me when I call.
6 God in his holiness hath spoke;
&emsp;herein I will take pleasure:
Shechem I will divide, and forth
&emsp;will Succoth's valley measure.

7 Gilead I claim as mine by right;
&emsp;Manasseh mine shall be;
Ephraim is of mine head the strength;
&emsp;Judah gives laws for me;
8 Moab's my washing-pot; my shoe
&emsp;I'll over Edom throw;
And over Palestina's land
&emsp;I will in triumph go.

9 O who is he will bring me to
&emsp;the city fortify'd?
O who is he that to the land
&emsp;of Edom will me guide?
10 O God, which hadest us cast off,
&emsp;this thing wilt thou not do?
Ev'n thou, O God, which didest not
&emsp;forth with our armies go?

11 Help us from trouble; for the help
&emsp;is vain which man supplies.
12 Through God we'll do great acts; he shall
&emsp;tread down our enemies.

## Psalm LXI.

*To the chief Musician upon Neginah, A Psalm of David.*

Here (1) David, in commemorating what the Lord had formerly done for him, and in the faith of what he had promised, cries to God for comfort and protection amidst great troubles, ver. 1-4, (2) In the view of what God had and would for ever do for him, he supplicates necessary mercies, and resolves on everlasting praise and thanksgiving to God as his bounden duty, ver. 5-8.

¶ So may all providences, distressful or smiling, lead my soul to thankful panting for God, as my <span class="sc">All</span> and <span class="sc">In All</span>.

O <span class="sc">GOD</span>, give ear unto my cry;
&emsp;unto my pray'r attend.
2 From th' utmost corner of the land
&emsp;my cry to thee I'll send.
What time my heart is overwhelm'd,
&emsp;and in perplexity,
Do thou me lead unto the Rock
&emsp;that higher is than I.

3 For thou hast for my refuge been
&emsp;a shelter by thy pow'r;
And for defence against my foes
&emsp;thou hast been a strong tow'r.
4 Within thy tabernacle I
&emsp;for ever will abide;
And under covert of thy wings
&emsp;with confidence me hide.

5 For thou the vows that I did make,
&emsp;O Lord my God, didst hear:
Thou hast giv'n me the heritage
&emsp;of those thy name that fear.
