Ver. 55. [*When Saul saw David.* From ver. 54 to ver. 6 of the next chapter is rejected by Boothroyd and A. Clarke as an interpolation, on the ground of the improbability of the narrative. Such criticism is worse than dangerous. It sets an example that justifies every cavil of infidelity against the sacred history. Besides, there is not a circumstance narrated in the least degree improbable.—*Whose son is this youth?* Where is the improbability of any man forgetting a youth when near manhood, whom he had known when a boy, and whom, in the intermediate time, he had not seen? *C.*]
