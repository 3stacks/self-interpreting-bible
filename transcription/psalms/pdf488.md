107:1 O give{a} thanks unto the LORD; for *he is* good: for his mercy *endureth* for ever.{1}

107:4 They{d} wandered in the wilderness in a solitary way; they found no city to dwell in.
107:5 Hungry{e} and thirsty, their soul fainted in them.{3}
107:6 Then{g} they cried unto the LORD in their trouble, *and* he delivered them out of their distresses.
107:7 And he {h}led them forth by the right way, that they might go to a city of habitation.
107:8 Oh{i} that *men* would praise the LORD *for* his goodness, and *for* his wonderful works to the children of men!
107:9 For{j} he satisfieth the longing soul, and filleth the hungry soul with goodness.
107:10 Such{k} as sit in darkness, and in the shadow of death, *being* bound in affliction and iron;
107:11 Because{l} they rebelled against the words of God, and contemned the {n}counsel of the most High:
107:12 Therefore{o} he brought down their heart with labour: they fell down, and {p}*there was* none to help.
107:13 Then{q} they cried unto the LORD in their trouble, *and* he saved them out of their distresses.
107:14 He{r} brought them out of darkness and the shadow of death, and brake their bands in sunder.
107:15 Oh{s} that *men* would praise the LORD *for* his goodness, and *for* his wonderful works to the children of men!
107:16 For{t} he hath broken the gates of brass, and cut the bars of iron in sunder.
107:17 {u}Fools {v}because of their transgression, and because of their iniquities, are afflicted.
107:18 Their{x} soul abhorreth all manner of meat; and they draw near unto the gates of death.
107:19 Then{y} they cry unto the LORD in their trouble, *and* he saveth them out of their distresses.
107:20 He{z} sent his word, and healed{4} them, and delivered *them* from their destructions.
107:21 Oh{a} that *men* would praise the LORD *for* his goodness, and *for* his wonderful works to the children of men!
107:22 And{b} let them sacrifice the sacrifices of thanksgiving, and declare his works with {5}rejoicing.
107:23 They{c} that go down to the sea in ships, that do business in great waters;
107:24 These see {d}the works of the LORD, and his wonders in the deep.
107:25 For{e} he commandeth, and {6}raiseth the stormy wind, which lifteth up the waves thereof.
107:26 They{g} mount up to the heaven, they go down again to the depths; {h}their soul is melted because of trouble.
107:27 They reel to and fro, and stagger like a drunken man, and are at their wit's end.{7}
107:28 Then{i} they cry unto the LORD in their trouble, and he bringeth them out of their distresses.
107:29 He{j} maketh the storm a calm, so that the waves thereof are still.
107:30 Then are they glad, because they be quiet; so he bringeth them unto their desired haven.
107:31 Oh{k} that *men* would praise the LORD *for* his goodness, and *for* his wonderful works to the children of men!
107:32 Let{l} them exalt him also in the congregation of the people, and praise him in the assembly of the elders.
107:33 He {n}turneth rivers into a wilderness, and the water-springs into dry ground;{8}
107:34 A fruitful land into barrenness,{9} for the wickedness of them that dwell therein.
107:35 He{o} turneth the wilderness into a standing water, and dry ground into water-springs:
107:36 And{p} there he maketh the hungry to dwell, that they may prepare a city for habitation;
107:37 And{q} sow the fields, and plant vineyards, which may yield fruits of increase.
107:38 He{r} blesseth them also, so that they are multiplied greatly, and suffereth not their cattle to decrease.
107:39 Again,{s} they are minished and brought low through oppression, affliction, and sorrow.
107:40 He{t} poureth contempt upon princes,{1} and causeth them to wander in the {2}wilderness, *where there is* no way.
107:41 Yet{u} setteth he the poor on high {3}from affliction, and maketh *him* families like a {4}flock.
107:42 The{v} righteous shall see *it*, and rejoice; and all iniquity shall stop her mouth.
107:43 Whoso{x} *is* wise, and will observe these *things*, even they shall understand the lovingkindness of the LORD.{5}

107:1 1 | This beautiful psalm was probably composed to celebrate the return of the Jewish exiles from Babylon, and designed to be sung at the dedication of the temple. It is *antiphonal* in construction—the chorus commencing with the stanza of general thanksgiving:—'O give thanks unto Jehovah, for he is gracious, and his mercy endureth for ever!' Then the leader chants the special causes of thanksgiving; the chorus breaking in at intervals (see ver. 8,15,21,31). Probably the concluding verse (43) may have been uttered by the high-priest, as a solemn exhortation to the whole people.—*P.*
