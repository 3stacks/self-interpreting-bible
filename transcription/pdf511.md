12 They in their hands shall bear thee up,
&emsp;still waiting thee upon;
Lest thou at any time should'st dash

## Psalm XCII.

*A Psalm or Song for the Sabbath-day.*

In this *Psalm or Song for the Sabbath-day*, (1) Praise, the business of the Sabbath, is earnestly recommended, ver. 1-3. (2) God's works, which are the reason of the Sabbath and ground of praise, are celebrated in their greatness and unsearchableness, ver. 4-6. (3) The ruin of sinners and the felicity of saints are beautifully contrasted. Sinners shall perish; but God, the portion and life of saints, shall endure for ever. Sinners, notwithstanding their temporary flourishing, shall be cut off and destroyed: saints shall be exalted and comforted. Sinners shall be confounded and ruined; but saints, through their union to and fellowship with Christ, shall prosper and flourish, to the manifestation of God's faithfulness and equity, ver. 7-15.

¶ While I keep the Sabbath of the Lord my God, let these things deeply affect my heart: and be it my care, by a steady conversation and manifest growth in grace, to verify these precious promises of the New Testament in Jesus' blood.

T<span class="sc">O</span> render thanks unto the Lord,
&emsp;it is a comely thing,
And to thy name, O thou most High,
&emsp;due praise aloud to sing.
2 Thy loving-kindness to show forth
&emsp;when shines the morning light;
And to declare thy faithfulness
&emsp;with pleasure ev'ry night,

3 On a ten-stringed instrument,
&emsp;upon the psaltery,
And on the harp with solemn sound,
&emsp;and grave sweet melody.
4 For thou, Lord, by thy mighty works
&emsp;hast made my heart right glad;
And I will triumph in the works
&emsp;which by thine hands were made.

5 How great, Lord, are thy works! each thought
&emsp;of thine a deep it is:
6 A brutish man it knoweth not;
&emsp;fools understand not this.
7 When those that lewd and wicked are
&emsp;spring quickly up like grass,
And workers of iniquity
&emsp;do flourish all apace;
It is that they for ever may
&emsp;destroyed be and slain:

8 But thou, O Lord, art the most High,
&emsp;for ever to remain.
9 For, lo, thine enemies, O Lord,
&emsp;thine en'mies perish shall;
The workers of iniquity
&emsp;shall be dispersed all.

10 But thou shalt, like unto the horn
&emsp;of th' unicorn, exalt
My horn on high: thou with fresh oil
&emsp;anoint me also shalt.
11 Mine eyes shall also my desire
&emsp;see on mine enemies;
Mine ears shall of the wicked hear,
&emsp;that do against me rise.

12 But like the palm-tree flourishing
&emsp;shall be the righteous one;
He shall like to the cedar grow
&emsp;that is in Lebanon.
13 Those that within the house of God
&emsp;are planted by his grace,
They shall grow up, and flourish all
&emsp;in our God's holy place.

14 And in old age, when others fade,
&emsp;they fruit still forth shall bring;
They shall be fat, and full of sap,
&emsp;and aye be flourishing;
15 To show that upright is the Lord:
&emsp;he is a rock to me;
And he from all unrighteousness
&emsp;is altogether free.

## Psalm XCIII.

This Psalm is a triumphant celebration of the government of <span class="sc">Jehovah</span>, and of Jesus our Redeemer, in (1) The awfulness, strength, stability, and duration thereof, ver. 1, 2. (2) In his triumphant victories over all opposition, ver. 3, 4. (3) In the faithfulness and certainty of his words, and the holiness of his court, ver. 5.

¶ While I sing, let my soul rejoice in the highness of my God, and in the glories of my Redeemer.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> Lord doth reign, and cloth'd is he
&emsp;with majesty most bright;
His works do show him cloth'd to be,
&emsp;and girt about with might.
The world is also stablished,
&emsp;that it cannot depart.
2 Thy throne is fix'd of old, and thou
&emsp;from everlasting art.

3 The floods, O Lord, have lifted up,
&emsp;they lifted up their voice;
The floods have lifted up their waves,
&emsp;and made a mighty noise.
4 But yet the Lord, that is on high,
&emsp;is more of might by far
Than noise of many waters is,
&emsp;or great sea-billows are.

5 Thy testimonies ev'ry one
&emsp;in faithfulness excel;
And holiness for ever, Lord,
&emsp;thine house becometh well.

## Psalm XCIV.

This is a Psalm for the afflicted people of God, in which (1) God's just wrath is implored upon their haughty, active, cruel, and atheistical oppressors, whose folly is exposed from the consideration of God's works of creation, providence, and grace, ver. 1-11. (2) His gracious comforts are presented to the persecuted saints; that he will bless them in and by their afflictions, and give them rest after them, while their enemies are ruined: that God will not cast them off when cast down, but will, in his due time, reduce things to their right course:—to verify which, the Psalmist produces part of his own experience, ver. 12-24.

¶ While I sing, let all revenge be rooted from my heart; all repining at afflictions be banished from my soul. But in every plunge of adversity let me cast my burden on the Lord, that he may sustain me, and help me in the time of need. Let me rejoice in the assured hope of God's yet reforming the church, and spreading his glory among the nations.

O <span class="sc">Lord</span> God, unto whom alone
&emsp;all vengeance doth belong;
O mighty God, who vengeance own'st,
&emsp;shine forth, avenging wrong.
2 Lift up thyself, thou of the earth
&emsp;the sov'reign Judge that art;
And unto those that are so proud
&emsp;a due reward impart.

3 How long, O mighty God, shall they
&emsp;who lewd and wicked be,
How long shall they who wicked are
&emsp;thus triumph haughtily?
4 How long shall things most hard by them
&emsp;be uttered and told?
And all that work iniquity
&emsp;to boast themselves be bold?

5 Thy folk they break in pieces, Lord,
&emsp;thine heritage oppress:
6 The widow they and stranger slay,
&emsp;and kill the fatherless.
7 Yet say they, God it shall not see,
&emsp;nor God of Jacob know.
8 Ye brutish people! understand;
&emsp;fools! when wise will ye grow?

9 The Lord did plant the ear of man,
&emsp;and hear then shall not he?
He only form'd the eye, and then
&emsp;shall he not clearly see?
10 He that the nations doth correct,
&emsp;shall he not chastise you?
He knowledge unto man doth teach,
&emsp;and shall himself not know?

11 Man's thoughts to be but vanity
&emsp;the Lord doth well discern.
12 Bless'd is the man thou chast'nest, Lord,
&emsp;and mak'st thy law to learn:
13 That thou may'st give him rest from days
&emsp;of sad adversity,
Until the pit be digg'd for those
&emsp;that work iniquity.

14 For sure the Lord will not cast off
&emsp;those that his people be,
Neither his own inheritance
&emsp;quit and forsake will he:
15 But judgment unto righteousness
&emsp;shall yet return again;
And all shall follow after it
&emsp;that are right-hearted men.

16 Who will rise up for me against
&emsp;those that do wickedly?
Who will stand up for me 'gainst those
&emsp;that work iniquity?
17 Unless the Lord had been my help
&emsp;when I was sore opprest,
Almost my soul had in the house
&emsp;of silence been at rest.

18 When I had uttered this word,
&emsp;(my foot doth slip away,)
Thy mercy held me up, O Lord,
&emsp;thy goodness did me stay.
19 Amidst the multitude of thoughts
&emsp;which in my heart do fight,
My soul, lest it be overcharg'd,
&emsp;thy comforts do delight.

20 Shall of iniquity the throne
&emsp;have fellowship with thee,
Which mischief, cunningly contriv'd,
&emsp;doth by a law decree?
21 Against the righteous souls they join,
&emsp;they guiltless blood condemn.
22 But of my refuge God's the rock,
&emsp;and my defence from them.

23 On them their own iniquity
&emsp;the Lord shall bring and lay,
And cut them off in their own sin;
&emsp;our Lord God shall them slay.

## Psalm XCV.

This Psalm contains (1) Solemn calls to celebrate the praises of Jesus Christ and his Father, on account of what he is, what he has done, and what he is to us, and we to him, ver. 1-7. (2) Alarming warnings to hear his voice, and not to harden our hearts as the Israelites in the desert, who on that account were debarred from the promised land, ver. 7-11.

¶ While I sing, let me behold the glories of my Redeemer, and believe my new-covenant relation to him; and while it is yet called to-day, let me beware of being hardened through the deceitfulness of sin. Since there is a promise left me of entering into his rest, let me take heed lest I should seem to come short of it through unbelief.

O <span class="sc">Come</span>, let us sing to the Lord:
&emsp;come, let us ev'ry one
A joyful noise make to the Rock
&emsp;of our salvation.
