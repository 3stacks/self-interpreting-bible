30:1 1 | Ephraim and Manasseh lay contiguous to Jerusalem, but not nearer than Reuben and Gad, and not so near as Simeon and Dan. Three reasons seem to have produced this special notice of the invitation to Ephraim, of which Manasseh was a dependency, as Benjamin was of Judah. (1) After Judah Ephraim was the greatest tribe. (2) It seems to have been the most hopelessly addicted to idolatry, and therefore its omission might have been expected, Ho.4.17; 13.1. (3) The king, by his pious and energetic letter, seeks to aid the noble efforts of Hosea for the reformation and salvation of his country. This special invitation to Ephraim seems to have arisen from the partial reformation produced by the preaching of Hosea, ch.13.12; 14.8, in which Manasseh, a dependency of Ephraim, as Benjamin was of Judah, had, most probably, participated. See Ps.80.2.—*C.*
