## Psalm LXXVI.

*To the chief Musician on Neginoth, A Psalm or Song of Asaph.*

This Psalm is very similar to the 48th, and was penned on occasion of some remarkable victory; but whether in the days of David, Asa, Jehoshaphat, or Hezekiah (2 Ch. xiv. xx. xxxii.), is uncertain. We have in it (1) Encouraging congratulations of the church's happiness in having God so manifested in her, and so near unto her; and in having his power so employed in her behalf to the advancement of his own glory, and the destruction of her enemies, ver. 1-6. (2) Important instructions how to improve this victory to the terror of enemies, the comfort of saints, and the excitement of all to vow to God, and to obey and revere him, ver. 7-12. ¶ While I sing this, let my soul bless the Lord that my lot hath been alway cast in a land of gospel light. Let me with thankful heart remember what he hath done for this church and land, and give him the praise of every deliverance. Let me never fear the rage of men; but, in every danger and doubt, commit my way to God, that he may bring it to pass.

I<span class="sc">N</span> Judah's land God is well known,
&emsp;his name's in Isr'el great:
2 In Salem is his tabernacle,
&emsp;in Sion is his seat.
3 There arrows of the bow he brake,
&emsp;the shield, the sword, the war.
4 More glorious thou than hills of prey,
&emsp;more excellent art far.

5 Those that were stout of heart are spoil'd,
&emsp;they slept their sleep outright;
And none of those their hands did find,
&emsp;that were the men of might.
6 When thy rebuke, O Jacob's God,
&emsp;had forth against them past,
Their horses and their chariots both
&emsp;were in a dead sleep cast.

7 Thou, Lord, ev'n thou art he that should
&emsp;be fear'd; and who is he
That may stand up before thy sight,
&emsp;if once thou angry be?
8 From heav'n thou judgment caus'd be heard;
&emsp;the earth was still with fear,
9 When God to judgment rose, to save
&emsp;all meek on earth that were.

10 Surely the very wrath of man
&emsp;unto thy praise redounds:
Thou to the remnant of his wrath
&emsp;wilt set restraining bounds.
11 Vow to the Lord your God, and pay:
&emsp;all ye that near him be,
Bring gifts and presents unto him;
&emsp;for to be fear'd is he.

12 By him the spirits shall be cut off
&emsp;of those that princes are
Unto the kings that are on earth
&emsp;he fearful doth appear.

## Psalm LXXVII.

*To the chief Musician, to Jeduthun, A Psalm of Asaph.*

This Psalm relates to (1) A most afflicted case, in which are remarkable fervent and incessant prayers, agonizing restlessness, heart-overwhelming meditations, melancholy fears, and almost desperate outcries concerning God, ver. 1-9. (2) The effectual cure of this mournful case, by serious meditation on, and solemn acknowledgment of the power, greatness, and grace of God as our God; and of the holiness and mysteriousness of his providential conduct, particularly in bringing Israel out of Egypt, and in leading them through the wilderness to the promised land, ver. 10-20. ¶ While I sing, let me stand in awe of that God who so chastiseth his favourite saints; let me sympathize with men of wounded spirits. If my soul is overwhelmed within me, let me call to mind the gracious character and great works of my God for his church, or for my soul.

U<span class="sc">NTO</span> the Lord I with my voice,
&emsp;I unto God did cry;
Ev'n with my voice, and unto me
&emsp;his ear he did apply.
2 I in my trouble sought the Lord,
&emsp;my sore by night did run,
And ceased not; my grieved soul
&emsp;did consolation shun.

3 I to remembrance God did call,
&emsp;yet trouble did remain;
And overwhelm'd my spirit was,
&emsp;whilst I did sore complain.
4 Mine eyes, debarr'd from rest and sleep,
&emsp;thou makest still to wake;
My trouble is so great, that I
&emsp;unable am to speak.

5 The days of old to mind I call'd,
&emsp;and oft did think upon
The times and ages that are past
&emsp;full many years agone.
6 By night my song I call to mind,
&emsp;and commune with my heart;
My sp'rit did carefully inquire
&emsp;how I might ease my smart.

7 For ever will the Lord cast off,
&emsp;and gracious be no more?
8 For ever is his mercy gone?
&emsp;fails his word evermore?
9 Is't true that to be gracious
&emsp;the Lord forgotten hath?
And that his tender mercies he
&emsp;hath shut up in his wrath?

10 Then did I say, That surely this
&emsp;is mine infirmity:
I'll mind the years of the right hand
&emsp;of him that is most High.
11 Yea, I remember will the works
&emsp;performed by the Lord:
The wonders done of old by thee
&emsp;I surely will record.

12 I also will of all thy works
&emsp;my meditation make;
And of thy doings to discourse
&emsp;great pleasure I will take.
13 O God, thy way most holy is
&emsp;within thy sanctuary;
And what God is so great in pow'r
&emsp;as is our God most high?

14 Thou art the God that wonders dost
&emsp;by thy right hand most strong:
Thy mighty pow'r thou hast declar'd
&emsp;the nations among.
15 To thine own people with thine arm
&emsp;thou didst redemption bring;
To Jacob's sons, and to the tribes
&emsp;of Joseph that do spring.

16 The waters, Lord, perceived thee,
&emsp;the waters saw thee well;
And they for fear aside did flee;
&emsp;the depths on trembling fell.
17 The clouds in water forth were pour'd,
&emsp;sound loudly did the sky;
And swiftly through the world abroad
&emsp;thine arrows fierce did fly.

18 Thy thunder's voice alongst the heav'n
&emsp;a mighty noise did make;
By lightnings lighten'd was the world,
&emsp;th' earth tremble did and shake.
19 Thy way is in the sea, and in
&emsp;the waters great thy path;
Yet are thy footsteps hid, O Lord;
&emsp;none knowledge thereof hath.

20 Thy people thou didst safely lead,
&emsp;like to a flock of sheep;
By Moses' hand and Aaron's thou
&emsp;didst them conduct and keep.

## Psalm LXXVIII.

*Maschil of Asaph.*

This Psalm is a brief but instructive history of the transactions between Israel and their God for about four hundred and eighty years, from Moses to David. Here is (1) The introduction, containing a solemn call to an attentive consideration of God's words and works, in order to a faithful transmitting of the knowledge thereof to posterity. that they might set their hope in God, and reform from their fathers' wickedness, ver. 1-8. (2) The history itself; in which are exhibited (1) God's favours to Israel before their settlement in Canaan, in plaguing the Egyptians; in dividing the Red Sea; in giving them water from the rock, and manna from heaven; in bringing them into Canaan, notwithstanding of their unnumbered provocations, particularly their forgetfulness of his mighty works, their ingratitude for his favours, their murmuring against his trying dispensations. their eagerness to satiate their lusts with his benefits, their impenitent obduracy, or hypocritical repentance under his rebukes, ver. 9-55. (2) Their ingratitude, treachery, and idolatry after their entrance into Canaan; with God's righteous resentment thereof, in removing his tabernacle from Shiloh, and in delivering up his ark and people into the hand of the Philistines, ver. 56-64. (3) God's merciful return to them, in obliging the Philistines, by the plague of emerods, to restore his ark, and in at length providing a habitation for it at Jerusalem; and in raising up and qualifying David, a descendant of Judah, to govern them in a manner both honourable and happy, ver. 65-72. These are more largely recorded in the books of Exodus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges, 1st and 2d Samuel, and part of 1st Kings. ¶ While I sing them, let me with grief and shame remember my own and my fathers' transgressions against the Lord. Let me adore the infinite patience, power, mercy, holiness, and equity of God. Let me bless his holy name for the multitude of his undeserved favours towards me and towards the church; and though he cause grief, let me hope that in due time he will have compassion.

A<span class="sc">TTEND</span>, my people, to my law;
&emsp;thereto give thou an ear;
The words that from my mouth proceed
&emsp;attentively do hear.
2 My mouth shall speak a parable,
&emsp;and sayings dark of old;
3 The same which we have heard and known,
&emsp;and us our fathers told.

4 We also will them not conceal
&emsp;from their posterity;
Them to the generation
&emsp;to come declare will we:
The praises of the Lord our God,
&emsp;and his almighty strength,
The wondrous works that he hath done,
&emsp;we will show forth at length.

5 His testimony and his law
&emsp;in Isr'el he did place,
And charg'd our fathers it to show
&emsp;to their succeeding race;
6 That so the race which was to come
&emsp;might well them learn and know;
And sons unborn, who should arise,
&emsp;might to their sons them show:

7 That they might set their hope in God,
&emsp;and suffer not to fall
His mighty works out of their mind,
&emsp;but keep his precepts all:
8 And might not, like their fathers, be
&emsp;a stiff rebellious race;
A race not right in heart; with God
&emsp;whose sp'rit not steadfast was.

9 The sons of Ephraim, who nor bows
&emsp;nor other arms did lack,
When as the day of battle was,
&emsp;they faintly turned back.
10 They brake God's cov'nant, and refus'd
&emsp;in his commands to go;
11 His works and wonders they forgot,
&emsp;which he to them did show.

12 Things marvellous he brought to pass;
&emsp;their fathers them beheld
Within the land of Egypt done,
&emsp;yea, ev'n in Zoan's field.
13 By him divided was the sea,
&emsp;he caus'd them through to pass;
And made the waters so to stand,
&emsp;as like an heap it was.
