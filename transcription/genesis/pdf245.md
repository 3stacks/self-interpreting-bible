## Chapter XVIII.
Ver. 2. When there were no public inns for travellers to refresh themselves, or lodge in, it was necessary and common for hospitable persons to invite them at noon or at eventide to their own houses, ch. 19. 1, 2; 24. 30, 31; Ju. 19. 20, 21; Job 31. 32.

Ver. 3. The person to whom Abraham addressed himself, and who was at least the chief speaker, was the Son of God and Judge of the world, ver. 25, with Jn. 5. 22.

Ver. 4. As people in those countries went barefooted, or with sandals, because of the heat, washing the feet after travelling was common and needful, ch. 19. 2; 24. 32; Ju. 19. 21; 2 Sa. 11. 8; 1 Ti. 5. 10; Lu. 7. 44.

Ver. 8. Abraham *stood* by them as their servant, to give them what they needed, Ne. 12. 44; Ga. 5. 13; Lu. 17. 8.

Ver. 10. Nine months hence, by my providence, and in fulfilment of my promise, Sarah shall have a son, ch. 17. 21.

Ver. 11. In respect of her age, and of the constitution of her body, she was past all hopes of child-bearing, ch. 17. 17; He. 11. 11; Ro. 4. 19.

Ver. 20, 21. By God's *inquiring* into things, is meant either his bringing the persons concerned to a proper sense of their condition and conduct, Ge. 3. 9; 4. 9, 10; 16. 8; 1 Ki. 19. 9, 13; Jn. 4. 4, 9; or it marks the wisdom, patience, and equity of his procedure, ch. 11. 5, 7; Ex. 3. 8; 33. 5; Mi. 1. 3.

Ver. 22, 23. Abraham's *standing before* and *drawing near* to the Lord, imports his bold and familiar intercession with him, 1 Sa. 14. 36; Ps. 73. 28; He. 7. 19; 10. 22; Ja. 4. 8.
