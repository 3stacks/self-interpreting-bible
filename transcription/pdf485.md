## Psalm XVIII.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David, the servant of the Lord, who spake unto the Lord the words of this song, in the day that the Lord delivered him from the hand of all his enemies, and from the hand of Saul. And he said—*

In this Psalm of thanksgiving for manifold deliverances, observe (1) David's ardent love to God in Christ, whom he believed to be <span class="sc">his own</span> in every gracious and saving relation: and whom he had experienced his merciful almighty, and seasonable Deliverer from depths of trouble, ver. 1-19. (2) His comfortable reflections on the integrity which the Lord had enabled him to maintain, and on the gracious kindness of God consequent thereupon, ver. 20-28. (3) His thankful ascription of all the glory of his noted exploits to God, as his director and strengthener, ver. 29-42. (4) His triumphant faith and hope of further assistance and favour from God, to himself, and to his seed, for evermore, ver. 43-50.

¶ But let me not forget Jesus, to whom Jehovah is so closely, so marvellously, so sweetly related:—Jesus, who so ardently loveth his eternal Father, and for ever returns the grateful remembrance of his kindness, to him and to his chosen seed in delivering him from distress, in raising him from the dead, in rewarding his obedience unto death, in giving him glory at his own right hand, and in rendering all nations his obedient subjects.—Let me sing this new song in the full assurance of faith that God in Christ is my <span class="sc">all</span> and <span class="sc">in all</span>; and with a heart ravished with the consolations of Christ, and in the sweet reviews of what he hath done, what he doth, and what he will for ever do, for my soul.

T<span class="sc">HEE</span> will I love, O Lord, my strength.
2 My fortress is the Lord,
&emsp;My rock, and he that doth to me
&emsp;deliverance afford:
&emsp;My God, my strength, whom I will trust,
&emsp;a buckler unto me,
&emsp;The horn of my salvation,
&emsp;and my high tow'r, is he.

3 Upon the Lord, who worthy is
&emsp;of praises, will I cry;
&emsp;And then shall I preserved be
&emsp;safe from mine enemy.
4 Floods of ill men affrighted me,
&emsp;death's pangs about me went;
5 Hell's sorrows me environed;
&emsp;death's snares did me prevent.

6 In my distress I call'd on God,
&emsp;cry to my God did I;
&emsp;He from his temple heard my voice,
&emsp;to his ears came my cry.
7 Th' earth, as affrighted, then did shake,
&emsp;trembling upon it seiz'd.
&emsp;The hills' foundations moved were,
&emsp;because he was displeas'd.

8 Up from his nostrils came a smoke,
&emsp;and from his mouth there came
&emsp;Devouring fire, and coals by it
&emsp;were turned into flame.
9 He also bowed down the heav'ns,
&emsp;and thence he did descend;
&emsp;And thickest clouds of darkness did
&emsp;under his feet attend.

10 And he upon a cherub rode,
&emsp;and thereon he did fly;
&emsp;Yea, on the swift wings of the wind
&emsp;his flight was from on high.
11 He darkness made his secret place:
&emsp;about him, for his tent,
&emsp;Dark waters were, and thickest clouds
&emsp;of th' airy firmament.

12 And at the brightness of that light,
&emsp;which was before his eye,
&emsp;His thick clouds pass'd away, hailstones
&emsp;and coals of fire did fly.
13 The Lord God also in the heav'ns
&emsp;did thunder in his ire;
&emsp;And there the Highest gave his voice,
&emsp;hailstones and coals of fire.

14 Yea, he his arrows sent abroad,
&emsp;and them he scattered;
&emsp;His lightnings also he shot out,
&emsp;and them discomfited.
15 The waters' channels then were seen,
&emsp;the world's foundations vast
&emsp;At thy rebuke discover'd were,
&emsp;and at thy nostrils' blast.

16 And from above the Lord sent down,
&emsp;and took me from below;
&emsp;From many waters he me drew,
&emsp;which would me overflow.
17 He me reliev'd from my strong foes,
&emsp;and such as did me hate;
&emsp;Because he saw that they for me
&emsp;too strong were, and too great.

18 They me prevented in the day
&emsp;of my calamity;
&emsp;But even then the Lord himself
&emsp;a stay was unto me.
19 He to a place where liberty
&emsp;and room was hath me brought;
&emsp;Because he took delight in me,
&emsp;he my deliv'rance wrought.

20 According to my righteousness
&emsp;he did me recompense,
&emsp;He me repaid according to
&emsp;my hands' pure innocence.
21 For I God's ways kept, from my God
&emsp;did not turn wickedly.
22 His judgments were before me, I
&emsp;his laws put not from me.

23 Sincere before him was my heart,
&emsp;with him upright was I;
&emsp;And watchfully I kept myself
&emsp;from mine iniquity.
24 After my righteousness the Lord
&emsp;hath recompensed me,
&emsp;After the cleanness of my hands
&emsp;appearing in his eye.

25 Thou gracious to the gracious art,
&emsp;to upright men upright:
26 Pure to the pure, froward thou kyth'st
&emsp;unto the froward wight.
27 For thou wilt the afflicted save
&emsp;in grief that low do lie:
&emsp;But wilt bring down the countenance
&emsp;of them whose looks are high.

28 The Lord will light my candle so,
&emsp;that it shall shine full bright:
&emsp;The Lord my God will also make
&emsp;my darkness to be light.
29 By thee through troops of men I break,
&emsp;and them discomfit all;
&emsp;And, by my God assisting me,
&emsp;I overleap a wall.

30 As for God, perfect is his way:
&emsp;the Lord his word is try'd;
&emsp;He is a buckler to all those
&emsp;who do in him confide.
31 Who but the Lord is God? but he
&emsp;who is a rock and stay?
32 'Tis God that girdeth me with strength,
&emsp;and perfect makes my way.

33 He made my feet swift as the hinds,
&emsp;set me on my high places.
34 Mine hands to war he taught, mine arms
&emsp;brake bows of steel in pieces.
35 The shield of thy salvation
&emsp;thou didst on me bestow:
&emsp;Thy right hand held me up, and great
&emsp;thy kindness made me grow.

36 And in my way my steps thou hast
&emsp;enlarged under me,
&emsp;That I go safely, and my feet.
&emsp;are kept from sliding free.
37 Mine en'mies I pursued have,
&emsp;and did them overtake;
&emsp;Nor did I turn again till I
&emsp;an end of them did make.

38 I wounded them, they could not rise;
&emsp;they at my feet did fall.
39 Thou girdedst me with strength for war;
&emsp;my foes thou brought'st down all:
40 And thou hast giv'n to me the necks
&emsp;of all mine enemies;
&emsp;That I might them destroy and slay,
&emsp;who did against me rise.

41 They cried out, but there was none
&emsp;that would or could them save;
&emsp;Yea, they did cry unto the Lord,
&emsp;but he no answer gave.
42 Then did I beat them small as dust
&emsp;before the wind that flies;
&emsp;And I did cast them out like dirt
&emsp;upon the street that lies.

43 Thou mad'st me free from people's strife,
&emsp;and heathen's head to be:
&emsp;A people whom I have not known
&emsp;shall service do to me.
44 At hearing they shall me obey,
&emsp;to me they shall submit.
45 Strangers for fear shall fade away,
&emsp;who in close places sit.

46 God lives, bless'd be my Rock; the God
&emsp;of my health praised be.
47 God doth avenge me, and subdues
&emsp;the people under me.
48 He saves me from mine enemies;
&emsp;yea, thou hast lifted me
&emsp;Above my foes; and from the man
&emsp;of vi'lence set me free.

49 Therefore to thee will I give thanks
&emsp;the heathen folk among;
&emsp;And to thy name, O Lord, I will
&emsp;sing praises in a song.
50 He great deliv'rance gives his king:
&emsp;he mercy doth extend
&emsp;To David, his anointed one,
&emsp;and his seed without end.

## Psalm XIX.

*To the chief Musician, A Psalm of David.*

Now the books of God are opened, not for my trial and condemnation in the last judgment, but for my instruction. Let my soul look and read thereon. (1) The book of creation and providence, in which all the works of God instruct mankind in general, concerning the eternal wisdom, power, and goodness of their Maker, ver. 1-6. (2) The book of inspiration, the sure, the right, the pure, the true, the perfect, and powerful oracles of which instruct, convert, comfort, and warn the members of the church; and in keeping of which there is an exceeding great and everlasting reward of glory obtained, ver. 7-11. (3) What conviction of sin! What supplication for pardon of it, and preservation from it; and for the acceptance of our duties, through Jesus' blood, doth, or ought to pursue upon a proper perusal of these volumes of heaven, ver. 12-14.

¶ While I sing these matters, let me, conscious of my own ignorance and folly, in all things consult the mind of God. Let me blush that my experience of the powerful influences of God's word is so scanty, and that in me still dwell such fearful remains of sinful corruption. Let me cry mightily to God for the subduing and destruction thereof.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> heav'ns God's glory do declare,
&emsp;the skies his hand-works preach:
2 Day utters speech to day, and night
&emsp;to night doth knowledge teach.
3 There is no speech nor tongue to which
&emsp;their voice doth not extend:
4 Their line is gone through all the earth.
&emsp;their words to the world's end.

&emsp;In them he set the sun a tent;
5 Who, bridegroom-like, forth goes
&emsp;From's chamber, as a strong man doth
&emsp;to run his race rejoice.
