## Psalm XXIII.

*A Psalm of David.*

Here is the children's Psalm, and which is, in very deed, a noted Song of all the children of God. Behold (1) New-covenant relation to God, as a full and everlasting security against hurtful wants, ver. 1. (2) Pleasant experience of God's acting up to his new-covenant character, in disposing of, directing, assisting, and feasting, and comforting his people, ver. 2, 3, 5. (3) Well-grounded hopes of God's careful and seasonable supplies; of his comfortable presence and help amidst distress and death; of the perpetual and everlasting manifestation of his mercy and grace to us-ward; and of our endless nearness to, and immediate enjoyment of him, ver. 1, 4, 5, 6.

¶ While I sing, let me think, as before God, whether I have the experimental knowledge of all these things. Let my soul be as the daughter of the horse-leech, crying mightily, <span class="sc">Give</span>, <span class="sc">Give</span> what is good. And let me, in all these forms, taste and see that God is good.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> Lord's my shepherd, I'll not want.
2 He makes me down to lie
&emsp;In pastures green: he leadeth me
&emsp;the quiet waters by.

3 My soul he doth restore again;
&emsp;and me to walk doth make
&emsp;Within the paths of righteousness,
&emsp;ev'n for his own name's sake.
4 Yea, though I walk in death's dark vale,
&emsp;yet will I fear none ill:
&emsp;For thou art with me; and thy rod
&emsp;and staff me comfort still.

5 My table thou hast furnished
&emsp;in presence of my foes;
&emsp;My head thou dost with oil anoint,
&emsp;and my cup overflows.
6 Goodness and mercy all my life
&emsp;shall surely follow me:
&emsp;And in God's house for evermore
&emsp;my dwelling-place shall be.

## Psalm XXIV.

*A Psalm of David.*

Probably this Psalm was penned for the use of the Hebrews, when David brought up the Ark of God to Jerusalem; or when Solomon brought it into the temple (2 Sam. vi.; 1 Kings viii.), in order to raise their hearts above their external ceremonies, to a reception of, and walking in Christ, who was thereby prefigured. Observe (1) Christ's kingdom of nature comprehending the whole world and all the inhabitants thereof, ver. 1, 2. (2) His kingdom of grace in the nature of it; the gracious character of its subjects; and their charter to their everlasting happiness above, ver. 3-6. (3) Under the figure of a call to admit the ark, we have a solemn summons, issued forth by God, for the heavens to receive Jesus, our glorious and almighty King, into their blissful abodes in his ascension: and for us to receive him into our hearts and societies below, ver. 7-10.

¶ While I sing, let me be affected with the double claim the Redeemer hath on me, as his creature, and as his ransomed one. Let me try whether I possess the distinguishing characters of a real saint: and whether I have received an abundance of the gift of righteousness and of blessedness from the God of my salvation. Let me charge, let me rouse up all my inward powers, to receive Jesus Christ the Lord, as made of God unto me wisdom, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> earth belongs unto the Lord,
&emsp;and all that it contains;
&emsp;The world that is inhabited,
&emsp;and all that there remains.
2 For the foundations thereof
&emsp;he on the seas did lay,
&emsp;And he hath it established
&emsp;upon the floods to stay.

3 Who is the man that shall ascend
&emsp;into the hill of God?
&emsp;Or who within his holy place
&emsp;shall have a firm abode?
4 Whose hands are clean, whose heart is pure,
&emsp;and unto vanity
&emsp;Who hath not lifted up his soul,
&emsp;nor sworn deceitfully.

5 He from th' Eternal shall receive
&emsp;the blessing him upon,
&emsp;And righteousness, ev'n from the God
&emsp;of his salvation.
6 This is the generation
&emsp;that after him inquire,
&emsp;O Jacob, who do seek thy face
&emsp;with their whole heart's desire.

7 Ye gates, lift up your heads on high;
&emsp;ye doors that last for aye,
&emsp;Be lifted up, that so the King
&emsp;of glory enter may.
8 But who of glory is the King?
&emsp;The mighty Lord is this;
&emsp;Ev'n that same Lord, that great in might
&emsp;and strong in battle is.

9 Ye gates, lift up your heads; ye doors,
&emsp;doors that do last for aye,
&emsp;Be lifted up, that so the King
&emsp;of glory enter may.
10 But who is he that is the King
&emsp;of glory? who is this?
&emsp;The Lord of hosts, and none but he,
&emsp;the King of glory is.

## Psalm XXV.

*A Psalm of David.*

In singing this Psalm, let me observe (1) What serious work prayer is; what lifting up of soul, what directing of eyes to God, and fixing them on him, must be in it, ver. 1-15. (2) What mercies ought to be prayed for: pardon of sin, ver. 6-18;—direction in duty, ver. 4, 5;—familiar intimacy with God, ver. 16;—deliverance from trouble, ver. 17, 18;—preservation from adversaries, ver. 20, 21;—and, in fine, safety and deliverance to the church, ver. 22. (3) What pleas are proper to be used in prayer: as the trust we have reposed in God, ver. 2, 3, 5-20, 21;—our own divinely effected sincerity in the Lord's way, ver. 21;—our distress and the malice of our enemies, ver. 2, 16-19;—but chiefly the mercy that is in God, and the glory which redounds to his name from his bestowing of new-covenant favours, ver. 6-11. (4) Strong encouragements to prayer, taken from the perfections of God's nature; from his promises of instruction and direction; from the fulness and grace of his covenant; and from his delight in allowing men familiar intimacy and fellowship with himself, ver. 8-14.

¶ Let these things, my soul, be the object of thy strictest care and attention in all thy addresses to God.

T<span class="sc">O</span> thee I lift my soul:
2 O Lord, I trust in thee:
&emsp;My God, let me not be asham'd,
&emsp;nor foes triumph o'er me.

3 Let none that wait on thee
&emsp;be put to shame at all;
&emsp;But those that without cause transgress,
&emsp;let shame upon them fall.

4 Show me thy ways, O Lord;
&emsp;thy paths, O teach thou me:
5 And do thou lead me in thy truth,
&emsp;therein my teacher be:
&emsp;For thou art God that dost
&emsp;to me salvation send,
&emsp;And I upon thee all the day
&emsp;expecting do attend.

6 Thy tender mercies, Lord,
&emsp;I pray thee to remember,
&emsp;And loving-kindnesses; for they
&emsp;have been of old for ever.

7 My sins and faults of youth
&emsp;do thou, O Lord, forget:
&emsp;After thy mercy think on me,
&emsp;and for thy goodness great.

8 God good and upright is:
&emsp;the way he'll sinners show.
9 The meek in judgment he will guide,
&emsp;and make his path to know.

10 The whole paths of the Lord
&emsp;are truth and mercy sure,
&emsp;To those that do his cov'nant keep,
&emsp;and testimonies pure.

11 Now, for thine own name's sake,
&emsp;O Lord, I thee entreat
&emsp;To pardon mine iniquity;
&emsp;for it is very great.

12 What man is he that fears
&emsp;the Lord, and doth him serve?
&emsp;Him shall he teach the way that he
&emsp;shall choose, and still observe.

13 His soul shall dwell at ease;
&emsp;and his posterity
&emsp;Shall flourish still, and of the earth
&emsp;inheritors shall be.

14 With those that fear him is
&emsp;the secret of the Lord;
&emsp;The knowledge of his covenant
&emsp;he will to them afford.

15 Mine eyes upon the Lord
&emsp;continually are set;
&emsp;For he it is that shall bring forth
&emsp;my feet out of the net.

16 Turn unto me thy face,
&emsp;and to me mercy show;
&emsp;Because that I am desolate,
&emsp;and am brought very low.

17 My heart's griefs are increas'd:
&emsp;me from distress relieve.
18 See mine affliction and my pain,
&emsp;and all my sins forgive.
