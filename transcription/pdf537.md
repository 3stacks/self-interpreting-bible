## Paraphrase XXXVI.

*Luke i. 46-56.*

This is the song of Mary the mother of our Lord. When we sing it, we may with her wonder at the riches of sovereign mercy, from whence all the blessings of redemption proceed. By what almighty power are they bestowed on us, notwithstanding all our unwillingness and unworthiness! What delightful ground for the firmest faith, the deepest humility, the profoundest admiration, the most ravishing joy and exalted praise! Thrice blessed to rejoice in a Saviour born for us and received into our heart, and in that salvation he brings near to all!

M<span class="sc">Y</span> soul and spirit, fill'd with joy,
&emsp;my God and Saviour praise,
Whose goodness did from poor estate
&emsp;his humble handmaid raise.

2 Me bless'd of God, the God of might,
&emsp;all ages shall proclaim;
From age to age his mercy lasts,
&emsp;and holy is his name.

3 Strength with his arm th' Almighty show'd;
&emsp;the proud his looks abas'd;
He cast the mighty to the ground,
&emsp;the meek to honour rais'd.

4 The hungry with good things were fill'd,
&emsp;the rich with hunger pin'd:
He sent his servant Isr'el help,
&emsp;and call'd his love to mind;

5 Which to our fathers' ancient race
&emsp;his promise did ensure,
To Abrah'm and his chosen seed,
&emsp;for ever to endure.

## Paraphrase XXXVII.

*Luke ii. 8-15.*

At the birth of princes great rejoicings are made among their subjects: they and their mirth soon die out. In the passage paraphrased we have an account of the birth of a Prince, the like to which was never on earth, that of the Prince of the kings of the earth. It is solemnized by a multitude of holy angels singing a song that shall never end: the subject of it is God incarnate, and all blessings through him to men, to me. To the highest glory of Jehovah, it is weekly kept up by the church militant, and will be the song of the triumphant for ever.

W<span class="sc">HILE</span> humble shepherds watch'd their flocks
&emsp;in Bethleh'm's plains by night,
An angel sent from heav'n appear'd,
&emsp;and fill'd the plains with light.

2 Fear not, he said, (for sudden dread
&emsp;had seiz'd their troubled mind;)
Glad tidings of great joy I bring
&emsp;to you, and all mankind.

3 To you, in David's town, this day
&emsp;is born, of David's line,
The Saviour, who is Christ the Lord;
&emsp;and this shall be the sign:

4 The heav'nly babe you there shall find
&emsp;to human view display'd,
All meanly wrapt in swaddling-bands,
&emsp;and in a manger laid.

5 Thus spake the seraph; and forthwith
&emsp;appear'd a shining throng
Of angels, praising God; and thus
&emsp;address'd their joyful song:

6 All glory be to God on high,
&emsp;and to the earth be peace;
Good-will is shown by Heav'n to men,
&emsp;and never more shall cease.

## Paraphrase XXXVIII.

*Luke ii. 25-33.*

Here we have the character of good old Simeon, his taking up the child Jesus in his arms, and his wishing for a dismission from the tents of separation below. May we not say, Thrice happy they who wait, and wait long, for the Lord. He will appear at last for their comfort and open their mouth in his praise; and it is only believing views of Jesus Christ that can render death and eternity pleasant and hopeful.

J<span class="sc">UST</span> and devout old Simeon liv'd;
&emsp;to him it was reveal'd,
That Christ, the Lord, his eyes should see
&emsp;ere death his eyelids seal'd.

2 For this consoling gift of Heav'n
&emsp;to Isr'el's fallen state,
From year to year with patient hope
&emsp;the aged saint did wait.

3 Nor did he wait in vain; for, lo!
&emsp;revolving years brought round,
In season due, the happy day,
&emsp;which all his wishes crown'd.

4 When Jesus, to the temple brought
&emsp;by Mary's pious care,
As Heav'n's appointed rites requir'd,
&emsp;to God was offer'd there,

5 Simeon into those sacred courts
&emsp;a heav'nly impulse drew;
He saw the Virgin hold her Son,
&emsp;and straight his Lord he knew.

6 With holy joy upon his face
&emsp;the good old father smil'd;
Then fondly in his wither'd arms
&emsp;he clasp'd the promis'd child:

7 And while he held the heav'n-born babe,
&emsp;ordain'd to bless mankind,
Thus spoke, with earnest look, and heart
&emsp;exulting, yet resign'd:

8 Now, Lord! according to thy word,
&emsp;let me in peace depart;

&emsp;and gladness fills my heart.

&emsp;now let their vigour cease;

&emsp;now let them close in peace.

10 This great salvation, long prepar'd,
&emsp;and now disclos'd to view,
Hath prov'd thy love was constant still,
&emsp;and promises were true.

11 That Sun I now behold, whose light
&emsp;shall heathen darkness chase:
And rays of brightest glory pour
&emsp;around thy chosen race.

## Paraphrase XXXIX.

*Luke iv. 18, 19.*

When we sing the commission of Jesus, may we not reflect how infinitely gracious he is, how appointed of the Father, and coming in his name to save us, and how answerable to all our needs. How suited to the most needy and most unworthy, shall we not hail thee the Desire of all nations! May we share of thy coming and kingdom. May thy name be known upon earth, thy saving health among all nations!

H<span class="sc">ARK</span>, the glad sound, the Saviour comes!
&emsp;the Saviour promised long;
Let ev'ry heart exult with joy,
&emsp;and ev'ry voice be song!

2 On him the Spirit, largely shed,
&emsp;exerts its sacred fire;
Wisdom and might, and zeal and love,
&emsp;his holy breast inspire.

3 He comes! the pris'ners to relieve,
&emsp;in Satan's bondage held;
The gates of brass before him burst,
&emsp;the iron fetters yield.

4 He comes! from dark'ning scales of vice
&emsp;to clear the inward sight;
And on the eye-balls of the blind
&emsp;to pour celestial light.

5 He comes! the broken hearts to bind,
&emsp;the bleeding souls to cure;
And with the treasures of his grace
&emsp;t' enrich the humble poor.

6 The sacred year has now revolv'd,
&emsp;accepted of the Lord,
When Heav'n's high promise is fulfill'd,
&emsp;and Isr'el is restor'd.

7 Our glad hosannahs, Prince of Peace!
&emsp;thy welcome shall proclaim;
And heav'n's exalted arches ring
&emsp;with thy most honour'd name.

## Paraphrase XL.

*Luke xv. 13-25.*

These verses contain a poetical version of almost all our Saviour's parable of the prodigal son. Here we may consider our conduct and condition as sinners, and God's conduct to us as a kind Redeemer. With respect to the first, how madly prodigal are we, covetous of present things, slaves to our lusts, abusing every gift of God to their service! How quickly, destitute of everything honourable, holy, or gladdening, we are involved in perplexity, disappointment, and wretchedness, yet obstinately bent on every course, however base or ruinous, rather than return to a gracious God! O the love of God in seeking out and saving the lost prodigal. How quick-sighted his mercy to espy our needs, and mark our cares and desires! How alert in hastening to our relief! How active and generous in drawing us to himself, embracing us when polluted, pardoning our sins, shedding abroad his love in our hearts, and supplying all our wants! What a change is produced by his grace; the lost is found, the dead in trespasses and sins quickened to God and his service! What ground of inexpressible joy to saints, to ministers, to angels, to God! Let us hear Jehovah's words and obey them: Return, O backsliding children! Behold we come unto thee, for thou art the Lord our God.

T<span class="sc">HE</span> wretched prodigal behold
&emsp;in mis'ry lying low,
Whom vice had sunk from high estate,
&emsp;and plung'd in want and woe.

2 While I, despis'd and scorn'd, he cries,
&emsp;starve in a foreign land,
The meanest in my father's house
&emsp;is fed with bounteous hand:

3 I'll go, and with a mourning voice,
&emsp;fall down before his face:
Father! I've sinn'd 'gainst Heav'n and thee,
&emsp;nor can deserve thy grace.

4 He said, and hasten'd to his home,
&emsp;to seek his father's love:
The father sees him from afar,
&emsp;and all his bowels move.

5 He ran, and fell upon his neck,
&emsp;embrac'd and kiss'd his son:
The grieving prodigal bewail'd
&emsp;the follies he had done.

6 No more, my father, can I hope
&emsp;to find paternal grace;
My utmost wish is to obtain
&emsp;a servant's humble place.

7 Bring forth the fairest robe for him,
&emsp;the joyful father said;
To him each mark of grace be shown,
&emsp;and ev'ry honour paid.

8 A day of feasting I ordain;
&emsp;let mirth and song abound:
My son was dead, and lives again!
&emsp;was lost, and now is found!

9 Thus joy abounds in paradise
&emsp;among the hosts of heav'n,
Soon as the sinner quits his sins,
&emsp;repents, and is forgiv'n.

## Paraphrase XLI.

*John iii. 14-19.*

The meaning of this passage is expressed in the following words: 'The Messiah must be put to death, in order to his bringing about the salvation of men; for as Moses lifted up the brazen serpent on a pole in the wilderness, as God's ordinance for healing the Israelites who had been mortally bitten by the fiery serpents, and did look on it for a cure; so the Messiah, though holy and harmless, appears in the likeness of sinful flesh, must be lifted up on the cross, afterwards lifted up to glory, and exhibited in the gospel as God's ordinance, for sinners to look to, by faith, for salvation; that every one, of whatever nation, who shall truly look to, and trust in him for salvation, may be completely delivered from all sin and misery. For God, who delights in mercy, freely made a deed of gift and grant of his own Son to sinners at large, that whoever receives him may not perish, but be blessed with holiness and happiness; for God sent not his Son into the world to pass and execute sentence of condemnation upon all others but Jews, but that persons of all nations may be saved; and every one, Jew or Gentile, who believes on Christ as offered in the gospel, is freed from condemnation; but whosoever rejects him remains under the curse, and has his guilt greatly increased.' Blessed God, let me see and feel my spiritual diseases, and look to Jesus and be healed.

A<span class="sc">S</span> when the Hebrew prophet rais'd
&emsp;the brazen serpent high,
The wounded look'd, and straight were cur'd,
&emsp;the people ceas'd to die.
