## Chapter XIII.
[Ver. 38. Geshur was a small kingdom on the north-eastern border of Palestine, beyond Jordan. It appears to have been in part at least identical with the present province of *Lejah*, formerly called Trachonitis. The Geshurites maintained friendly relations with the Israelites east of the Jordan; probably from community of interest, both being extensive cattle-owners. The community of occupation may have led to the alliance between David and the daughter of Talmai, king of Geshur. Absalom was the fruit of this marriage; and the wild acts of his life were doubtless to some extent the results of maternal training. *P.*]

**Reflections.**—Righteous, but awful, are the judgments of God; especially when he punishes sin with sin, and that remarkably similar! Children are apt to imitate their parents' sins without imitating their repentance. And near relatives should be cautious that their tender affection does not degenerate into lawless lust. How unbridled is lust when once indulged! the body often suffers through vile affections: and no station can render the man happy whose lusts rage within him. A wicked and subtile friend, who leads to or flatters in sin, is a dreadful curse. Such as harbour no ill designs themselves, are often made instruments of iniquity by the crafty sinner, as they are most unsuspicious. Deeds of filthiness seek for secrecy.
