## Chapter V.
[Ver. 6. *Except thou take away the blind and the lame, thou shalt not come in hither.* Who are 'the blind and the lame?' Some think certain invalids whom the Jebusites had placed upon their lofty walls to express their contempt of David's assault. Others consider them the images of their gods, whom the Hebrews contemptuously called 'lame and blind,' and whom, nevertheless, the Jebusites placed on the battlements as a protection; and this opinion is countenanced by the description, 'hated of David's soul:' words supposed inapplicable to anything but idolatrous images. But though images might justly be called 'blind,' they could not be called 'lame,' as it implies some capacity of walking. We feel, therefore, compelled to adopt the first interpretation. Coverdale translates part of ver. 6 thus:—'Thou shalt not come hither, but the blind and the lame shall drive thee away;' which seems the true rendering.
