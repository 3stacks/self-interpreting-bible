## Psalm CV.

This Psalm was a part of the solemn hymn sung at the translation of the ark from the house of Obed-edom to the city of David, 1 Ch. xvi.; and contains (1) Solemn calls to meditate on, seek, magnify, and praise God, who is so great, so gracious, and so nearly connected with us, ver. 1-7. (2) A narrative of the particular mercies of God to Israel for the space of 480 years, as grounds of praise: particularly, 1. His covenant with their patriarchs, ver. 8-11; 2. His care of them while strangers and sojourners, ver. 12-15; 3. His advancement of Joseph to be the *shepherd and stone of Israel*, ver. 16-22; 4. His increasing them in Egypt, and at last delivering them from it, by inflicting manifold plagues on the Egyptians, 23-38; 5. His care of them in the wilderness, and settling them in Canaan as a peculiar people to himself, 39-45. ¶ While I sing, let my soul, with *affecting wonder*, think what God is to me, and what he hath done for me and his church in prosecution of his *promise* through the Lord Jesus Christ.

G<span class="sc">IVE</span> thanks to God, call on his name;
&emsp;to men his deeds make known.

2 Sing ye to him, sing psalms; proclaim
&emsp;his wondrous works each one.

&emsp;to glory do accord;
And let the heart of ev'ry one
&emsp;rejoice that seeks the Lord.

4 The Lord Almighty, and his strength,
&emsp;with stedfast hearts seek ye:
His blessed and his gracious face
&emsp;seek ye continually.

5 Think on the works that he hath done,
&emsp;which admiration breed;
His wonders, and the judgments all
&emsp;which from his mouth proceed;

6 O ye that are of Abr'ham's race,
&emsp;his servant well approv'n;

&emsp;whom he chose for his own.

7 Because he, and he only, is
&emsp;the mighty Lord our God;
And his most righteous judgments are
&emsp;in all the earth abroad.

8 His cov'nant he remember'd hath,
&emsp;that it may ever stand:
To thousand generations
&emsp;the word he did command.

&emsp;with faithful Abraham,
And unto Isaac, by his oath,
&emsp;he did renew the same.

10 And unto Jacob, for a law,
&emsp;he made it firm and sure,

&emsp;which ever should endure.

&emsp;for heritage to you;

&emsp;in number very few:

13 While yet they went from land to land
&emsp;without a sure abode;

&emsp;did wander far abroad;

&emsp;no man to do them wrong;

&emsp;kings, who were great and strong.

&emsp;that mine anointed be,

&emsp;did him in prison try.

&emsp;did send to set him free.

21 A lord to rule his family
&emsp;he rais'd him, as most fit;
To him of all that he possess'd
&emsp;he did the charge commit:

22 That he might at his pleasure bind
&emsp;the princes of the land;
And he might teach his senators
&emsp;wisdom to understand.

23 The people then of Israel
&emsp;down into Egypt came;
And Jacob also sojourned
&emsp;within the land of Ham.

24 And he did greatly by his pow'r
&emsp;increase his people there;
And stronger than their enemies

25 Their heart he turned to envy

With those that his own servants were
&emsp;to deal in subtilty.

&emsp;Aaron his chosen one.

27 By these his signs and wonders great
&emsp;in Ham's land were made known.

28 Darkness he sent, and made it dark;
&emsp;his word they did obey.

29 He turn'd their waters into blood,
&emsp;and he their fish did slay.

30 The land in plenty brought forth frogs
&emsp;in chambers of their kings.

&emsp;in all their borders brings.

&emsp;into their land he sent:

&emsp;trees of their coasts he rent.

&emsp;locusts did much abound;

&emsp;and all fruits of their ground.

&emsp;chief of their strength each one.

&emsp;their fear on them did light.

&emsp;and fire to shine by night.

40 They ask'd, and he brought quails: with bread
&emsp;of heav'n he filled them.

&emsp;in deserts like a stream.

&emsp;and servant Abr'ham, thought.

&emsp;with gladness, forth he brought.

&emsp;he of the heathen gave;

&emsp;inheritance might have.

&emsp;according to his word;

## Psalm CVI.

This Psalm may be considered as a supplement to the preceding. That exhibited to our view the marvellous kindness of God to Israel: this exhibits the fearful rebellions of Israel against their gracious God. We have here (1) A preface, ascribing praise to God, exhibiting comfort to his saints, and requesting favours from God, ver. 1-5. (2) An alarming confession or narrative of Israel's wickedness against their God, notwithstanding his marvellous kindness to them,— in provoking him at the Red sea, ver. 6-12; in lusting after sensual gratifications, ver. 13-15; in mutinying against his vicegerents, ver. 16-18; in worshipping the golden calf, ver. 19-23; in murmuring contempt of the promised land, ver. 24-27; in joining with the Moabites and Midianites in the worship of Baal-peor, ver. 28-31; in quarrelling with Moses at Kadesh, ver. 32, 33; in incorporating themselves with the Canaanites, and imitating their idolatrous and cruel customs, ver. 34-39. (3) An affecting view of God's great compassion to Israel, notwithstanding they had provoked him to inflict sore and almost destructive punishments upon them, ver. 40-46. (4) An affectionate improvement of his kindness in imploring his powerful deliverance, and ascription of endless praises to his name, ver. 47, 48. ¶ While I sing, let my soul be deeply affected with the dreadful nature of sin, and with men's obstinacy, nay, my own obstinacy therein. Let me, with amazement, behold the unbounded mercy of God to men; and that where sin hath abounded grace doth much more abound.

G<span class="sc">IVE</span> praise and thanks unto the Lord,
&emsp;for bountiful is he;
His tender mercy doth endure
&emsp;unto eternity.

2 God's mighty works who can express?
&emsp;or show forth all his praise?

3 Blessed are they that judgment keep,
&emsp;and justly do always.

4 Remember me, Lord, with that love
&emsp;which thou to thine dost bear;
With thy salvation, O my God,
&emsp;to visit me draw near:

5 That I thy chosen's good may see,
&emsp;and in their joy rejoice;
And may with thine inheritance
&emsp;triumph with cheerful voice.

6 We with our fathers sinned have,
&emsp;and of iniquity
Too long we have the workers been;
&emsp;we have done wickedly.

7 The wonders great, which thou, O Lord,
&emsp;didst work in Egypt land,
Our fathers, though they saw, yet them
&emsp;they did not understand:
And they thy mercies' multitude
&emsp;kept not in memory;
But at the sea, ev'n the Red sea,
&emsp;provok'd him grievously.

8 Nevertheless he saved them,
&emsp;ev'n for his own name's sake;
That so he might to be well known
&emsp;his mighty power make.

9 When he the Red sea did rebuke,
&emsp;then dried up it was:
Through depths, as through the wilderness,
&emsp;he safely made them pass.
