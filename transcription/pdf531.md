# Translations and Paraphrases, in Verse, of Several Passages of Sacred Scripture

*With Notes, Explaining the Sense, and for Directing and Animating Devotion.*

## Paraphrase I.

*Gen. i.*

Let me stand still and consider this wonderful work of God! O how his eternal power and Godhead shine forth, in forming so vast, so well furnished worlds of creatures! O his wisdom marked in the vast variety, the comely order, and the mutual subserviency of all things to the good of one another, and to his glory! O his goodness in forming such multitudes of beings to partake of his bounty, as their all in all, and especially toward man, in forming him after his own image, and in so preparing this lower world to be a commodious lodgment for him, in his way to the mansions of light. In singing these lines let me rise to nature's God; let me think of the divine personal Word, by whom all things were made, and look forward to the new heavens and the new earth wherein dwelleth righteousness.

L<span class="sc">ET</span> heav'n arise, let earth appear,
&emsp;said the Almighty Lord:
The heav'n arose, the earth appear'd,
&emsp;at his creating word.
2 Thick darkness brooded o'er the deep:
&emsp;God said, "Let there be light:"
The light shone forth with smiling ray,
&emsp;and scatter'd ancient night.

3 He bade the clouds ascend on high;
&emsp;the clouds ascend, and bear
A wat'ry treasure to the sky,
&emsp;and float upon the air.
4 The liquid element below
&emsp;was gather'd by his hand;
The rolling seas together flow,
&emsp;and leave the solid land.

5 With herbs, and plants, and fruitful trees,
&emsp;the new-form'd globe he crown'd,
Ere there was rain to bless the soil,
&emsp;or sun to warm the ground.
6 Then high in heav'n's resplendent arch
&emsp;he plac'd two orbs of light,
He set the sun to rule the day,
&emsp;the moon to rule the night.

7 Next, from the deep, th' Almighty King
&emsp;did vital beings frame;
Fowls of the air of ev'ry wing,
&emsp;and fish of ev'ry name.
8 To all the various brutal tribes
&emsp;he gave their wondrous birth;
At once the lion and the worm
&emsp;sprung from the teeming earth.

9 Then, chief o'er all his works below,
&emsp;at last was Adam made;
His Maker's image bless'd his soul,
&emsp;and glory crown'd his head.
10 Fair in the Almighty Maker's eye
&emsp;the whole creation stood.
He view'd the fabric he had rais'd;
&emsp;his word pronounc'd it good.

## Paraphrase II.

*Gen. xxviii. 20-22.*

While I sing these lines let me think, Have I ever enjoyed Bethel visits from a redeeming God? How happy we are, when our fellowship with God leaves a lasting impression on our spirits, and cheerfully constrains us to surrender ourselves to him, and all we have to his service, and renders us content with a moderate share of the good things of life!

O <span class="sc">GOD</span> of Bethel! by whose hand
&emsp;thy people still are fed;
Who through this weary pilgrimage
&emsp;hast all our fathers led:
2 Our vows, our pray'rs, we now present
&emsp;before thy throne of grace:
God of our fathers! be the God
&emsp;of their succeeding race.

3 Through each perplexing path of life
&emsp;our wand'ring footsteps guide;
Give us each day our daily bread,
&emsp;and raiment fit provide.
4 O spread thy cov'ring wings around,
&emsp;till all our wand'rings cease,
And at our Father's lov'd abode
&emsp;our souls arrive in peace.

5 Such blessings from thy gracious hand
&emsp;our humble pray'rs implore;
And thou shalt be our chosen God,
&emsp;and portion evermore.

## Paraphrase III.

*Job i. 21.*

Under our trials, views of death should wean us from perishing things. All we have is God's gift, lent or given us, and whatever we lose, enough is left, which well deserves our thankfulness. How powerful the influence of God's grace, that can render his people composed, and heartily resigned to his will under the sharpest trials. In all my trials let me view God's hand, and acquiesce in his will.

N<span class="sc">AKED</span> as from the earth we came,
&emsp;and enter'd life at first;
Naked we to the earth return,
&emsp;and mix with kindred dust.
2 Whate'er we fondly call our own
&emsp;belongs to heav'n's great Lord;
The blessings lent us for a day
&emsp;are soon to be restor'd.

3 'Tis God that lifts our comforts high,
&emsp;or sinks them in the grave:
He gives; and, when he takes away,
&emsp;he takes but what he gave.
4 Then, ever blessed be his name!
&emsp;his goodness swell'd our store;
His justice but resumes its own;
&emsp;'tis ours still to adore.

## Paraphrase IV.

*Job iii. 17-20.*

These words exhibit the grave as levelling all distinctions, and ending all the troubles of the present life. This surely should teach us wisdom, and the exercise of humility and moderation, a patient bearing of the afflictions of life, and a dying daily. O how blessed are the dead that die in the Lord; they rest from their labours, and their works follow them.

H<span class="sc">OW</span> still and peaceful is the grave!
&emsp;where, life's vain tumults past,
Th' appointed house, by Heav'n's decree,
&emsp;receives us all at last.
2 The wicked there from troubling cease,
&emsp;their passions rage no more;
And there the weary pilgrim rests
&emsp;from all the toils he bore.

3 There rest the pris'ners, now releas'd
&emsp;from slav'ry's sad abode;
No more they hear th' oppressor's voice,
&emsp;or dread the tyrant's rod.
4 There servants, masters, small and great,
&emsp;partake the same repose;
And there, in peace, the ashes mix
&emsp;of those who once were foes.

5 All, levell'd by the hand of Death,
&emsp;lie sleeping in the tomb;
Till God in judgment calls them forth,
&emsp;to meet their final doom.

## Paraphrase V.

*Job v. 6-12.*

What stupidity appears in thinking anything comes to us by chance! The more we are acquainted with the sinfulness of our nature, the less reason have we to murmur at our afflictions? Under sore trials let me try to search and remove the sinful cause, and let me turn all my fretful murmurs into humble supplications.

T<span class="sc">HOUGH</span> trouble springs not from the dust,
&emsp;nor sorrow from the ground;
Yet ills on ills, by Heav'n's decree,
&emsp;in man's estate are found.
2 As sparks in close succession rise,
&emsp;so man, the child of woe,
Is doom'd to endless cares and toils
&emsp;through all his life below.

3 But with my God I leave my cause;
&emsp;from him I seek relief;
To him, in confidence of pray'r,
&emsp;unbosom all my grief.
4 Unnumber'd are his wondrous works,
&emsp;unsearchable his ways;
'Tis his the mourning soul to cheer,
&emsp;the bowed down to raise.
